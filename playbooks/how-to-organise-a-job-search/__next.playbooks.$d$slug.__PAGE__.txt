36:T1e2e,<p>A job search is a project with many small moving parts: roles you saved, applications you sent, people you need to follow up with, interviews to prepare for. Most people run it from memory and open browser tabs, and then lose good opportunities to <b>plain disorganisation</b>. This guide shows a simple system you can set up in an afternoon.</p><h2 id="why-job-searches-fall-apart">Why job searches fall apart<a class="pba" href="#why-job-searches-fall-apart" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><p>Effort is rarely the problem. The usual failures are quieter: you forget which version of your CV went to which company, a recruiter replies and you miss it for a week, or you apply to twenty roles in a rush and cannot say why you chose any of them.</p><aside class="pbo note"><b>Note</b><div>Honest note: a better system will not fix a CV that does not match the roles you apply for. Fix the match first, then organise.</div></aside><h2 id="step-1-keep-every-role-in-one-place">Step 1: Keep every role in one place<a class="pba" href="#step-1-keep-every-role-in-one-place" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><p>Pick one home for all roles and companies. A spreadsheet works. A dedicated tracker such as <a href="https://hvworld.haritt.com/vault/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HV Vault</a> does the same job with less typing, because you can save a job from a link instead of copying details by hand. What matters is that nothing lives only in your head or in an open tab.</p><figure><img src="/playbooks/img/ut4bgrd5qyxmv5u.webp" alt="HV Vault pipeline board with wishlist, saved, preparing and applied stages" width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Every role in one board, from wishlist to offer. HV Vault.</figcaption></figure><h2 id="step-2-record-the-few-details-that-matter">Step 2: Record the few details that matter<a class="pba" href="#step-2-record-the-few-details-that-matter" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><div class="pbtb"><table><thead><tr><th>Detail</th><th>Why it matters</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Role and company</td><td>So you can compare options fairly</td></tr><tr><td>Date applied and CV version</td><td>So you know what each company saw</td></tr><tr><td>Contact person</td><td>So you can follow up by name</td></tr><tr><td>Next action and date</td><td>So nothing waits unnoticed</td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2 id="step-3-plan-the-day-not-just-the-search">Step 3: Plan the day, not just the search<a class="pba" href="#step-3-plan-the-day-not-just-the-search" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><p>A long to-do list of applications creates pressure without direction. Choose <mark>two or three outcomes for the day</mark>, for example: send two tailored applications, follow up with one recruiter, practise one interview answer. A one-task-at-a-time planner such as <a href="https://hvworld.haritt.com/reset/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HV Reset</a> suits this, because it keeps one task on screen with a focus timer instead of a wall of tasks.</p><figure><img src="/playbooks/img/xr7iljuq97ywzdd.webp" alt="HV Reset focus timer showing one task at a time" width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>One task on screen, with a timer. HV Reset.</figcaption></figure><h2 id="step-4-follow-up-on-a-schedule">Step 4: Follow up on a schedule<a class="pba" href="#step-4-follow-up-on-a-schedule" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><p>Following up is where most candidates lose ground, because it is easy to forget and slightly uncomfortable. Set the next action date when you apply. A short, polite message after about a week is normal. If there is still no reply, one more nudge is reasonable, then move on.</p><figure><img src="/playbooks/img/6l0aj3sf8lai16k.webp" alt="HV Vault action centre listing follow-ups that are due" width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>A good tracker tells you who to follow up with today. HV Vault.</figcaption></figure><ol><li>Apply and write the follow-up date in your tracker.</li><li>Send one short message on that date.</li><li>If there is no reply, send one more a week later.</li><li>Then mark it closed and put your energy into live opportunities.</li></ol><h2 id="step-5-review-every-week">Step 5: Review every week<a class="pba" href="#step-5-review-every-week" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2><p>Spend twenty minutes once a week.
