What GA4 does for HV Verse, next to your own counter

GA4 is Google's free analytics tool. Learn what it adds, and which tool you trust for decisions.

Your own counterPrivate, simple, yours

No cookies, no user IDs, totals per day, device based.

vs
GA4Richer, Google-run

Cookies, per-event detail, traffic sources, Google links.

1
GA4 stands for Google Analytics 4. It is a free tool from Google that records what happens on a site or app: who arrived, from where, which pages they saw and which actions they took. You already have something similar: your own counter, which keeps only daily totals, uses no cookies or user IDs, and respects Do Not Track. A GA4 property for HV Verse was also added on 20 October. So you have two tools, and they will not agree, because the counter counts devices per day while GA4 counts cookie-based users.
2
Why have GA4 at all? Three reasons. It breaks down traffic by source in a standard way. It lets you mark important actions as key events and compare them across channels. And it can link to Google Search Console later, when search starts to matter. What it costs you is privacy: it sets first-party cookies, and your own privacy text once said "no third-party trackers". My reading of your files is simple: use your own counter for decisions, GA4 as a second opinion, and set GA4 up so it collects as little as possible.
3
This lesson teaches the whole setup from nothing, even though a property already exists, because you need to know every step to check that it is right. Where a screen is shown, it is an illustration, and Google renames menus often, so look for the same idea under a nearby name.
Do you need this now? Yes, but only the careful basics. The apps are live and the launch window is when you most want clean numbers. Do not build elaborate reports yet. What you need now is a working tag on every page, a handful of correct events, source tags on your links, and privacy settings that match your promise. Traffic is small, so GA4 will tell you about direction, not exact rates.

Creating the property and the web data stream

A property is the container for one product's data. A data stream is the feed from one site.

GA4: Admin, create propertyIllustration
analytics.google.com > Admin (gear, bottom left) > Create > Property
1Admin gearBottom left. Account and property settings live here.
2Create, then PropertyOpens the new property form.
3Property nameFor example HV Verse. Pick time zone and currency.
4Business detailsChoose industry size and goals. They do not change the data.
5Data stream, then WebEnter the site address and a stream name.

Skip this part if the HV Verse property exists and you can open it. Otherwise, sign in at analytics.google.com with the Google account that will own it. Use a shared team address you can keep for years, not a throwaway. A Google Analytics account is a folder; inside it a property holds one product's data; inside the property a data stream is one source of data, such as a website. One web stream for thehvverse.com is enough, because the apps live on the same site. A separate stream is only needed for a native Android or iOS app later.

1Account, then propertyOpen Admin.

Click the gear icon, labelled Admin, at the bottom left. Choose Create, then Account if you have none, or Property inside the account. Name the property HV Verse, set the reporting time zone to India and the currency to Indian rupee.

2They can be skipped lightlyAnswer the business questions.

Pick an industry and size that fit and any goals. These only shape suggestions. Accept the terms. Do not turn on sharing options you do not need.

3Data streamChoose Web.

When asked for a platform, choose Web. Type thehvverse.com and the name HV Verse site. Leave Enhanced measurement on for now, which records page views, scrolls, outbound clicks, site search, file downloads and page changes in a single-page app.

4Starts with G-Copy the measurement ID.

After you click Create stream, the stream details page shows a measurement ID that starts with G- (HV Verse's is G-ERS8360P56 in your files). Save it. In the same page you can open the tag instructions, which show the script to paste into your pages.

5Admin, then Data collection and modificationSet data retention.

Open Data retention and choose the longer option for event data. The shorter option is two months. A longer window lets you compare back to launch.

Installing the tag and proving it works

Add the tag to every page, then check Realtime and DebugView.

GA4: DebugViewIllustration
Admin > Data display > DebugView
1Device pickerTop: choose the debug device that is sending events.
2Event streamEach event appears as a bubble in time order.
3Click an eventOpens its parameters, such as the app name.
4Seconds timelineShows what fired, and in what order.
5Realtime reportReports, then Realtime, shows the last 30 minutes.

The tag is a short script from Google, called gtag.js, that sends events to your property. It must load on every page you want to measure, including each app page, not only the home page. Your files show the tag is not visible in the local copy of the HV Verse site, so you cannot yet be sure it loads everywhere. Install it by pasting the snippet from the tag instructions into the head section of every page or into the shared template that builds the pages. Do not install it on admin pages.

GGive it the same defaults as the group siteOrder matters: set consent before the tag

Before the main script runs, set the consent defaults: ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization denied, analytics storage granted. Then turn off Google signals and ad personalization signals in the config line. This copies the settings your parent site uses, and it means no advertising features are ever on. Skip the tag entirely when someone has Do Not Track, Global Privacy Control, or your opt-out set, as your own counter does. Ask whoever edits the pages to do this, and use your change log.

ASeconds, not hoursRealtime check.

Open your site in a private window and tap around. In GA4 open Reports, then Realtime. Within a minute you should see one user, the page title, and your events. Realtime shows the last half hour only, and it is the fastest proof that the tag works.

BOne user, every eventDebugView check.

Realtime lists counts. DebugView shows each event with its parameters, for one device at a time. Turn on debug mode by using Google's Tag Assistant, or by adding debug_mode set to true to the config call while testing. Then open Admin, then Data display, then DebugView and watch the stream while you use an app.

CNot just the home pageTest each app.

Open HV Reset, HV Hunt, HV Assess and HV AI, one by one. Do the activation action in each, such as finishing a first task. If page views appear but the app is missing, the tag is not in that app's template.

DInternal trafficExclude yourself.

Your own visits will pollute small numbers. In the web stream settings, find Configure tag settings, then Define internal traffic, and add your IP rule. Then open Data filters and set the internal traffic filter to Testing first and Active only after you have checked it. Phone networks change IPs, so this is only partly reliable.

Part 5 of 7

Events, key events and the activation events of each app

Send your existing activation events to GA4, then mark only the ones that matter as key events.

GA4: Events listIllustration
Admin > Data display > Events
1Events tableLists every event name GA4 has received.
2Mark as key eventA toggle or star beside the name.
3Create eventBuilds a new event from an existing one by rule.
4Custom definitionsUnder Data display: register parameters as dimensions.
5Key events pageA list of what you marked, with counts.

An event is one action GA4 records, such as a page view or a click. Some are automatic. Your own events, like activation, are custom events: your app code sends them with one extra line. Wherever the app already records an event for your own counter, send the same name to GA4 as well, for example gtag('event', 'task_done', { app: 'reset' }). Use the same lowercase names with underscores in both tools, so the two can be compared. Never put an email, a name, a job title or an assessment answer in an event or parameter. Google forbids sending personal data, and your HV Assess promise says answers stay in the browser.

A parameter is an extra detail attached to an event. If every app sends an event called activation, add a parameter app with the value reset, hunt, assess or ai, so you can split the count. GA4 collects the parameter but only shows it in reports once you register it as a custom dimension: Admin, then Data display, then Custom definitions, then Create custom dimension, with scope Event and the parameter name. It takes about a day to appear.

1One or two per appSend the events.

Add the activation event for each app with the app parameter, plus test_start, test_complete and share_link for HV Assess. Skip anything you cannot name a decision for.

2Check the Events listSee them arrive.

Events appear in Admin, then Data display, then Events, after GA4 receives them, often within a day. Use DebugView to see them at once.

3The ones that equal successMark key events.

Mark as a key event: activation (all four apps) and perhaps application sent on the Beta page. Key events were once called conversions. There is a limit on how many a property can hold, so mark few. Page views are not key events.

4Sign-in is not activationDo not mark noise.

A sign-in only proves a Google button worked. Use the first useful action as the key event.

Part 6 of 7

UTM tags, Google signals off, consent and privacy

Tag your own links, turn off the advertising features, and make your privacy text true.

GA4: Data collectionIllustration
Admin > Data collection and modification > Data collection
1Google signalsTurn data collection off. It links users across devices for ads.
2Granular locationLeave off unless you need city level detail.
3Ads personalizationTurn off, since there are no ads.
4Data sharing settingsUntick the sharing options you do not need.
5Data retentionNext section: pick the event retention window.

A UTM tag is a label added to the end of a link, such as ?utm_source=whatsapp&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=202610_launch&utm_content=hostel1. It tells GA4 and your own counter where the visit came from. The four parts are source (the platform), medium (the type), campaign (the push) and content (the exact post). Without a tag, visits from WhatsApp, Instagram and LinkedIn apps show as Direct, since those apps hide where the click came from, and you cannot tell which effort worked. Your admin has a link maker on the Links tab that builds the tags, and eight short links such as /wa, /ig and /li add a source. Keep names lowercase and consistent, because GA4 treats whatsapp and WhatsApp as two sources. Replace ?src= with utm_ tags everywhere, since your counter does not read src. Do not tag links inside your own site, which would overwrite the real source.

Next, the settings that protect your students. Google signals is a feature that links a person across devices using Google accounts, and it exists to support advertising. You run no ads, so turn it off: Admin, then Data collection and modification, then Data collection, then switch off Google signals data collection. Turn off ad personalization too, and review Data sharing settings so nothing is shared that you do not need. Keep granular location off.

SSay what is trueConsent and the privacy text

Your HV Verse privacy text said "No ads, no third-party trackers" before GA4 was added. The changelog says notices were updated, but the live wording cannot be checked from your files. Open the live page and read it. If GA4 is loaded, say so in one plain line: GA4 is used with ads features off, with an opt-out. Your students include people under 18, and Indian data protection law has special rules for children, including consent from a parent. This is not legal advice: have a qualified person check the wording and the sign-in flow.

YYour choiceBanner or no banner

Today there is no cookie banner. Analytics storage is granted by default, with opt-out through Do Not Track, Global Privacy Control or your link. A consent banner that asks before GA4 loads is clearer and safer, though it adds friction and loses some data. You decide; either way, the text must match the setup.

Reading GA4 and fixing it when numbers look wrong

Start with three reports, then investigate mismatches in a fixed order.

What do your numbers show?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyTag not loading

Most likelyEvent code

Look in DebugView for the name. Check spelling.

Most likelyMissing UTM tags

Most likelyConsent or blockers

Different counting and some visitors opt out.

Most likelyCustom dimension missing

Register the parameter, then wait a day.

Most likelyInternal traffic

Check the filter is Active, not Testing.

For reading, three places are enough at first. Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition shows sessions by channel, where your UTM tags appear. Reports, then Engagement, then Events lists each event with its count. The key events view shows how many times each key event fired. Compare a full week with the week before, and do not read any rate from fewer than a few hundred sessions. A fuller reading guide comes in lesson 11.

When a number looks wrong, test in this order. First, is the tag on that page: Realtime. Second, does the event fire: DebugView. Third, is it named and spelled the same as in your plan. Fourth, is the source tagged. Fifth, are your own visits filtered. Sixth, does the gap with your own counter make sense, given cookies and device-day counting? Change one thing, repeat the test, then wait a day before judging reports, since standard reports lag.

1GA4 against your counterCompare weekly.

Once a week, compare total sessions in both. They will differ, but the gap should stay steady. A sudden change in the gap points at a broken tag.

2Date, change, reasonKeep a setup log.

Each change to the tag, events or filters gets one line. It saves hours when numbers jump.

3Ads decisionReview at three months.

When you revisit paid ads, GA4 setup is the base. If GA4 never changed a decision, consider dropping it and keeping only the counter.
