1
Right now HV Verse has two ways to count visitors, and they do not agree with each other. The first is your own small counter, a script called a.js. It adds one to a number in your database each time something happens, and it feeds the admin dashboard you already built, with its tiles, funnel panels, the Ecosystem tab and the retention table. The second is Google Analytics 4, which everyone calls GA4. It is a free Google tool, and your HV Verse property was added on 20 October. A dashboard is simply a few numbers on one screen that tell you whether things are on track, what changed and what to do next.
2
This lesson teaches you to read both, and to build a small report you open on the same day every week. It covers the main GA4 reports (Realtime, Acquisition, Engagement and Retention), the Explore area where funnels live, comparisons, key events, and a free Looker Studio page that shows GA4 numbers as a clean dashboard. Menu names change from time to time, so treat every click path here as a guide and look for the closest label if one has moved.
Do you need this now? Yes, but a small version. HV Verse launched on 1 October, you decided on organic only, and there are no numbers on file yet. You do not need a big dashboard. You need one weekly page with about five numbers, and you need the weekly-active-users tile on your admin dashboard to stop showing 0, because a zero there means you cannot trust the retention side. Fix that first. Looker Studio can wait until GA4 has a few weeks of data.

The reason to learn both tools is simple. Your counter is private and already shaped around your three apps and their funnels. GA4 is better at following the same person over several visits and at slicing data by channel, device and country. Using both lets you check one against the other, which is how you catch bugs like the zero.

Reports are only as good as what feeds them. Before you trust any number, go through the checks below. For each one you will see why it matters, how to check it, and where HV Verse stands from your files.

AThe GA4 tag is really on your pagesNo tag means an empty report, however well you build it.

The tag is a small piece of code that sends each visit to Google. Your files show the property for thehvverse.com (including HV Reset, HV Hunt and HV Assess) was added on 20 October, but the live pages are not in the local files, so I cannot confirm the tag is installed.

How to check: open thehvverse.com on your phone, then in GA4 open Reports, then Realtime. Within a minute you should see one user. If you do, the tag works. If not, the tag is missing or blocked.

BKey events are chosenKey events are the few actions you want counted as success.

A key event is an event you mark as important, such as a sign-up or finishing a test. GA4 used to call these conversions, and some screens still do. The files say only the default tag is in place, so no key events are set. Your apps already fire events like signup, activation and test_complete in your own counter, but GA4 only knows events that are sent to it.

How to check: open Admin, then Data display, then Events, and look for a star or toggle beside each event.

CLinks carry UTM labelsWithout labels, GA4 files most visits under Direct.

A UTM label is text added to the end of a link, such as ?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=beta-oct. GA4 reads it and files the visit under the right channel. Your short links like /ig and /wa already tag a source. Beta applications still carry no source at all.

DThe weekly-active-users tile worksA zero here means the retention side cannot be trusted.

On your admin dashboard the weekly and monthly active users tiles showed 0 in the 13 October screenshot, although 60 device-days were not new. Those zeros cannot be right. The cause has not been found, so fixing it comes before any growth push.

EYour own visits are filtered outYour own tests inflate a tiny base very fast.

Your counter already has a switch called "Real visitors only (without me)". GA4 has its own way: define your internet address as internal traffic under Admin, then Data collection and modification, then Data filters. On a phone with changing addresses this is weak, so also test from a browser profile you can ignore.

If the tag works and the zero is understood, you are ready for the reports. If not, spend the week on those two fixes, since reading broken numbers is worse than reading none.

Open analytics.google.com, pick your HV Verse property at the top left, and choose Reports from the left menu. Each report answers one question. Menu names can change, so if a label has moved, look for the closest one.

RRealtimeUse it to check that something just worked.

Reports, then Realtime. It shows users in the last 30 minutes, the pages they are on and the events firing. Use it as a test bench: click a UTM-labelled link on your phone and watch it appear with the right source. It is not for judging how you are doing, because half an hour of data on a young site is mostly noise.

AAcquisitionWhere visitors come from.

Reports, then Acquisition. Two reports matter. User acquisition shows how new users first arrived. Traffic acquisition shows the channel of each session, so a returning person who comes from a link is counted again. Your own dashboard has the same split as first touch and last touch. For HV Verse, read these for Direct, Organic Social, Organic Search and Referral. A big Direct share usually means missing labels. The line "Unassigned" means GA4 could not classify the visit.

EEngagementWhat people do once they arrive.

Reports, then Engagement, then Events, Key events (or Conversions on older screens), and Pages and screens. Events lists every action with its count. Pages and screens shows which pages get views and how long people stay. Check that your own events appear: if activation is missing here, GA4 is not receiving it.

RRetentionDo they come back.

Reports, then Retention. You see new users against returning users, a retention curve, and a cohort table where each row is a group of people who first came in the same period. The cohort table needs weeks of data before it shows anything useful, so expect blanks at first.

CComparisonsCompare slices side by side.

At the top of most reports there is an option to add a comparison. Choose a dimension such as Device category, then a value such as Mobile. GA4 shows that slice next to everyone else. Try Mobile against Desktop, and New users against Returning users. Most students will be on phones, so a gap between the two is an early warning about your pages.

Most mistakes come from reading one number alone. Here is each metric in plain words, with what it means for HV Verse and how it differs from your own counter.

MetricWhat it meansHow to read it for HV Verse
UsersActive users: different people (really browsers or devices) who had an engaged session in the period.Your "reach". One student on phone and laptop counts as two.
New usersUsers whose first ever visit fell in the period.Compare with Users. If almost all are new, nobody returns.
SessionsVisits. A new one starts after 30 minutes of inactivity.Sessions divided by users shows how often each person visits.
Engaged sessionsSessions that lasted over 10 seconds, or had a key event, or had 2 or more page views.Your counter uses a close rule: 10 seconds, 2 pages or an event.
Engagement rateEngaged sessions divided by sessions.Low rate on one channel means that channel sends the wrong people or a weak first screen.
Event countHow many times an event fired.Counts actions, not people. One student can fire 20.
Key eventsEvents you marked as success.Mark sign-up and activation. Count them per channel.
Retention cohortA group that started together, followed over time.Read across a row: does it flatten above zero?
1
Two traps are worth remembering. First, GA4 numbers are often smaller than your counter's because GA4 needs the tag to load and counts only engaged users in some views, while your counter counts every page load. Second, your dashboard says "Visitors" means daily unique devices added up across days, so Visitors across a month is not the number of people. Never quote it as people. GA4's Users figure is the closer answer to "how many different people".
2
A single week of a young site moves a lot. Look at the trend over several weeks and write down what you did that week (a post, a poster, a fix) so a rise or fall has a possible reason. Small cohorts swing wildly, and your dashboard warns about this too.

Build the funnel and a free Looker Studio dashboard

Funnel exploration for drop-off, then a one-page Looker Studio report you refresh weekly.

GA4: Explore, Funnel explorationIllustration
Explore > Template gallery > Funnel exploration
1Steps (left panel)Add events in order, such as page view, signup, activation.
2Open or closed funnelClosed needs step one first; open lets people join anywhere.
3BreakdownSplit by device or channel to see who drops off.
4Elapsed timeHow long people take between steps.
5Date rangeLimited by event data retention in Admin.

Funnel analysis counts how many people reach each step and where they leave. The share lost between two steps is the drop-off. The biggest drop is the first place to look.

1Make a funnel explorationOpen Explore from the left menu of GA4.

Click Explore in the left menu, then the Template gallery, then Funnel exploration. Click the pencil beside Steps. Add each step as an event, in order. For HV Reset use the same steps as your admin dashboard: visit, click sign in, signed in, saved a plan, completed a task. Choose a closed funnel so people must start at step one, then add a Breakdown by Device category.

Events appear here only if GA4 receives them. If your apps fire activation and test_complete only into the first-party counter, GA4 will not show them, so check Reports, then Engagement, then Events first.

2Extend event data retentionOne setting that decides how far back you can look.

Go to Admin, then Data collection and modification, then Data retention. The default for event data in Explorations is short. Set it to the longest option offered, otherwise your cohorts and funnels lose old days.

3Connect Looker StudioFree, shareable, and it refreshes itself.

Open lookerstudio.google.com, click Create, then Report. When it asks for data, choose the Google Analytics connector, pick your account, then your HV Verse property, and click Add. A blank page appears. Add a Scorecard for each number, and a Time series for trend. Use the same five numbers each week, such as users, new users, engaged sessions, sign-ups (a key event) and activation.

Add one table: sessions by default channel group. Set the date range to "Last 28 days" with comparison to "Previous period". Name the report clearly and keep it private.

To update it weekly, open the report. It refreshes from GA4 by itself, so you only change the date range if you fixed one. Then copy the five numbers into a Google Sheet row with the date and one line on what you did. The sheet is your memory; the dashboard is only today's picture. If GA4 later becomes your main source, this view replaces the manual sheet. Until then, your own admin dashboard stays the main one.

When the numbers look wrong

Match the symptom to the likely cause, check it, fix it, then compare again next week.

When a number looks odd, resist the urge to change the product. First decide whether the number is wrong or the world is. Work from the cheapest check to the most expensive one.

1TrackingIs the data being collected at all?

Use Realtime in GA4 and the live counts in your admin. Visit with a labelled link and see whether it appears. If it does not, fix collection before anything else.

2Compare sourcesDo two sources tell the same story?

Pick one single day and compare GA4 Users with the dashboard's daily unique devices. They will not match exactly, but they should be close. A big gap on one day points to a bug or a blocked tag. Do not compare a month of device-days to a month of users.

3Slice itWhich slice is behaving differently?

Add comparisons: mobile against desktop, one channel against another, new against returning. A problem that shows up in one slice is a page or audience problem. A problem in all slices is probably tracking or the product.

4Fix and retestWrite the fix and one line of what you expect.

Change one thing, note the date, and read the same number next week. If tiny traffic makes this hard, compare before and after with care and keep the note of anything else that changed. Remove any tile that has no owner and leads to no action. The goal is a page you read in ten minutes and act on, not a page that looks full.
