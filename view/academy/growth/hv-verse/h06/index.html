Why community is your main route

Word of mouth among students is the one growth route you can start for free, today.

CommunitySitting inside groups

A few real students try it and tell their friends.

1
Community here means the groups students already live in: WhatsApp groups of a class or a hostel, college clubs, placement cells, Discord servers, and the circle of friends around a few active students. Word of mouth means one of them telling another, "try this planner, it helped me". It is the oldest form of growth and, for a free product with no ad budget, it is the most natural one. Your own files agree: the Student Beta programme is listed as HV Verse's strongest route, because it lets students test real apps, report bugs and earn points, and that gives them a reason to talk about it.
2
The second idea in this lesson is the small creator, sometimes called an influencer. Do not picture a famous person with a million followers. For HV Verse the useful person is a final-year student who posts study tips, a club lead who runs a placement group, or a fresher who shares job-hunt diaries. Because the apps are free there is no commission to share, so the offer is early access, credit and a real say in what you build. Any gift or payment needs your approval and a clear disclosure that it was given, which we cover later.
3
Why does this beat posting alone? A post on your own account is seen mostly by people who already know you, and the name HV Verse is only a few days old (the rename happened on 19 October). A message from a classmate comes with trust already attached. It also reaches the exact audience you have drafted: students aged 16 to 22 and freshers aged 21 to 26.
Do you need this now? Yes, but in a careful order. Community is the main organic route you have decided on, and it costs only time. The Beta campaign is built, yet it is not ready to send because it still has unfilled placeholders and mixed details. So the first job is to fix the assets, then send to one or two groups. Influencer work waits until a few real testers have said good things.

Before you send anything to a group, check six things. If most are red, starting would be premature: students would meet a poster with a blank link, a form you cannot trace, and a name that differs from the one on the page. That wastes the first impression, and in a small college circle you do not get a second one.

AThe posters and message must have a real link and contactFill every placeholder.

Both posters, the 19 second video and the WhatsApp message still show "[Application link]" and "[WhatsApp number / email]" in your files. A call to action that is a placeholder gives a student nothing to do. Put in thehvverse.com/beta/ with a tag (explained in part 4) and one contact line.

BMake the campaign say one thingOne name, one duration, no other brand's logo.

The campaign is called Student Beta Program in some places and Beta Lab in others, and the length is 7 to 10 days in one place and 10 days in another. The posters and video also carry logos of other brands, which goes against your rule that HV Verse and its apps are branded on their own. Choose one name and one length, and remake the footer.

CSave where each applicant came fromThe form must tell you which group worked.

The Beta form stores name, profession, why, WhatsApp or phone, and email, but no source. If ten students apply you will not know whether the college poster or the hostel group sent them. Lesson 9 covers adding a hidden source field. Without it, community results stay a guess.

DWalk through each app as a strangerApps must work for a first-time visitor.

Open HV Reset, HV Hunt, HV Assess and HV AI on a phone, signed out. If sign-in fails or a page still shows an old name such as HV World, fix that first. Students who join the Beta will try the apps within minutes.

EDecide who answersTwo people, a few minutes each day.

Your social plan asks for about 30 minutes of outreach a day. Decide who replies to WhatsApp and email applicants, and how fast. A student who applies and hears nothing for a week is gone.

FTell applicants how their details are usedYour privacy line must match what you do.

The consent wording on the Beta form cannot be checked from your files. Add one line saying how the answers are used. Your students include 16 and 17 year olds, so keep what you collect to the minimum.

Planning: who to approach, and with what

Pick one first group, one message, and a short list of real people.

GroupsClass, hostel, club groups

Ask the admin first. Never join to spam.

Student ambassadorsEarly access and credit

Free, honest, no payment.

Small creatorsCareer and study accounts

Only later, and only with disclosure.

Start by choosing the first target group. Your plan lists students and freshers, but it is a draft and no first group is chosen. For the community route, freshers are the more natural pick, because every app points at job seekers and a fresher group is full of people who are about to apply for jobs. That is my reading; you decide. Whichever you choose, write one message for them in a single line: who it is for, what they get, what you want them to do. The Beta posters already have strong lines, such as "Your next project shouldn't run on dummy data".

Then make a short list of real people, not a list of platforms. Write down five student creators or club leads you already know. Ask each one for a small, honest thing: try the app, tell you what is confusing, and share the link if they like it. Offer early access and credit by name only if they agree. This is the lowest-risk way to start, and the list teaches you more about your audience than any post.

HHow to behave in a WhatsApp or college groupSharing in groups without being a nuisance

Ask the group admin before posting. Share once, in plain words, with a reason it helps them, and stay to answer questions. Do not message members one by one without being asked. A group that finds you pushy will remove you and warn others, and that damages a name that is still new.

KKeep this for laterPaying or gifting a creator

A small fee to a micro creator who covers careers and study topics is possible, but it needs your approval, a clear disclosure such as "sponsored" or "gifted", and a tagged link so you can count the result. Until a few free tests work, skip it. It is still organic-only for now.

The link is an example. The exact medium the tool adds is decided by the tool. Menu names can change.

A tagged link is a normal web address with a short label added on the end, so your analytics can see where a visit came from. The label has four parts: source (the platform), medium (the type of use), campaign (the push it belongs to) and content (the exact poster or group). Without it, a visit from a WhatsApp group shows up as Direct, because WhatsApp hides where the click came from. Your admin dashboard already has a link maker that builds these for you, and your counter reads all four parts.

1Make one link per groupOpen the link maker.

In your admin dashboard, open the Links tab and find the link maker. Pick the page (the Beta page), pick the channel, keep the default campaign or type your own, and add a content tag such as hostel1 or poster1. Copy the finished link. Menu names may change, so look for the link maker in whatever the Links area is called.

2Use utm_ tags, not ?src=Fix the poster note.

Your Beta notes suggest ?src=poster1, but the counter does not read src. Use the form /beta/?utm_source=qr&utm_content=poster1 instead, as your own plan suggests. For a poster, turn the link into a QR code so a phone can open it.

3Start small and watchSend to one group first.

Send the message to one group, or two. Keep a note of the date, the group, the exact words and the tag. Do not send the next group until you have read what the first one did.

4Write one line per sendKeep a simple log.

Date, group, link tag, number reached, replies, applications. This becomes your learning record, and your files show no such log exists yet.

Community results are small and personal, so read them as a short chain. First, how many people were reached (an estimate is fine). Second, how many opened the page: in your dashboard, look at the channels and campaigns tables for the tag you used. Third, how many applied: the Signups page lists every Beta application with a status of new, contacted, joined or closed. Fourth, how many joined. Fifth, how many then did something real in an app, such as finishing a first task in HV Reset or adding a first job in HV Hunt. That last step matters most, because the point is use, not sign-ups.

The made-up example shows the shape. Of 120 people reached, 36 visited, 14 applied, 10 joined and 6 did a first task. The leak that matters is whichever step drops most, not the biggest number. The application count itself is not on file for HV Verse, and the source is not stored yet, so for now you may need to match applicants to groups by hand until the form is fixed.

TThree signals, in order of strengthHow to tell it worked

Strongest: students who joined and then used an app again a week later. Next: unprompted replies, shares or friends tagged. Weakest: views and likes. Also read the free text. Bug reports and ideas from the Beta are the best feedback you will get this month.

TTen people can misleadBe careful with small numbers

With a handful of applicants, one extra person moves a rate a lot. Compare groups only when you have repeated the same message in several, and treat one good group as a lead, not a result.

1ReachedHow many saw the message

An estimate from the group size. It is the only guessed number.

Write it down at the time you send. Without it you cannot find the rate of the next step.

2Visits with your tagDid they open the link

In the Campaigns panel, find your content tag. Divide visits by reached.

Low rate: message, timing or the wrong group. Zero visits with replies on WhatsApp: the link was broken or untagged. Action: test the link on a phone, then rewrite the first line.

3AppliedDid the page convince them

Count on the Signups page, newest first. Divide applications by visits.

Many visits and few applications points to the page or form: too many fields, no clear button. Action: shorten the form, then resend to a similar group.

4JoinedDid you follow up

Applicants whose status you set to joined. Divide by applications.

A big gap means slow or missing replies. A student who hears nothing for a week is gone. Action: set a reply time and keep to it.

5First taskDid they use an app

The activation event inside the app, counted in your analytics. Divide by joined.

Joined but no first task: the product first minute or the wrong expectation. Ask five of them what stopped them.

When a community push does not work, resist the urge to send it to more groups. Find the first step in the chain where the numbers break, because that tells you which part to fix. Tap through the symptoms above, then decide what to change and what to keep the same.

1Change a single thingOne change at a time.

If the page is the problem, change the page. If the message is the problem, rewrite the first line. Changing both means you will not know which helped.

2Send the new version to a comparable groupRetest in a similar group.

Use a group of similar size and type, with a new content tag such as hostel2. Compare the two chains side by side. Poster A in one college and poster B in another is a fair, simple A/B comparison for your traffic.

3Decide with the log in front of youScale, repeat or stop.

If a group gave you several joins who used the app, repeat with similar groups and ask the first group to share. If three different groups gave nothing and the chain broke at the same step, stop sending and fix that step. Come back after the next three-month review you already set.
