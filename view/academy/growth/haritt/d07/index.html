Email for a studio means follow-ups

Most lost projects are lost to silence, not to a no.

Newsletter thinkingMass email to a big list

You have no list and no sign-up.

vs
Follow-up thinkingA short note to one real person

You have enquiries to answer today.

1
When people hear "email marketing" they picture newsletters and big lists. That is not your situation. Haritt Developers wins business through enquiries, one form and a handful of conversations. The email that matters most is the short note you send to one business owner who has already shown interest: after they send the form, after a call, after a proposal, after a project goes live. Marketers call the messages sent at each stage of a client's journey lifecycle emails, and for a studio the set is small.
2
From your files: the site sends an enquiry-received email to the person who filled the form and a new-enquiry email to your team, and a Developers welcome email exists but is sent by hand. Intro-email designs exist in 5 to 6 versions that frame a business problem. Nothing is recorded as sent. Missing are a follow-up after a proposal, a nudge for people who chose "Not sure yet", a check-in after launch, and a message to past clients.
3
Two other terms: outbound means you write first to someone who did not contact you (cold email), and a lead magnet is a free tip or download offered in return for an email address. Your site has no newsletter or lead-magnet sign-up today.
Do you need this now? Follow-up emails to people who already enquired: yes, now, by hand, with your go on each send. Cold outbound to strangers: later, small and only after the checks in Part 2. A newsletter: not yet, since you have no list and no regular things to say.

Email goes into a person's own inbox, so mistakes cost more than a weak post. Before you write the first message, check the five things below. They also decide whether you are ready for anything beyond hand-sent follow-ups.

AA reason to write to each personPermission comes from the situation.

Writing to someone who sent you an enquiry is expected: they asked. Writing to a stranger needs a real, specific reason, such as a visible problem on their site that you can name. Without that, it is spam, and it harms your name. Your files suggest sending a short note to a few relevant firms, not a blast.

How to check: for each recipient, write one sentence on why this email is useful to them. If you cannot, remove them.

BA simple way to stopAdd it before any first outbound send.

Your files say the footer says "reply STOP" and there is no unsubscribe link. For replies to enquiries this is fine. For anything outbound, tell people clearly how to opt out and honour it at once. Keep a list of those who said no, and never write to them again.

How to check: read your footer text and send yourself a test. Would a stranger know how to stop it?

CPipeline stages that match your follow-upsOtherwise you cannot tell who needs a nudge.

The inbox has New, Contacted and Closed, with notes and CSV export. It has no stage for proposal sent, quoted, won or lost. A follow-up needs to know who is waiting on a proposal. Until the inbox changes (a change that needs your OK), keep a small sheet matching each enquiry with its stage and the date of your last message.

How to check: pick any enquiry and see whether you can say in ten seconds what happened last and what happens next.

DA sender and limits you understandGmail works for dozens, not thousands.

Free Gmail sending allows about 100 recipients a day. That is plenty for your needs now. Keep a normal conversational tone, avoid large images and many links, and send individually. A dedicated email service with unsubscribe and open tracking is a later step if volume grows, and a small budget.

How to check: note which address sends and replies. Recipients should see the same one every time.

EA form tick for future contactNeeded before any newsletter.

The form has a consent tick for the privacy notice, but nothing for hearing from you again. If you ever want a list for later messages, add a clear, separate tick. This is also a form change that needs your approval.

How to check: read the consent text and see whether it covers more than answering the enquiry.

Planning the few emails that matter

Seven messages, each with one job and one trigger.

Thank you noteProposalFollow-up 1Follow-up 2
Launch check-inReview askReferral ask

Plan on one page. Write the trigger (what happened), the job of the message, and the one thing you want the reader to do. Keep each message short enough to read on a phone in half a minute, and always from a real address.

TThe reply to a new enquiryQuick and personal beats clever.

The automatic email confirms you got it. Your own message within one working day should answer their actual question, say what happens next, and offer two time slots for a short call. Your files show the form collects budget band and timeline, so refer to them: it shows you read it.

TThe follow-up after a proposalThe most valuable message you can send.

Send a short, kind note a few days after a proposal, asking if anything is unclear or if their plans changed. If there is no reply, send one more after a longer gap, then stop. Offer something useful: an option with a smaller first phase, or an honest answer to a likely objection. Never pressure.

TThe nudge for "Not sure yet"For people who chose it in the services list.

Those owners know they have a need but not the solution. A short note with two or three questions about their business, and an offer of a free first conversation, is often enough. Keep it about their problem, not your services.

PPost-launch check-in and review askWhen the project is live.

A few weeks after launch, ask how it is working. If the answer is good, ask for a two-line quote and permission to use it, then ask for one introduction. This one message feeds the proof lesson and the referral lesson at once.

CCold intro to a few firmsLater, small, and only with your go.

Your intro-email designs frame a business problem and open with "Hello," without a personal name. If you use them, add a real detail about the recipient before sending, or it will read as a template. Start with about 20 recipients chosen by hand and review the results before sending more.

Writing and sending with Gmail

Labels, templates and scheduled send keep follow-ups on time.

2BodyThree to five short lines. One clear question at the end.
3TemplatesIn the compose menu (three dots), insert a saved reply if enabled.
4Schedule sendThe arrow beside Send lets you pick a date and time.
5LabelsAdd a label such as Proposal sent to find the thread later.

You do not need a new tool to run these emails. Gmail already has what you need, and the admin inbox holds the record. The screens below are written generally, because Google renames menus from time to time. If something is not where described, use the search box in Gmail's settings.

1Save your messages as templatesWrite each once, edit before each send.

In Gmail's settings, open the Advanced tab and turn on Templates if it is offered, then save your proposal follow-up and launch check-in as templates. When composing, open the three-dot menu and insert a template. Always change the first line and one detail for the person. A template is a starting point, not a send button.

2Use labels as your stagesOne label per stage.

Create labels such as Enquiry replied, Proposal sent, Follow-up 1 sent and Won. Apply one to each thread. Gmail's left menu then shows what is waiting. This works until the admin inbox gets proper stages.

3Schedule the next nudgeSo you do not rely on memory.

Write the second follow-up when you send the first, and use the arrow beside Send to schedule it for a few days later. Cancel it if the person replies. Do the same for the launch check-in.

4Tag any link in the emailYour Links tab has an Email channel.

If an email contains a link to your site, make it in the Links tab with the email channel and a campaign name, so visits from emails show in analytics instead of Direct. Do not add tracking pixels or hidden images: your files show no open tracking, and replies are a better signal anyway.

5Log every sendThe admin inbox notes or your sheet.

Write date, message type and reply in the inbox note for that enquiry. Keep the habit even when it feels slow. This is the only data you will have, and it is what lets you improve.

Remember your own rule: no real send happens without your go. For outbound batches, check the list and the first message before anything leaves.

Made-up example, one small round. Bar widths are not to scale.

1
Because there is no open tracking, you cannot see who opened an email, and you should not try to guess from clicks. Count what is visible: replies, calls held, proposals sent and projects won. For follow-ups, the number to watch is simple: of the proposals you sent, how many got a reply after the first follow-up, and after the second.
2
For outbound batches, the reply rate is replies divided by emails sent. Treat a no as a useful reply, since it clears your list. Read the replies for patterns: what questions come back, which words people use, which firms respond. That is worth more than the rate itself, especially with only 20 recipients.

That is a decent first round for hand-built emails, though 20 is far too small to prove a rate. If instead you get 1 reply, change one thing and try 20 more. Never judge a message from five sends.

When nobody replies

Check delivery, the list, the reason, the message and the timing, in that order.

A silent inbox is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Walk down the chain from the technical to the human, and stop when you find the break. The box above lists common patterns, so tap the one closest to your log.

1Did it arrive?Test with a second address.

Send the same message to a personal address on another provider and check whether it lands in the inbox, the promotions tab or spam. Spam placement is more likely with many links, images, all-capital subject lines and sudden bursts to people who never heard of you. Fix those, send fewer, and ask a few friendly contacts to reply to build normal activity.

2Did it reach the right person?Read the list again.

If addresses are generic or old, the message goes nowhere. Prefer people with a visible need and a role that decides on websites. Twenty carefully chosen firms beat a hundred guessed ones.

3Was there a reason to reply?Read the first two lines as a stranger.

If your note starts with who you are and what you sell, it will be deleted. Start with the person's situation, and one useful observation. Ask one easy question. Compare the replies from two versions of the opening on the next batch.

4Was the next step small?Look at replies versus calls.

"Let me know if interested" asks for too much thought. Offering a short call with two time slots, or one question they can answer in a line, is easier to say yes to.

5Was it the offer or the proposal?Listen to what they say.

If conversations happen and projects do not, the cause is outside email: the price band, a missing proof, or a mismatched service. The proof and referral lessons address those. Email is only the messenger.

Retest by changing one thing per batch and keeping a log with the date, belief, change, result and decision. With small batches, expect noise, and repeat before concluding.

Growing from here

Move up only when the manual way starts to hurt.

Keep going by handWhile volume is small

Under about a few dozen messages a month.

Add a sign-upWhen you have things to share

A tick on the form, then useful notes.

Use an email serviceWhen you need unsubscribe and opens

A small budget, and your approval.

1
For now, hand-sent, personal emails are the right size. The signs that you have outgrown them are plain: you forget follow-ups, you send the same message more than a few times a week, or people ask to be added to updates.
2
If you add a sign-up, do it honestly: a separate tick on the form, and a promise of how often you will write and about what. Do not add anyone who did not agree. When you have enough regular contact to need unsubscribe links, opens and clicks, move to a proper email service with a small budget. This needs your approval, and the privacy notice must be updated to match.
3
Revisit this lesson once you have a month of logged follow-ups and at least one finished project to refer to. Update the intro emails with real proof, and drop any message type that has produced no replies after a fair try.
