What a lead is, and why the enquiry is only the start

An enquiry is not a client. You need to see what happens after the form.

TodayEnquiry, then silence

Inbox shows New, Contacted, Closed.

vs
GoalEnquiry to proposal

Every lead has a stage, a date and a next step.

1
A lead is a person or business that has shown interest in working with you. For Haritt Developers, the clearest lead is someone who sends the form after clicking Start a project. The site exists for that one action. But an enquiry is only the start of a long road: a reply, a conversation, a proposal (a written offer with scope and price), a decision. In a project business a buyer takes weeks to decide, and most enquiries will not become projects. If you only watch how many enquiries arrive, you miss where the real losses happen.
2
Right now your admin inbox for project enquiries has three states: New, Contacted and Closed. It also has private notes, a reply button and a CSV export (a plain file you can open in a sheet). That is a good base. What it cannot tell you is which enquiries were a good fit, which ones got a proposal, what each was worth, and which ones you lost and why. The form also saves no source, so you cannot tell whether a lead came from LinkedIn, Justdial, Google or a friend. Not on file: how many enquiries have come in so far.
3
This lesson is about the stretch from enquiry to proposal, and how to follow it with a simple free sheet. It does not need software you pay for.
Do you need this now? Yes. This is the cheapest and most useful growth work you can do, because it costs only a little time and every later decision depends on it. Your plan is no paid ads for the first 90 days, so the money lesson is not urgent, but you cannot judge any free channel until each enquiry has a source and a stage. Start the sheet before you do more outreach, so the first replies are already counted.

Before you build anything, check the ground. Each item below is something that has to exist for a pipeline (the road a lead follows until it is won or lost) to tell you the truth. For each one I say how to check it and where you stand today, from your files.

AThe form catches enough to sort leadsName, company, services, budget band, timeline and website are already asked.

The form asks for name, email, company, current website, which of the seven services they want (or Not sure yet), a project description, a budget range and a timeline. That is enough to judge fit. How to check: open the three most recent enquiries and see if you could tell, from the form alone, whether each is worth a call. If you could, the form is ready. There is no phone number field and no source field, which are the two gaps that hurt most.

BEvery enquiry lands in one placeForm saves go to the inbox; email fallbacks do not.

Every enquiry is saved to your database and emailed to the team. If the save fails, the form falls back to a pre-filled email, and those leads never appear in the inbox. Leads from Justdial, Google or direct email also never enter it. How to check: for one week, note every lead that reaches you by any route, then see if each one is in the inbox. Any that are missing must be typed into your sheet by hand.

COne person owns repliesThe team is two founders with no set roles.

Not on file: any owner for enquiries. A pipeline with no owner becomes a list nobody updates. How to check: ask who will reply to the next enquiry and who will update the sheet that day. If the answer is vague, decide it first. It can be one person for replies and the same one for the sheet.

DA written meaning of a good leadWithout it, every enquiry gets the same attention.

Nothing on file defines a good lead. A working suggestion, which you should confirm with your co-founder: an enquiry for a service you offer, with a real brief, a budget band you can work with and a timeline. If any of those is missing, it is not yet good. Write your own version in two lines and keep it in the sheet's first tab.

The stages from enquiry to proposal

Five stages with a written rule for moving from one to the next.

Qualifiedfits your rule
Call heldtalked, scope clear
Proposal sentoffer in writing

A stage is a named step on the road. The important part is not the name, it is the rule for entering it. Write the rule once, so that on a busy week you do not argue with yourself about whether a lead is real. Keep the stages few. Five is plenty for a two-person studio.

StageRule to enter itNext action
NewThe form arrived, nothing done yetReply within one working day
QualifiedMatches your written good-lead rule, or the sender answered your questions and now matches itOffer a call with a booking link
Call heldA real conversation took place and you know scope, budget and timelineSend a proposal, or close it politely
Proposal sentA written offer with scope, price and timeline went outFollow up on a set date
Won or LostThey said yes, or no, or went silent after two follow-upsRecord the value and the reason
1
Notice what the stages do to your habits. A lead that sits at New for three days is a visible fault. A lead at Proposal sent with no follow-up date is also a fault. These are your two most common leaks in a small studio: slow first replies, and proposals that go out and are never chased. The sheet makes both visible at a glance.
2
Your current inbox has Contacted, which fits between New and Qualified, and Closed, which you can keep as Lost. The missing ones are Qualified, Call held, Proposal sent and Won. Adding them to the inbox itself needs your OK, because it changes the admin tool. Until then, the sheet does the job alongside it.

Build the free pipeline sheet

Google Sheets, one row per lead, a dropdown for the stage.

A spreadsheet is enough at your size. If you already use Google, open Google Sheets, create a new sheet and call the first tab Leads. Each row is one enquiry. You can paste in the CSV from your admin inbox to start, then add the columns the inbox lacks. Menu names change from time to time, so if you cannot find an item, look for the same words in the Data or Format menu.

1
Setting up the dropdown. Click the letter above the Stage column, open Data, choose Data validation, add a rule, set the criteria to a dropdown, and type your stage names. Then use Format and Conditional formatting to colour each stage, so a glance down the column shows what needs you. Updating it: every time you reply or hold a call, change the stage and the next action date in the same minute. If you leave updating for the weekend, the sheet is always wrong.
2
The source column. The form does not save the source, so ask. Put a line in your first reply: "Where did you hear about us?" and write the answer in the sheet. Where a person came from a link you posted, add a tagged link (a web address with a label such as utm_source=linkedin on the end) so the visit shows the channel in your analytics. Putting a "How did you hear about us" question on the form itself is an open decision for you.
3
If you outgrow it. A free tier of a CRM tool (software that tracks leads and clients) is an option once the sheet has more rows than you can follow. Check the current free limits before you choose. Do not start there. A sheet you update beats a tool you do not open.

Reading the numbers

Count how many leads reach each stage, and how long they wait.

20enquiries in the month
1050% of new

Made-up example: 20 enquiries in a month. Your real numbers are not on file.

At the end of each month, count how many leads entered each stage, then divide each count by the one before it. The result is a conversion rate between stages. The chart above uses made-up numbers only to show the shape: most leads fall away early, and the numbers get small. With a handful of enquiries a month, one more win changes a rate a lot, so do not read a trend from one month. Read it as a list of leaks.

Also split everything by source. If three of four wins came from referrals and none from the site, that is the most useful fact in the sheet. Your source is the one thing the inbox cannot give you, which is why the column matters.

When leads stall or never convert

Find the stage where leads disappear, then work out why.

Where do leads disappear?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyWrong audience or unclear offer

Check days to first reply. Test a shorter reply with one booking link.

Most likelyCall has no clear close

After the call, say what you will send and by when.

Most likelyNo follow-up, or no trust

Check follow-up dates. Check whether proof is missing.

Compare lost quotes with the budget band the lead chose.

Most likelyHabit problem

Fix the owner and a fixed update time.

When the numbers look poor, resist the urge to say "we need more traffic". Use the sheet to find the stage where most leads leave. Then ask why, in a fixed order, from the cheapest check to the hardest.

1Is the sheet telling the truth?Check that every lead is in the sheet and stages are current.

Compare the sheet with the inbox and your email. Any lead missing, or sitting at New for weeks while you spoke to them, means the habit has slipped. Fix that first, because every other number depends on it.

2Is it the audience?Leads that do not fit the good-lead rule.

If most enquiries fail your rule, look at the source column and the service column. One channel may bring people with no budget or a request you do not do. Your site lists seven broad services and no ideal client, so a mixed crowd is likely. Not on file: which kind of client you want most. That is an open decision.

3Is it the reply?Slow or vague first response.

Look at days to first reply. The automatic email says the enquiry was received, but a human reply with three questions and a booking link is the real first step. Write one template and send it within one working day.

4Is it the proposal or the proof?Calls go well, proposals stall.

You have no client case studies or testimonials yet, and HV Verse is the only build you can show. A buyer who cannot see proof may go silent. Ask the next client for a short quote and approval to publish a case study.

Fix and retest. Pick one cause, change only that, and track the same rate for the next 10 to 15 leads before you decide. Small numbers are noisy, so ask for a clear gap before you believe it. Write the change, the date and the result in a Notes tab. If the rate does not move, cross the cause off and test the next one.

Next steps and when to revisit

Do three things this week, then upgrade the tool only if needed.

Replywithin one working day
Updatestage and next date

Find the leakone stage only

1
Here is what to do first, in order. Write your two-line good-lead rule. Create the Leads sheet and paste in every enquiry you can find from the inbox, your email and Justdial. Write the first-reply template with three questions and a booking link, and decide who sends it. After that, the pipeline runs as a loop: reply, update, count, find the leak, change one thing, count again.
2
Revisit the tool when the sheet has become hard to follow, or when you want automatic reminders. At that point consider asking for your OK to add Qualified, Proposal sent, Won, Lost and quote value to the admin inbox, since that keeps everything in one place. The next lesson on money maths uses the quote value and the won projects from this sheet, so the sooner you record them the sooner your cost per client means something.
3
One last check on readiness. After a month, ask yourself three plain questions. Do I know where each of my last ten leads came from? Do I know what stage each one is in today? Do I know what I will do next for each one? If any answer is no, the pipeline is not yet working for you, and the fix is the habit, not a new tool.
