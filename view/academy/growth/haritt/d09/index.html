Why the enquiry page is your whole sales counter

With about 10 visits a day, every visitor who leaves without writing is expensive.

More trafficPour more in

Needs reach, time or ad money you do not have yet.

vs
A better pageLose fewer

1
Picture a small workshop with a counter at the front. Ten people walk in each day, look around, and most walk out again without saying a word. You could spend money on a bigger signboard to bring in twenty people a day, but if the counter is confusing, you will just lose twenty people instead of ten. For Haritt Developers the counter is the page where a business owner decides to write to you: the Start a project page with the enquiry form, and the pages that lead to it.
2
This work has a name, conversion rate optimisation, or CRO. Conversion rate means the share of visitors who do the thing you want. Here the thing you want is a sent enquiry, which your own counter already records as enquiry_sent. From the 13 Oct screenshot you have 301 sessions in 30 days, roughly 10 a day, and the enquiry rate is not on file. So you cannot say yet what share of those visits turns into an enquiry. That is the first thing this lesson fixes.
3
There is one more idea to know now. Most of CRO is not testing two versions against each other. At your traffic it is plain repair work: remove anything that makes writing to you harder, slower or scarier, and make sure the page answers the visitor's silent questions. Who are you, can you do what I need, what does it cost, what happens after I press send.
Do you need this now? Yes. Your plan says no paid ads for the first 90 days, so every visit comes from search, listings and posts, and each one is hard to win. Repairing the page and the form costs no money and every future channel (search, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, email) will land on it. Do it before you add more traffic, and do it before you run any test, because a test on a page with obvious problems only tells you which broken version is less broken.

From your files, the form is better than most small-studio forms. It has nine inputs and a hidden trap field for bots (a honeypot). The inputs are Name and Email (both required), Company, Current website, the services you offer plus "Not sure yet", About the project (required, at least 10 characters), Budget range, Timeline and a required consent tick. If saving fails, the form opens a pre-filled email instead, so a lead is not lost. That is real care, and it means you do not need to rebuild anything.

What is missing is on the other side of the form. There is no phone or WhatsApp option, no question on how the person found you, and the saved record carries a fixed source text instead of the real channel. Above the form there is no proof for a visitor like them: your Work page shows in-house builds, including HV Verse, and there are no client case studies, testimonials or logos. The seven services all sit on one Services page, and each "Enquire about" button opens the form with that service ticked. And no PageSpeed or mobile result is on file, so whether the page is slow is not known.

Before you start repairing, check three things so you can later tell whether anything worked.

1Can you see enquiries counted?Without a count there is nothing to improve.

Open the admin dashboard Developers tab and note the enquiry rate and form completion. Their values are not in any file, so write down today's numbers on a sheet before you touch anything. If the tab shows nothing, send a test enquiry and confirm it appears.

2Is GA4 receiving enquiry_sent?Two tools should agree.

The next lesson sets up GA4 properly. For this one, you only need your own counter to be trustworthy. Compare the number of enquiries in the inbox with the number your counter says were sent over the same week. If they differ by more than one or two, fix tracking first.

3Do you know who the page is for?An ideal client is not chosen yet.

Your social plan lists small-business owner, founder or startup, and referrer, but no ideal client is chosen. Without one, headline and proof cannot be specific. My reading: pick one for now, even a guess, and write it on a card beside the page you edit.

Repairing the form: remove friction, keep qualification

Friction is anything that makes writing to you harder, slower or scarier.

Landknows it is right place
Decidetrusts and sees next step
Start formform_start fires

Friction means anything that makes the action harder, slower or scarier. Every field costs the visitor a little effort, and every unclear message makes them wonder whether something bad happens when they press send. Your form is already short enough for a service business, so the goal is not to cut it down. The goal is to remove doubt and to add the two things you need to learn from every lead.

AAdd a source questionEvery lead should tell you how they found you.

Add one optional question, "How did you hear about us?", with a few answers: Google search, Google Maps or Justdial, LinkedIn, Instagram, a friend or referral, other. Also save the UTM values from the link they arrived on in hidden fields (the next lesson explains UTM tags). Together these turn each lead into data about a channel, which today it is not. Changing the form is a change to the live site, so draft the wording and ask for the change to be made in the website thread before you expect it to appear.

OOffer a way to reply that people preferSome owners would rather give a number than an email.

Many small-business owners in India live on WhatsApp. An optional phone or WhatsApp field, with a plain line saying you will use it only to reply to this enquiry, can win people who would otherwise leave. Keep it optional so it does not become a new reason to quit.

TTell them what happens nextFear of silence or sales calls stops a lot of forms.

Next to the button, say when you will reply (for example "We reply within one working day", only if you can keep it), what you will do with their details, and that nobody will be added to a mailing list. A short privacy line and the unticked consent box you already have do part of this. Also check the budget question. It helps you sort leads, but it may scare some people off. "Not sure yet" exists already, so keep it and leave the question optional.

SShow a clear confirmationA silent submit makes people send twice or doubt it worked.

After a successful save, show a thank-you message or page that repeats the next step. A separate thank-you page is also the cleanest way to count the conversion later. Make sure enquiry_sent fires only after the save succeeds, and decide what the mailto fallback should count as, so the numbers are not inflated by people who opened an email draft and never sent it.

Page, headline and proof: say who it is for and show it works

A landing page has one audience and one action. Yours needs a clearer promise and some proof.

PromiseProofAction
Result for whomA real project, a quoteOne button to the form

A landing page is a page built for one audience and one action. When a visitor arrives from a search, a post or a listing, they should see the same promise that made them click. Marketers call that message match. Your Home page serves many goals at once, and your single Services page covers all seven services, so a visitor who searched for one thing has to hunt for it.

Your headline today is "We build digital experiences that help businesses grow." It is broad and it fits almost any agency. In your own files, the sharper idea is the second sentence, that technology, brand and marketing support each other. A better headline names a result for a type of client, for example the problem a small shop owner has when their site does not bring enquiries. Use the client you chose in Part 2, and use their words. Ask your first five enquirers what they typed into Google, and borrow it.

Your call to action (CTA) is the button that names the next step. "Start a project" is clear but generic. It does not say what happens next or how long it takes. Try a verb plus outcome, like "Get a project plan" or "Send my enquiry", and keep one main CTA per page. Do not fake urgency or scarcity, because India's 2023 dark pattern guidelines treat that as unfair (check the current text).

ABuild one page for your top serviceStart with the service most enquiries ask about.

Look at which service ticks appear most in your enquiries and in the svc_ events. Give that service its own page: promise, proof, one action. Link posts, listings and your Google Business Profile to it with tagged links. Keep the site menu off this page if you can, so the only way forward is the form.

BAdd proof you can honestly showYou have no client cases yet.

Do not invent any. Honest options are: a short build story of your in-house work (what the problem was, what you built, what it took), screenshots with permission, the technology you use, and how an engagement runs from enquiry to delivery. When the first real client is happy, ask for a short quote and a permission to name them.

CAdd answers to doubtsPrice, time and risk are the three.

You have no prices on the site, only a budget band on the form. Without quoting, you can still explain how you price (by scope, with a written quote), how long a typical project takes if you know, and what the client owns at the end. A FAQ block on the page is enough. Only write what is true for you.

1
Your funnel is already visible in the dashboard: Visitors, then Services or Work, then Contact, then Started form, then Sent enquiry. Each step has its own conversion rate, which is simply the share who moved on from the step before. Say 100 people see the contact page, 30 start the form and 8 send it (these numbers are made up). Then 30 percent start, and about 27 percent of starters finish. Those are two different problems. Low starts point to the promise, the proof or the traffic. Low finishes point to the form itself.
2
Write down these numbers for four whole weeks before you change anything: sessions, contact page views, form_start, enquiry_sent, and enquiries in the inbox. With around 10 sessions a day, a week holds only about 70 visits, so numbers will jump around. One more enquiry changes the rate a lot. Judge by four weeks, not by a day. Then change one thing, wait four more weeks and compare. Write what else changed in that time, such as a new listing or a post that went well, because it can fake a result.
3
A good sign is a rising share of starters who finish, falling time spent on the form before sending, and enquiries that mention the new proof or page in their text. A poor sign is a rise in starts with no rise in sends, which usually means a new friction point appeared. Always check enquiry quality too, using the budget band and project text, because more low-quality enquiries is not a win.

Use the numbers to tell traffic, promise, form, speed or tracking problems apart.

1
When enquiries stay low, the cause is one of a handful of things, and the numbers can tell you which. Work from the top of the funnel down, because a problem at the top makes every number below it look worse than it is. First ask whether the right people are reaching the page at all. Then ask whether those who arrive understand and trust the page. Then ask whether the form is easy. Finally ask whether you are measuring the whole thing correctly. Tap the examples above for the typical patterns.
2
Do not fix everything at once. Choose the biggest drop, which is the step with the lowest share moving on, write a one line guess, and change only that. For example: "Because most visitors view the contact page and few start the form, I believe adding a short build story above the form will raise starts." After four weeks, compare starts and sends against your notes. If it rose and enquiry quality held, keep it and move to the next biggest drop. If nothing changed, undo it if it makes the page worse and note what you learned. If the number is too small to read, wait for more data before deciding.
3
Know when to stop repairing the page. If the funnel numbers after the contact page look healthy and the real problem is that too few people arrive, more page work will not help. At that point the work moves to search, listings and referrals. Those are the channels your plan relies on while there are no ads.
