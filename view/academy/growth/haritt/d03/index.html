Why you pick one channel at a time

A channel is a route to clients. Eight routes are in the plan and none is proven.

Spread thinA little of everything

Eight accounts, no result you can read.

vs
One at a timeOne route, a written stop rule

A clear answer after a few weeks.

1
A channel is a route that brings you customers, such as Google search, LinkedIn, a listing, a friend's introduction or an email. Haritt Developers has many routes prepared: Google Business Profile, Justdial, the enquiry form, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, X, intro emails and plain search. None has paid spend, partnerships or events. Most are drafts, and none has a result on file.
2
It is tempting to start all of them, because each one is cheap. But the real cost is your time, and a studio with a small team has very little of it. The social plan alone asks for three posts a week on LinkedIn, three on Instagram, two on Facebook and a Short on YouTube each week. That is a lot of work with nothing to tell you which part brought a client. If you run eight routes at once and one enquiry arrives, you will not know why.
3
So the discipline is simple: choose the route closest to your buyers, run it properly with tracking and a written stop rule, read the result, then add the next one. Your plan already supports this, since it says no paid ads for the first 90 days (still marked open).
4
Do you need this now? Yes, but only after the ideal client is chosen (lesson d02). Choosing a channel is a thinking job and costs nothing. Starting the posting or outreach itself should wait until you can track where an enquiry came from. If you are not ready, spend the time on the tracking sheet and the source question instead of opening a new account.

Choosing the channel: three candidates, scored

Local search, LinkedIn from the founders' profiles, and referrals.

Candidate 1Local search and listings

Owners already looking. Free. Slow to build.

Candidate 2LinkedIn, founders' profiles

Shows your thinking. Needs steady posting.

Candidate 3Referral partners

Trust comes ready-made. Needs relationships.

Score each channel on four things: how close it is to a buyer who wants a builder, what it costs in money and time, how fast it can show a result, and how well it fits what you can show today. Give each a score from 1 to 5, write your reason in a cell, and add the scores. The numbers are your judgement and not measured data, so do it with your co-founder and argue about the reasons.

My reading of your files is below. The form offers budget bands from Under ₹50,000 to Above ₹5 lakh, so your buyers are small businesses with real money to spend, and they look for a builder or trust a friend. That points to local search, LinkedIn and referrals as the likely best three, and paid search as a later test.

1Local search and listingsFree and permanent, but slow.

People type what they need into Google. A complete profile, a corrected Justdial listing and a few clear pages get you in front of them. It takes weeks to show anything, and you may not get many searches for a studio, but it costs only effort. Lesson d04 covers it step by step.

2LinkedIn from the founders' profilesNeeds a decision on faces and who posts.

Owners and founders are there, and your plan names it as a main channel. Your own build stories (such as how you built HV Verse) are real material. The cost is steady writing. Open decisions about showing faces or only screens must be settled first.

3Referral partnersAccountants, designers, printers and others who know owners.

Your social plan lists the referrer as an audience. A referrer sends you work if they trust you and it helps their own client. Start with a few people you already know, not a mass email. Results can take a while, but each lead arrives warm.

A fourth option, paid search or boosted posts, appears in your files as a possible later test after you approve a budget. Skip it for now: the plan says no ads for 90 days, and without a clear client and tracking you would pay to learn very little.

Setting up a tracked test

Tag every link so the dashboard can tell the channels apart.

Admin analytics: Links tab (link maker)Illustration
Admin > Analytics > Links
1PagePick which page the link opens, such as the developers.haritt.com contact page.
2ChannelLinkedIn post, DM or bio; Email; Friend referral; and so on.
3CampaignA short name for this test, such as the month and the purpose.
4Content tagOptional. Names the exact post or message, so you can compare two.
5Result linkCopy the finished link with its UTM labels. Use only this in the post.
1
A UTM tag is a short label added to the end of a link, such as utm_source=linkedin. When someone clicks, your counter reads the label and files the visit under that channel. Without it, visits from the LinkedIn or WhatsApp apps usually show up as Direct, because those apps hide where the click came from. With it, the dashboard can say how many people came from the post and how many sent an enquiry.
2
Your admin already has a link maker. Go to Analytics, open the Links tab, choose the page and the channel, type a campaign name and an optional content tag, and copy the link it builds (menu names can change). It produces utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content. Use lowercase and the same spelling every time, because LinkedIn and linkedin would be counted as two things.
3
Then read it back. In the developers.haritt.com tab, open the section on where sessions come from and the table of conversions by channel. The Attribution switch changes who gets credit: Last touch credits the final route before the enquiry, and First touch credits the route that first brought the person. For a service where people think for a while, look at both.
4
One gap remains. The enquiry record itself still saves the same fixed label, so the dashboard counts enquiries by channel but your inbox does not show the source. Add the source to your sheet by hand from the dashboard and by asking, and consider asking for a small change to store the UTM on each enquiry (it needs your approval).
5
Before you launch, write three lines: the channel, the one number you will judge it by (enquiries and qualified conversations from that channel), and the stop rule, which is the date or the amount of effort after which you stop or change course if the number is still zero. Writing it before you start stops you from drifting.

Reading the results of the test

Compare channels on the same few numbers, in the same period.

60Dashboard, tagged links
13Form started events
6Enquiry sent events
3Your sheet

Made-up example for one channel, not your data.

1
For each channel, collect four numbers over the same period: visits, forms started, enquiries sent and qualified conversations. The first three come from the dashboard, with the channel shown in the channels table. The last one comes from your sheet, because only you know which enquiries were good. In the made-up example above, 60 visits led to 13 forms started, 6 enquiries and 3 qualified conversations.
2
Do not stop at enquiries. Two channels can give the same number of enquiries and very different quality. A channel that sends 6 enquiries of which 3 are real is better than one that sends 10 of which 1 is real. Also write down the time it took you, because a channel that costs ten hours a week for one conversation is not cheap, even when it is free.
What you seeWhat it usually meansWhat to do
Visits high, enquiries zeroWrong people or weak pageCheck the audience and the page, see the next part
Few visits, good enquiriesRight people, too fewDo more of it, same message
Many enquiries, few qualifiedMessage attracts the wrong peopleSay who the work is not for
Nothing at allNot enough time passed, or tracking brokenTest your own link first

Be careful with small numbers. A few visits a day means one lucky post can look like a trend. Judge by weeks of data and by whether the pattern repeats, not by a single day.

A channel that gives nothing is not a failure if you can say why. The trap is guessing that the channel is bad when the tracking is broken. Work in this order, from the cheapest check to the hardest.

1Is tracking telling the truth?Click your own tagged link and watch the dashboard.

Open the link from a phone, wait a minute and check the dashboard. If it did not appear, the link, the tag or the counter is at fault, and every conclusion so far is unreliable.

2Is it the audience?Look at who actually visited and who enquired.

Check the company and current website in the enquiries. If the people are students, hobbyists or very large firms, you are reaching the wrong crowd. Change who the post speaks to.

3Is it the message or the page?Compare the promise with the first line of the page.

If people arrive and leave, the page does not match what the post promised. If they stay but do not start the form, they need more trust: shown work, a clear first step and an answer to "what will it cost?".

4Is it the amount of effort?Count posts or outreach actually done.

Three posts in a month is not a test. Compare what you planned with what you did before judging the channel.

After you find the suspect, change only that thing, run the same plan for the same time, and compare with the earlier period. Write the belief, the change and the result in a log. If the number improves, keep the change and consider doing more. If a channel meets its stop rule with no improvement after fixes, stop it, write why, and move to the second candidate.

Loops: growth that feeds itself

Two loops your studio can build, and when to start them.

Deliver a projectDo it well
Ask approvalClient agrees to a case study
Publish proofPage, LinkedIn, profile
Attract ownersSearch and posts bring visits
New enquiryLeads to the next project
1
A loop is when the result of one customer feeds the next, so growth does not depend only on your own effort. No loop is running at Haritt Developers yet. Two are possible, and both depend on a finished client project.
2
The proof loop is shown above. A delivered project becomes a case study, which gives your listing, your posts and your site something real to show, which brings new visits and enquiries, which become the next project. The site already promises case studies "only with the client's approval", so ask for that approval before the project ends, not after. Until a client project is finished, use an honest write-up of your own builds, including HV Verse, as the first piece of proof.
3
The referral loop works through people. Near the end of each project, ask the client and each partner for one introduction, and write it down in the sheet with the source. Loops are slow at the start and fast later, so do not judge them in the first weeks. Judge them by whether each finished project produces one proof piece and one introduction request.
4
Order of work: pick one channel from the three candidates, run it with tags and a stop rule, build the proof loop as soon as the first project is delivered, and add the referral loop beside it. Come back to this choice every time a channel meets its stop rule, and before you consider any paid test.
