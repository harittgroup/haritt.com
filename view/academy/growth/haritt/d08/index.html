What search ads are and whether you need them

You pay only when someone who is already searching clicks, then judge it by enquiries.

Normal search resultFree, slow, not guaranteed

Your brand name already ranks first and second.

vs
Search adPaid per click, fast, capped

Shows for service words you choose.

1
When someone types "website developer" and a city into Google, the first few results marked Sponsored are search ads, also called SEM (search engine marketing). You write a short ad, choose the words that should trigger it, and pay Google a small amount each time a person clicks, which is why it is also called pay-per-click. The idea suits a studio better than most paid channels, because the person is already looking for what you sell. You are not interrupting anyone, you are appearing at the moment of need.
2
Your files say there is no Google Ads account, no tag and no conversion anywhere. The plan says no paid ads for the first 90 days, and whether to run any is still an open question. Brand-name searches already put developers.haritt.com first and second (14 Oct), so ads on your own name are not needed. The only sensible test is service words plus a city, run only after you approve a budget.
3
Two related ideas, quickly. Paid social means ads on Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn, shown to people by interest rather than by search. Your files point out that a business owner rarely clicks an ad for a custom project, so those would cost a lot per lead, and you have no pixel (a small tracking code that lets ad platforms recognise your visitors) or audience yet. A boosted post, a normal post you pay to show more widely, is a smaller test of a finished project. This lesson goes deep on search ads, because they are the best first paid test for you.
Do you need this now? No. Your own plan says wait 90 days, the form, proof and tracking are not ready, and the enquiry count is not even on file. This lesson is the playbook for when you are ready: what must be true first, how to set up a capped test, how to read it, and how to stop if it fails. Until then, spend nothing and fix the groundwork in the proof, referral and email lessons.

What must be ready first

Seven checks. Ads multiply what exists, good or bad.

GA4 key eventenquiry_sent not confirmed
Lead sourceFixed src, no field
Ideal clientNot chosen yet
Proof on pageNo client proof
Value of a leadEnquiry count not on file
Privacy and consentDevelopers setting unchecked
Budget and stop ruleNot decided

Opening an ad account is the easy part and the wrong first step. Ads bring more people to whatever you already have. If your page, proof and follow-up work, ads make them bigger. If not, ads make the problem bigger and more expensive. Check the ground first. For each item you get why it matters, how to check it, and where you stand.

Aenquiry_sent reaches GA4 as a key eventHow Google learns what counts as a win.

A key event (older name: conversion) is an action in GA4 you mark as important. Your files say the code sends enquiry_sent to the site's own counter, and GA4 events for this site cannot be checked from files. The GA4 property G-ERL2FMV2TG was added on 20 Oct, but its tag and key events are unconfirmed. Without this, Google Ads cannot learn which clicks became enquiries.

How to check: submit a test enquiry and watch GA4 Realtime. Part 3 shows the clicks.

BKnow where each lead came fromOtherwise you cannot judge any channel.

The enquiry saves a fixed src and no UTM, so no lead can be tied to a channel. For ads you need at least a way to see that an enquiry came from a Google Ads click, either in GA4 or through a plain "How did you hear about us?" field.

How to check: open a saved enquiry in the admin inbox and look for any source information.

CAn ideal client and a clear promiseKeywords and ad text depend on it.

Your files say the audience is not chosen. Search ads need you to know who you want: a clinic, a shop, a school? Without it your ads attract anyone who types a similar phrase.

How to check: finish the sentence "We build websites for ___ in ___." If it takes long, you are not ready.

DProof on the page they land onA paid visitor is a stranger.

There are no client case studies, testimonials or logos. A stranger arriving from an ad compares you with other results above and below. Proof near the form lifts every number later.

How to check: open a service page on a phone and ask whether you would trust it with money.

EThe value of one good leadSo you can set a cost cap.

Project values and the number of enquiries per month are not on file. Work backwards: how much is a signed project worth to you, how many calls become projects, and so what is the most you would pay for one qualified conversation? Write the number down.

How to check: count enquiries from the admin inbox for a few months and note how many became calls.

FPrivacy and consent are consistentAds touch cookies and ad settings.

On haritt.com your GA4 settings deny ad storage by design, and the Developers setting cannot be checked. There is no cookie banner. Google Ads works best with ad cookies and Google signals, which you have turned off for privacy. My reading: decide how you will measure ads without them, for example by relying on your own counter and the enquiry source field, and update the privacy notice to match before spending.

How to check: read the privacy page and the tag settings, and write what ad tracking you will and will not use.

GBudget and a written stop ruleDecide while calm, not mid-test.

Pick the most you can lose without a sale, a start date, a length, and the conditions for stopping. Writing it down protects you from adding money because the first days looked promising or bad.

How to check: can you state the cap and the stop rule in two sentences?

Make enquiry_sent a GA4 key event

The first hands-on job. It costs nothing and is useful even if you never run ads.

GA4: AdminIllustration
Admin, then Data display, then Events
1PropertyPick the Haritt Developers property (G-ERL2FMV2TG).
2Events listenquiry_sent appears here only after it has fired once.
3Mark as key eventToggle on beside enquiry_sent (or use the Key events page).
4Realtime reportReports, then Realtime: confirms the test enquiry arrives.
5DebugViewAdmin, then Data display, then DebugView: shows each event as it fires.

GA4 is Google Analytics 4, the free tool that records visits and actions on a site. Your site also has its own counter, and the two run side by side without being reconciled. For ads, GA4 matters because Google Ads can import its key events. Menu names change, so if a label differs, use the search bar at the top of GA4.

1Check that the tag is on the pageWithout it nothing else works.

Your files cannot confirm the GA4 tag on the live Developers site. Open the site, then open GA4 and go to Reports, then Realtime. Visit the site in another tab. If your visit appears, the tag works. If not, the tag is missing or blocked, and that is a site change to make before anything else.

2Make the event reach GA4A one-line site change, with your approval.

The code sends enquiry_sent to the site's own counter. For GA4 to receive it, the same moment must also send an event named enquiry_sent through the GA4 tag. That is a small change in the site code made by whoever maintains it. Do it only after your go, and keep the same spelling.

3Test with a real submissionSubmit the form yourself.

With Realtime open, fill the form with a clearly marked test name and send it. After a few seconds enquiry_sent should appear under event count. DebugView, if you turn on debug mode, shows each step in more detail. Delete the test entry from the admin inbox afterwards.

4Mark it as a key eventOne switch.

Go to Admin, then Data display, then Events. Find enquiry_sent in the list (it appears after it has fired at least once, sometimes after a day) and turn on the key event toggle. From then on it counts as a win in GA4 reports.

5Link GA4 to Google Ads laterOnly once you have an ads account.

In GA4 go to Admin, then Product links, then Google Ads links, and choose the account. In Google Ads you then import the key event as a conversion (Goals, then Conversions, then New conversion action, then Import). Consider the consent point from Part 2 before linking.

Part 4 of 7

Planning the test on half a page

Seven decisions. A cap and a stop rule come first.

1 Capmost you can lose
2 Goalqualified enquiries
3 Wordsservice plus place
4 Ad and pageone promise

Before opening the ad tool, write the plan. It is short, and it saves most of the money. Every number you need for the cap comes from your own business, so none is invented here: where a figure is not on file, the lesson says so.

CCap and goalSpend less than you can afford to lose.

Decide the total for the whole test, not only per day. A small total that you will not miss is enough to learn something. Then write the goal as one result with a quality bar: for example "3 qualified enquiries with a budget band chosen". Not clicks, not impressions.

SSearch words (keywords)Choose words a buyer types, with a place.

A keyword is the word or phrase you want to show for. Service-area businesses win on specific local phrases such as a service plus a city (for example "website developer" with your city). Your service areas are not on file, so pick the places you can truly serve. Match types control how loosely Google reads your word: exact (in square brackets) shows for that meaning, phrase (in quotation marks) allows extra words around it, and broad lets Google guess widely, which wastes money in a small test. Start with exact and phrase.

NNegative keywordsWords you never want to pay for.

A negative keyword tells Google not to show your ad when a search contains it. Common ones for a studio are free, jobs, course, tutorial, template and salary. Add these on day one, then add more from the search terms report as you learn what people actually typed.

TThe ad and the pageOne promise from the search to the form.

A responsive search ad has up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, and Google mixes them. Use real facts: the service, the place, and what happens next, such as a reply within one working day if you can keep that promise. Send each ad group to the matching service page, not the home page, because your site has a page and an enquiry button for each of the 7 services. The page needs the proof from Part 2.

Last, predict. Write "I expect about X clicks to cost about ₹Y and about Z enquiries" before you start. Your prediction teaches you quickly where your picture of the market was wrong.

Part 5 of 7

Building the campaign, click by click

Capped, search-only, one place, one goal. Screens and names change, so follow the meaning.

Google Ads: new campaignIllustration
Campaigns, then New campaign, then Leads, then Search
1Goal and typeChoose Leads, then the Search campaign type.
2Conversion goalSelect the imported enquiry_sent conversion only.
3BiddingPick Clicks, and set a maximum cost per click limit.
4NetworksUntick Search partners and the Display Network.
5LocationsOnly your service areas, presence setting.
6BudgetSet the average daily budget from your cap.

Google Ads is the free-to-open, pay-as-you-go tool at ads.google.com. Opening an account costs nothing, and you pay only for clicks, after adding a payment method. If the tool offers a simplified mode, look for the link to switch to Expert Mode, since the simple mode hides the settings below. Do this only after your go and your cap are written.

1Start the campaignCampaigns, then New campaign.

Click Campaigns in the left menu, then the plus button, then New campaign. When asked for an objective, choose Leads, then choose the Search campaign type. Select Website visits as the result you want and enter the site address. Give the campaign a name that includes the date and the service.

2Choose the goal it learns fromOnly the real win.

On the conversion goals step, keep only your enquiry_sent conversion. Remove page views and clicks if they are listed, because Google tries to maximise whatever it is told to count.

3Set bidding with a capClicks with a maximum price.

Bidding means how Google decides what to pay. With no conversion history yet, choose Clicks as the focus and tick the option to set a maximum cost per click bid limit. Pick a limit you can afford based on your lead value. After you have a few enquiries, you may switch to a conversion-based option, but not before.

4Turn off what you do not wantNetworks, places and language.

Untick the Search Partners network and the Display Network, so ads appear only on Google Search. Under locations, add only your service areas and choose the option for people in or regularly in those places, not people merely interested in them. Set the language to match your pages.

5Set the budgetDaily budget from your cap.

Enter an average daily budget equal to your total cap divided by the days of the test. Google can spend up to roughly twice that on a single day but keeps the monthly spend within about thirty times the daily figure, so the total cap still has to be watched by you, not by the tool.

6Add keywords and the adOne ad group per service.

Name an ad group after one service. Add 5 to 10 keywords in exact and phrase form, add your negatives, then write the responsive search ad with the final URL set to that service page. Under the ad, check the preview on mobile.

7Publish and check statusThen look for Eligible.

Click Publish campaign. In the campaign list the status should read Eligible, or Under review at first. If it says Disapproved, open the reason. Check also that Auto-tagging is on, found under Admin, then Account settings, so that clicks are tagged for analytics.

1
For the first week, only check, do not edit. Each day confirm the ads are running and not disapproved, the spend is within your cap, and new enquiries from the ad appear in your own analytics. Day one is noisy, and every change restarts part of Google's learning. Stop at once only for something broken, such as a link to an error page.
2
Now the metrics. In Google Ads, the Campaigns table shows columns you can edit with the columns button. In GA4, open Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition and look for sessions with source/medium google / cpc. Read the table in the order the buyer lived it.

Made-up example, only to show the method: you spend ₹1,500. The ad is shown 2,000 times and gets 60 clicks, a 3 percent click rate and ₹25 per click. GA4 records 50 visits, 10 form starts and 3 enquiries, so each enquiry cost ₹500. You read all three and judge one is a fit, so a qualified lead cost ₹1,500. Whether that is acceptable depends on the lead value you set in Part 2. Also open the search terms report (in the left menu under keywords, then Search terms) and read the real phrases. Add irrelevant ones as negative keywords, and note the good ones for new exact keywords.

When it fails, and how to fix and decide

Check tracking first, then words, ad, page, form, and the market.

What do your numbers show?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyTracking or link problem

Check auto-tagging, final URL and the GA4 tag.

Most likelyWords or bids

Keywords too narrow, bid limit too low, or a paused ad.

Most likelyAd or words mismatch

Read the search terms. Rewrite the headlines.

Most likelyMessage mismatch

Does the page open with what the ad promised?

Most likelyForm

Test on a phone. Are budget and timeline fields in the way?

Most likelyWrong searchers

Search terms, negatives, budget band chosen.

Most likelyMarket or offer

Compare with your cap. Test, or stop.

Many tests end up weaker than hoped, and that is a result. The mistake is to blame the page or the ad by guesswork. Work through these, cheapest check first, and stop when you find a clear culprit.

1Is the tracking telling the truth?Compare clicks with visits.

Google Ads clicks and GA4 visits will not match exactly, but a gap of half or more points to a problem. Check that auto-tagging is on, the tag is on the page, and the link works on a phone. Submit a test enquiry and confirm it is counted. If tracking is broken, treat the week's conclusions as unreliable.

2Are the words and the people right?Search terms and impressions.

Open the search terms report. If many searches are for jobs, courses, free tools or the wrong place, add negatives and tighten match types. If impressions are tiny, the words are too narrow or the bid limit is too low. Pattern: low click rate across every ad points to the words, not the ad.

3Is it the ad?Compare ads on the same words.

If different ads for the same search get very different click rates, the weaker headline is the problem. Rewrite using the buyer's own phrases, and put the place and the service in the first headline.

4Is it the page or the form?Read GA4 engagement, form starts and enquiries.

Short visits right after the click mean mismatch. Visits with form starts but few sends mean the form is too heavy. Your form asks for company, website, services, project text, budget, timeline and consent. Test removing a field only with your approval.

5Is it the offer or the market?The last suspect.

If tracking is sound, searchers are right, the ad gets clicks and the page is clear, but enquiries remain too costly, the market for those words may simply be expensive or crowded. That is an answer, not a failure.

To retest, change only one thing, write your guess with a number first, run the same budget and length, and compare with the earlier round. Keep a log of date, belief, change, result and decision. If the cost per qualified lead stays within your cap, raise the budget slowly, about a fifth every few days, and step back if cost rises. If after two or three honest rounds it is still far above your cap, pause the campaign. Stopping on purpose saved you from a larger loss, and you can return when proof and referrals make your page stronger. Revisit when the 90 days pass.
