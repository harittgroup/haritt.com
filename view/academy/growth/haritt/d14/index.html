Outbound and proof: why you reach out, and what you show

Outbound means writing first. A case study is what makes the message believable.

InboundThey find you

vs
OutboundYou write first

A short message to a person you chose.

1
Outbound means you contact people first, instead of waiting for them to find you. For Haritt Developers this is a natural fit, because your buyers are few, the decision is slow, and a small business owner often wants to know who is behind the site before sending a form. Your site gets about 300 visits a month (301 in the 30 days to 13 October, real visitors only), so waiting for visitors alone will bring only a few enquiries. Writing to a short list of the right people can add leads that the site would never have caught.
2
Outbound is prepared but not started. Intro-email designs for Developers exist, with a business-problem framing, a plain "Hello," greeting and five to six versions, and none is recorded as sent. Plans for X posts exist, and whether they went out is not on file. The social plan names referrers (accountants, designers, printers) as an audience, but nobody has been approached. You also have a firm rule: no email goes out without your go.
3
A case study is a short written story of a project you did for a client: the problem, what you built, and what changed. It is the thing outbound needs most, because a stranger will not reply to a message that has nothing behind it. Today you have no client case studies, testimonials or logos. The only proof you own is the HV Verse build.
Do you need this now? Partly. Start the proof work now, because it is free and it is your biggest gap. Start outbound sending only after you have chosen one type of ideal client and have at least one honest story to point to. Before that, a message has nothing to say. Meanwhile, you can prepare the list and the draft without sending anything.

Sending too early wastes your best contacts, because you cannot send a first message twice. Go through the list below. For each item you will see how to check it and where you stand.

AOne kind of client in mindWithout it, the message is vague.

Your site lists seven broad services and no ideal client. A message that says "we build websites, marketing, automation and more" gets ignored. How to check: finish this sentence in one line: "We help [type of business] with [one problem]." If you cannot, you are not ready. Not on file: which type of business you want most, so this is your decision. Pick the nearest one to your past work or your area.

BAt least one honest storyProof makes a stranger take you seriously.

The HV Verse build is the only real proof you have, and it is an in-house project. That is still a fair story if you tell it honestly: the problem, the approach, what exists now. Do not call it a client project. How to check: could you show a visitor one page that tells this story in under two minutes? If not, write that page first.

CPermission and rulesCold messages have legal and platform rules.

Cold email and WhatsApp messages need consent or a clear legal basis. India has messaging rules (TRAI and DLT) and a data-protection law (DPDP). Check the current rules on the official sources before you send to people who do not know you. Your own email tool has no unsubscribe link yet, and the footer only says to reply STOP, so for now keep outbound to individual, personal messages. Anyone who says no goes on your Do not email list.

DA place to log every replySo you know what happened.

Use the pipeline sheet from the earlier lesson. Give outbound leads their own source name, for example outreach, so you can compare them with Justdial, referrals and the site. Without this, you will not know if outbound worked.

Choose who to write to, and what to say

Start with ten people you can name, and one reason for each.

AccountantsDesignersPrintersSpecific idea

The best outreach for a new studio is not cold. It starts with people who already know you or your work, then moves to partners, and only last to strangers. This order costs nothing, and the replies are warmer.

SStep one: the first tenName ten people, with one reason to contact each.

Open a blank tab in your sheet and list ten people or businesses. Next to each, write the reason you are writing to them, such as "runs a shop with an old site" or "refers clients who need websites". If you cannot write a reason, take them off the list. Not on file: any list of contacts. Build it from your own memory and your own network.

SStep two: write the messageShort, about their problem, one easy question.

Your intro designs already follow the rules you set: the recipient's first name, a question about a pain they might have in the subject, very short text, and a sign-off from the team rather than a person. Keep to them. A good message is four lines: why you are writing to them, one specific thing you noticed, one thing you can do about it, and a small ask such as "Would a short call this week help?" Do not attach a long proposal. Do not list all seven services.

SStep three: the partner messageOffer something useful to a referrer.

Accountants, designers and printers meet small businesses that need websites. A partner message should say what you do in one sentence, what kind of client you suit, and what you will do for their client. Keep any reward free at first, such as introducing clients back to them. A paid referral fee needs your approval.

Send with your go. Show each message to your co-founder, confirm it, and send in small batches by hand. Gmail allows only about 100 recipients a day, and one-by-one sending protects your name. Follow up once after a few days and then stop.

Two jobs sit here. The first is the case study. The second is making sure you can tell which message brought a visit.

TThe case studyProblem, work, result, with approval.

Write the HV Verse story first, because it is yours to publish. Use four short parts. The problem: what you needed and why. What you built: the pages, the apps and the choices you made. What changed: only results you can show, such as what is live and working. Not on file: usage numbers, so say none rather than inventing one. For a client project, the site says case studies are added only with the client's approval, so ask in writing before you publish a name, a screenshot or a result. Ask for a short quote at the same time.

WWhere it goesAbove the form, and in your messages.

Put a short version on the Work page and link to it from your messages. Proof placed near the form helps a doubtful visitor decide. Put the same short version into your Justdial and Google profile text, after you fix the founding-year line, because the About page says founded in 2026, your story says 2020 and Justdial says 2018.

TTagging the linkHow a link tells you what worked.

A UTM tag is a label added to the end of a web address, like utm_source=linkedin, that your analytics reads when someone clicks. It tells you which channel and which message brought the visit. Your admin has a link maker on the Links tab: pick the page, the channel and a campaign name, and optionally a content tag, then copy the result. Without it, visits from WhatsApp and Instagram often show up as Direct, because those apps hide where the visitor came from. Menu names can change, so look for the tab with the link maker if the path differs.

Outbound is a numbers game only in a loose sense. You will send ten, then twenty, not thousands. So read each message as a small experiment. After every batch, count how many were sent, how many replied, how many agreed to a call and how many reached a proposal. Then divide each by the one before it. With ten messages, one extra reply moves the rate by ten points, so do not decide from a single batch. Look at two or three batches before judging the list or the wording. Read the actual replies, since a polite no with a reason is worth more than a count.

Work out whether it is the list, the message, the ask or the proof.

A quiet inbox is not a verdict. It is a question about where the road breaks. Check in this order, which runs from the cheapest check to the hardest.

1The listRight people with a real problem?

Look at the reason you wrote for each person. If many reasons are weak, the list is the problem. Try ten new people with a clear need, such as a business whose site is old or does not work on a phone, before you blame the message.

2The messageShort, specific, one easy ask?

Read it aloud. If it takes more than twenty seconds, cut it. Change only the first two lines and send to the next batch, so you can compare. Change one thing per batch, otherwise you cannot tell which change helped.

3The proofDo they see why to trust you?

If people open your link but do not enquire, check what they see. With no client logos or testimonials, a stranger has only your word. A short, honest HV Verse story and a visible privacy notice help.

Retest and decide. Write down the change, send to a fresh batch, compare reply rates, and keep the winner. If three batches in a row bring nothing, pause outbound and put the time into proof and referrals. Stopping is a result, not a failure.

That single act gives you a case study that is worth more than anything you can say about yourself. Then repeat the outreach with the new story and compare the reply rate with the first round.
3
Keep one rule through all of it: say only what is true and what you can prove. The founding-year lines must agree everywhere, no result may be invented, and nobody is named or shown without their written approval. Trust is slow to build for a small studio and quick to lose, so every claim in a message should be one you would be happy to show a client on a call.
