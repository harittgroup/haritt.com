What a growth audit is, and what it shows about Haritt Developers

An audit is an honest scorecard. A weekly rhythm turns it into steady progress.

Weekly rhythmWhere we are going

1
A growth audit is a structured look at every part of how you win clients, rated honestly. It is not a report to impress anyone. It tells you where the weak points are, so that you work on the one that matters most instead of the one that feels most fun. A weekly rhythm is the habit that follows: a short, fixed time each week to look at a few numbers, decide one thing and write down what you learned.
2
Your overall reading, from the files: Haritt Developers has the strongest measurement of your three brands, a working enquiry form and inbox, and a strong brand-name search position on 14 October. It has no ideal client, no client proof, no source on the lead, no pipeline after the enquiry, and no growth numbers beyond the 13 October screenshot. The real constraint is not traffic. It is that nothing connects a visit, a lead and a won project.
3
The team is two founders with no fixed roles. The tools exist: your admin, a database, email scripts, GA4 (Google's analytics tool) and the site host. What does not exist is the habit. There is no weekly growth meeting and no owner for enquiries on file.
Do you need this now? Yes, and it is light. Do the audit once now, which takes an hour or two, and set up the weekly slot this week. Both are free. Do not wait for more traffic or more clients. The audit tells you what to fix first, and the weekly slot makes sure it gets done.

How to run the audit

One pass, twelve questions, one honest rating each.

Open one sheetOpen the dashboardOpen the inbox
Read the areaFind the evidenceBuilding or Gap
Pick top 3Assign an ownerWrite the next action

Make a sheet with the twelve areas in rows and these columns: rating, evidence, what is missing, next action, owner. Go down the rows. For each area, find evidence you can point to, not memory. If you cannot find evidence, the rating is Gap, however busy you feel.

1Rate on evidenceA page, a number, a record.

For measurement, evidence is the dashboard and an event you can see. For pipeline, it is the sheet. For proof, it is a published case study. Write the place you looked next to each rating. When you cannot verify something, such as whether GA4 receives the enquiry event, write Not checked rather than guessing.

2Answer the open decisionsSix questions only you can answer.

Your files list them: choose one ideal client or keep seven services open; save a how-did-you-hear answer and the source with each enquiry; choose the founding-year line; add pipeline stages and project value to the inbox; show a from price or budget hint; ask the next client for approval to publish a case study. Each is a yes, no or not yet. Write your answer and date it.

3Pick the top threeNot twelve.

Choose the three actions with the biggest effect for the least effort. A quick way is to score each idea from 1 to 10 on how much it helps, how sure you are, and how easy it is, then add them. The scores are your judgement, not data. Your files rank saving the source with each enquiry, fixing the year line and verifying the Google profile near the top.

1
An example of one row. For Search and local, you might write: rating Building; evidence, the brand name ranked first and second on 14 October and Justdial third; missing, the Google profile status and any Search Console data; next action, verify the profile; owner, whoever handles listings. Notice that the row names a source for each claim, a gap and a single action. Do the same for every row, and the sheet becomes your plan.
2
Re-run it. Rate again after a few finished fixes, not every week. If you re-rate too often you will be measuring your mood, not your progress.

The weekly rhythm

One slot, five numbers, one decision, one note.

Lookfive numbers
Askwhat moved and why
Decideone change
Doduring the week
Writeone line in the log

The rhythm is small on purpose. A team of two will keep up a short habit and drop a long one. Pick a fixed slot each week, the same time, about thirty minutes. Make one founder the owner of enquiries and replies, and one the owner of the review or content. If you share them, name who decides when you disagree.

TThe five numbersChosen to follow the whole road.

Use sessions (visits), contact page views, enquiries sent, qualified leads and proposals sent. The first three come from the admin dashboard, with the range set to 7 days and the real-visitors view on. The last two come from your pipeline sheet. A sixth number, projects won, is added when wins happen. Keep the same five every week so you can compare.

TThe learning logWhat you tried, what happened.

Open a tab with four columns: date, what we believed, what we changed, what happened. Write one line each week. After three months this is the most valuable page you own, because it records what works for your own business and stops you repeating a failed idea.

TThe one-page how-toSo any task can be handed over.

For each regular task, such as replying to an enquiry or updating the sheet, write the steps on one page. Then either founder can do it, and a future helper can start quickly. Not on file today: any such page.

Running the first review. Open the sheet, read the five numbers aloud, and say in one sentence what moved. Then ask one question: what is the single thing that would most improve the weakest step before the next review? Write the answer as a task with a name and a day. Close the sheet. If the meeting runs past the slot, you are discussing too many things, so cut the list. A short meeting held every week beats a long one held when you remember.

Read from the top. The tiles tell you the size of the audience. The funnel tells you where visitors drop: if many reach Services but few reach the Contact page, the problem is the path or the message. If many reach Contact but few start the form, look at the form's first screen. If many start but few send, the form may be too long or confusing. The 13 October screenshot showed 301 sessions, 633 page views and about 70 percent engaged in 30 days, but how many enquiries it brought is not on file.
3
Two cautions. The visitors tile adds daily devices, so the same person on two days counts twice. And the visitor retention panel shows zeros that look like a bug, which is not useful for a lead site. Judge the site by enquiries and by what happens after them, which only your pipeline sheet can show.

A flat week is not a failure, and a single odd number is not a trend. At your traffic, a few visits more or less change every percentage. So use the pattern across several weeks, and prefer the biggest drop between two steps over small shifts.

1Is the count right?Check tracking before judging.

Send a test enquiry and confirm it shows as a sent enquiry in the dashboard and appears in the inbox. Check whether GA4 receives the same event. Two counters that disagree mean at least one of them needs fixing.

2Which step loses most?Find the biggest gap.

Use the funnel and the pipeline together, from visit to proposal. The step with the largest fall, after allowing for small numbers, is your next test.

3Change one thingThen wait for enough data.

Write your guess in the log, make a single change, and wait for a sensible number of visits or leads before judging. With about ten sessions a day, a small change can take weeks to show, so judge it on the next 10 to 15 leads rather than a few days. If nothing moves, record that and try the next cause.

Scale, repeat or stop. When a fix clearly helps, keep it and move to the next weakest step. When it does not, undo it and note why. When a whole channel produces no qualified lead after honest tries, stop it for now and give its hours to one that does. Stopping is a decision you write in the log, not a silent drift.

Next steps and when to revisit

Do the audit once, set the slot, then repeat on a calm cycle.

NowFix the foundation

Source on leads, year line, GA4 check, ideal client.

NextBuild proof and pipeline

Google profile, first case study, pipeline stages, reply template.

LaterGrow what works

Referrals, question pages, a from price, a capped paid test if you approve.

1
Your files already sort the work into three groups. The first is the foundation: capture the source of every enquiry with a self-reported answer, use the same founding-year line everywhere, check that GA4 receives the enquiry event, and choose or confirm the ideal client. The second is proof and pipeline: verify the Google profile and publish the prepared posts, write the first case study with approval, add pipeline stages and project value, and add a first-reply template. The third is growth from what works: referrals with tagged links, question pages for searches that do not use your name, a package or from price, and a capped paid test only if you approve one.
2
Do them in that order. Keep the audit sheet open, mark each item as it is finished, and re-rate the twelve areas after a few are done. Revisit the plan when something real changes: your first client, a published case study, a month of source data, or a decision about money. Until then, the weekly slot and the learning log are the whole system.
