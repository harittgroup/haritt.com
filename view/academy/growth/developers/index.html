Growth vs brand marketing

Binet & Field, The Long and the Short of It (2013); Sharp, How Brands Grow (2010)

DefinitionBrand marketing builds memory and reputation so that people think of you first when a need appears. Its payoff is slow and spread across many buyers. Performance marketing buys or earns a defined action (a click, a lead, a sale) and pays or optimises against that action, so every result can be traced to a cost. Growth marketing goes wider: it treats the whole customer path, from first touch to repeat purchase and referral, as a system to improve through experiments, working together with product and sales. Binet and Field analysed the IPA Effectiveness Databank and found that campaigns work best when long-term brand building and short-term activation run together. Sharp argues that growth comes mostly from reaching many buyers (mental and physical availability), not from deepening loyalty of a few.

Why it mattersTeams that only run performance campaigns hit a ceiling: they harvest existing demand and costs rise. Teams that only do brand work cannot prove what worked. Knowing which job a piece of work does decides how you judge it and how long you wait for results.

In practiceLabel each activity as demand creation (brand, content, community, PR) or demand capture (search ads, retargeting, email to existing leads, local listings). Judge capture work on cost per result. Judge creation work on reach, branded search growth and direct or referral traffic over months. For a young business with a small budget, capture work and owned channels come first, because they pay back sooner and can be measured from day one. See for how the two map onto the customer path.

The marketing funnel and the customer journey

Lewis (1898), AIDA; Kotler, Marketing 4.0 (2016)

DefinitionThe marketing funnel models how a large group of people narrows down to a few customers. The oldest form is AIDA, attributed to E. St. Elmo Lewis (1898): Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Modern versions use awareness, consideration, conversion and often add retention and advocacy. Kotler's 5A path (Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate) makes the later stages explicit. Teams also say TOFU, MOFU and BOFU for top, middle and bottom of the funnel. The customer journey is the real, messier version: the touchpoints a person passes through, in any order, across channels. Google's "messy middle" research (2020) describes the middle as a loop of exploring and evaluating, not a straight line.

Why it mattersEach stage needs a different message and a different metric. Asking for a purchase at the awareness stage wastes money. Talking only about the brand at the decision stage loses sales.

In practiceDraw a one-page journey map: stages across the top; below each, what the customer does, thinks, asks and where they meet you (search, social, referral, site, WhatsApp, call). Mark pain points and drop-offs. Then assign one metric and one owner per stage. Pull the numbers from GA4 and your lead sheet. The weakest stage, not the biggest, is usually the first thing to fix. See for a startup version of the same idea.

Pirate metrics (AARRR)

McClure, Startup Metrics for Pirates (2007)

DefinitionAARRR, called pirate metrics because of how it sounds, is a five-stage framework by Dave McClure (2007) for measuring a startup: Acquisition (people arrive), Activation (they have a first good experience), Retention (they come back), Referral (they tell others) and Revenue (they pay). Some teams reorder to put Revenue before Referral. Each stage gets one conversion rate and one clear event definition.

Why it matters"Traffic is low" is rarely the real problem. The framework shows where the path actually leaks, so effort goes to the stage that limits growth. Fixing activation before buying traffic is a common outcome.

In practiceDefine each stage as a trackable event, then compute stage-to-stage rates. For a product, activation might be "first plan created"; for a service business, acquisition is an enquiry and activation is a qualified call. Put the five numbers on one sheet or a GA4 funnel exploration (see ). Pick the lowest stage, run experiments on it (), then re-measure. Remember the model is linear; real growth also comes from loops ().

At Haritt Developers

For a lead business the five stages read: acquisition (visit), activation (a first real conversation), revenue (a signed project), retention (repeat or support work), referral (a client sends another). Only the first is measured today, through visits and the enquiry_sent event Verified.

Activation, revenue and referral have no event and no field in the enquiry record Gap. Retention has a service to hang on, "Consulting & Support" Verified, but no client records.

North Star metric and input metrics

Ellis; Amplitude, North Star Playbook; Croll & Yoskovitz, Lean Analytics (2013)

DefinitionThe North Star metric is the one number that best captures the core value customers get from you and that predicts long-term revenue. It is not revenue itself. Input metrics are the few levers a team can move that drive it; Amplitude groups them as breadth, depth, frequency and efficiency. Lean Analytics calls the single focus metric of a stage the One Metric That Matters (OMTM) and separates actionable metrics (they change what you do) from vanity metrics (they only look good, such as total sign-ups or page views).

Why it mattersWithout one shared metric, teams chase different numbers and optimise against each other. A good North Star keeps product, marketing and sales pointed at the same outcome: customers getting value.

In practiceTest a candidate with four questions. Does it show customers getting value? Does it lead revenue rather than lag? Can a team influence it weekly? Can anyone understand it? Then break it into inputs and give each input an owner. Review the tree weekly (). Replace the metric when the business model changes. Keep revenue and cost as guardrails so you do not grow the wrong way.

Product-market fit and message-market fit

Andreessen, The Pmarca Guide to Startups (2007); Ellis, Sean Ellis test

DefinitionMarc Andreessen (2007) defined product-market fit (PMF) as being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market. The Sean Ellis test measures it with one survey question: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" with the options very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, not disappointed. Ellis proposed that if at least 40 percent answer "very disappointed", the product has probably reached fit. This is a heuristic, not a law. Message-market fit (MMF) is the earlier question: does your message resonate with a specific group enough that they respond? You can test it before the product is complete.

Why it mattersScaling paid acquisition before fit just buys leakage. Fit decides whether growth work compounds or evaporates. Without message-market fit, even a good product gets ignored.

In practiceCheck three signals together. Survey: run the Ellis question on people who used the product at least a few times, and ask what they would use instead and what the main benefit is. Behaviour: the retention curve flattens instead of falling to zero (). Pull: people come through referrals or search without being pushed. For message fit, run a small landing page or ad test with two or three promises and see which gets replies (). For a services business, fit looks like repeat clients, referrals and a stable win rate.

No client testimonial, case study or repeat project exists on the site or in the files Gap, and the only work shown is in-house (HV Verse suite, haritt.com, the Developers site) Verified. Sean Ellis style surveys need users, and a studio has few clients, so use interviews and outcomes instead.

Growth loops vs funnels

Balfour; Reforge, growth loops

DefinitionA funnel is linear: inputs go in at the top, some customers come out at the bottom, and nothing flows back. A growth loop, a model developed by Brian Balfour and taught at Reforge, is a closed system in which the output of one cycle becomes the input of the next: users or content or revenue created by the loop feeds the loop again. Common types are viral (invites bring new users), content (user or company content attracts search traffic that creates more content), paid (revenue is reinvested in ads that return more revenue), and sales or partner loops.

Why it mattersFunnels need constant new spend to stay full. A loop compounds, so the same effort yields more over time. Thinking in loops also shows what to build, such as shareable output, proof pages and referral mechanics, not just what to buy.

In practiceWrite the loop as steps: input, action, output, reinvestment. Measure the conversion at each step and the cycle time. Improve the weakest step first. A loop only works if its output is better than its cost, so check unit economics (). Start with one loop; most small teams cannot run several. See for viral loops in detail.

Ideal customer, segments and jobs to be done

Christensen et al., Competing Against Luck (2016); Ulwick, Outcome-Driven Innovation

DefinitionThe ideal customer profile (ICP) describes the type of customer for whom your offer works best and who is most valuable to you. In B2B it is written at account level: industry, company size, location, tools used, budget and trigger events (a new funding round, a hire, a failing site). In B2C it describes a segment by need, behaviour and context, not only age and gender. A buyer persona is a fictional but research-based person within the profile: role, goals, objections, where they look for answers. Jobs to be done (JTBD), from Clayton Christensen and Tony Ulwick, says people "hire" a product to make progress in a situation. A job statement reads: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."

Why it mattersEvery later choice depends on it: which keywords, which channels, which message, which price. "Everyone" as a target produces weak ads, weak pages and a high cost per customer.

In practiceStart from real data, not guesses. List your best customers or users and look for shared traits. Run short interviews and ask about the last time they faced the problem: what triggered the search, what they tried, what almost stopped them. Review sales calls, support chats and reviews for their exact words. Write the ICP on one page, and add a negative ICP: who you should not chase. Keep it in a doc or Notion page and update it from win and loss reasons (). Segment by job first; demographics second.

Value proposition and offer

Osterwalder & Pigneur, Value Proposition Design (2014)

DefinitionThe value proposition states the benefit you deliver, to whom, and why it is better than the alternatives. The Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder and Pigneur) maps it: on the customer side, their jobs, pains and gains; on your side, products and services, pain relievers and gain creators. The offer is the concrete deal built on that proposition: exactly what is given, for what price or action, with what risk removed. A lead magnet is a free item (checklist, template, audit, calculator) offered in exchange for contact details. An offer ladder moves a person from a free step to a small paid step to the core product.

Why it mattersAds, pages and emails can only amplify what is on offer. A weak offer cannot be fixed by better targeting or better design. Conversion often rises more from a clearer offer than from any layout change.

In practiceWrite the proposition in one sentence: "For [customer] who [job], we [result], unlike [alternative], because [reason to believe]." Check it with the five-second test: show the page to someone for five seconds and ask what you sell and for whom. Strengthen the offer with a clear scope, a clear next step, and risk reversal (free plan, trial, sample, cancel anytime, a guarantee only if you can honour it). Test two offers against each other with a small traffic split (). Align the words on the page with your positioning ().

Leads: MQL, SQL and PQL

B2B demand generation practice

DefinitionA lead is a person or company that has shown interest and can be contacted. A marketing qualified lead (MQL) meets agreed marketing criteria for fit and interest, for example the right company type plus a pricing-page visit or a content download. A sales qualified lead (SQL) has been checked by sales and accepted as a real opportunity, often against a checklist such as BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline, an older IBM framework). A product qualified lead (PQL) is a user of a free product or trial who has done actions that show buying intent, such as hitting a usage limit or inviting teammates. Lead scoring gives points for fit (who they are) and behaviour (what they did).

Why it mattersCounting all leads equally hides quality. Marketing is then judged on volume while sales complains of junk. Clear definitions let both sides agree on what to hand over and when.

In practiceAgree stage definitions in writing, with who decides each. Add a service-level agreement (SLA): marketing hands over by a set rule, sales responds within a set time and records an outcome or a disqualification reason. Track conversion at each step: lead to MQL, MQL to SQL, SQL to customer (). A small team can run this in Google Sheets or a free CRM tier; fields needed are source, date, fit, stage and reason lost (). For a services company, the same ladder reads: enquiry, qualified call, proposal, won.

Channel strategy: the Bullseye framework

Weinberg & Mares, Traction (2014)

DefinitionA channel is a route to reach customers. In Traction (2014), Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares list 19 traction channels: viral marketing, public relations, unconventional PR, search engine marketing, social and display ads, offline ads, search engine optimisation, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing, targeting blogs, business development, sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements and community building. The Bullseye framework chooses among them in three rings: brainstorm one idea for every channel (outer ring), rank the most promising (middle ring), then run cheap tests on the top few to find the one (inner ring) to focus on. They also advise the "50 percent rule": spend about half your effort on product and half on traction.

Why it mattersMost small teams fail by spreading thin over every platform. One channel that works beats five that are half-tried. Channels also differ in how fast they pay and how much you control.

In practiceScore each channel on three questions: how many of your ideal customers can it reach, what would one customer cost, and can you start within weeks? Test in small steps, each with a defined metric and stop point (). Keep notes in a learning log (). Remember channel-model fit: the channel must suit your price, sales cycle and audience. A high-priced custom service rarely scales on cheap impulse ads; a free consumer app rarely scales on outbound sales.

SEO: how search traffic is won

Google Search Central documentation

DefinitionSearch engine optimisation (SEO) is improving a site so search engines can find, understand and rank its pages for the queries people actually type. A search engine works in three steps: crawling (bots fetch pages), indexing (pages are stored and understood) and ranking (results are ordered for each query). Work falls into technical SEO (the site can be crawled, loads fast, is mobile friendly), on-page SEO (titles, headings, content that matches the query) and off-page SEO (links and mentions from other sites, see ). Google's quality guidance uses E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. It is a quality concept used by raters, not a single ranking switch.

Why it mattersSearch shows people who are already looking, so intent is high and each good page can bring visitors for years without a per-click fee. It is slow to start, so it needs early, steady work.

In practiceStart with search intent: informational (learn), navigational (find a site), commercial (compare) and transactional (buy or enquire). Match page type to intent. Find keywords from customer questions, Google autosuggest, "People also ask", Google Trends and the free Keyword Planner. Verify the site in Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, and read the Performance report (queries, clicks, impressions, average position, CTR) and the Indexing report. Fix titles and descriptions of pages with many impressions but low CTR. Add internal links between related pages. Add structured data where it fits. Check speed in PageSpeed Insights (). Track organic sessions and conversions in GA4. Generative answers in search results are changing how clicks flow; clear, specific, well-sourced pages remain the safest base.

Local search and Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile Help

DefinitionLocal search covers queries with local intent, such as "web designer near me", and shows the local pack, the map and three listings at the top of results. The main free tool is the GBP (Google Business Profile, formerly Google My Business): a verified listing with name, category, address or service area, hours, services, photos, posts and reviews. Google says local ranking rests on relevance, distance and prominence. NAP means Name, Address, Phone: it must be identical everywhere you appear. A citation is any listing of those details on another site, for example directories such as Justdial, Sulekha or IndiaMART in India.

Why it mattersFor any business that serves a place or is searched by "near me", this is often the cheapest source of calls and enquiries, and the profile appears even when the website is weak.

In practiceClaim and verify the profile. Choose the most accurate primary category and add secondary ones. Fill every field: services, description, hours, attributes, photos and updates. Use a service area if you work at client sites. Keep NAP the same on the website, social pages and directories. Ask real customers for reviews with a simple link, reply to every review, and never buy reviews or filter who may leave one (Google's policy prohibits both). Add UTM tags to the website link () so visits show in GA4. In the profile's Performance view, watch searches, calls, direction requests, messages and website clicks. See for review practice.

Paid metrics and the small-budget test

Standard advertising metrics

DefinitionPaid media is judged by a short chain of metrics, each a ratio. They connect: the cost of a result equals the cost of a click divided by the conversion rate of clicks. Cost per result can only be called good or bad against your margin and customer value ().

Why it mattersWithout these numbers, spending is guessing. Each metric points to a different problem: low CTR means the ad or targeting is weak; high CPC with low CVR means the page or offer is weak; high CPM means a costly audience.

In practiceRun a small-budget test like a lab experiment. Write the hypothesis and the one thing you change. Fix the maximum you are willing to lose; that is the test budget. Decide success and stop rules before you start, from your target cost per lead or per customer. Run one audience or keyword group and one offer at a time. Do not edit mid-test. Wait until there are enough clicks and conversions to read: a handful of leads is noise (, ). Write the result and the decision in the learning log (). Anything that spends money needs approval first. Example only: spend 2,000, 100 clicks, 5 leads gives CPC = 20, CVR = 5 percent, CPL = 400; the identity CPL = CPC / CVR holds.

Conversion rate optimisation (CRO)

Laja, ResearchXL (CXL); Fogg Behavior Model

DefinitionConversion rate optimisation (CRO) is the systematic work of raising the share of visitors who take a wanted action. Conversion rate = conversions / visitors (or sessions; state which). A macro conversion is the main goal (sale, enquiry); a micro conversion is a step towards it (click on contact, scroll, form start). BJ Fogg's behaviour model says an action happens when motivation, ability and a prompt come together; most CRO reduces effort or raises motivation at the right moment. Study that page with free Microsoft Clarity heatmaps and recordings, plus a one-question survey in Google Forms. Write a hypothesis (), rank it () and test it (). With low traffic, make bolder changes and compare before and after carefully (). Remember that traffic quality limits any page.

A/B testing and statistics

Kohavi, Tang & Xu, Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments (2020)

DefinitionAn A/B test (online controlled experiment) randomly splits users into a control (A) and one or more variants (B) and compares one metric. Random assignment is what lets you claim cause. Statistics tells you whether the difference is bigger than chance.

Why it mattersBefore-and-after comparisons are fooled by seasons, campaigns and news. Only a concurrent random split isolates your change.

In practiceDecide the numbers before the test. A rule of thumb from Kohavi and colleagues for about 80% power and 5% alpha: sample per variant n ≈ 16 × σ² ÷ Δ², where σ² is the metric's variance and Δ is the smallest change you care about. For a conversion rate p, σ² = p × (1 − p). Example: baseline 5%, you want to detect +1 percentage point: σ² = 0.0475, n ≈ 16 × 0.0475 ÷ 0.0001 = 7,600 per variant. Run in whole weeks to cover weekday effects. Do not stop at the first significant result (peeking). Always check for sample ratio mismatch: if you planned 50/50 and the split is visibly different, the data is broken. Free tools: Google Sheets formulas, online calculators, or the tool below. Google Optimize shut down in September 2023; options now include other testing tools (some paid, so Small budget), or a coded split with GA4.

DefinitionCAC (customer acquisition cost) = total sales and marketing spend in a period ÷ new customers won in that period. Why it mattersGrowth that costs more than it returns is not growth. These three numbers say how much you can afford to pay for a customer and how long cash is tied up. Peter Fader's work stresses that customers differ in value, so averages hide the best and worst.

The ratio LTV ÷ CAC of about 3 is often quoted as healthy (a rule of thumb popularised by David Skok, not a law). Treat early LTV as an estimate. Referral and viral loops

Reichheld, HBR (2003); Balfour

DefinitionA referral is when a customer brings in another. The viral coefficient (K-factor) = invites sent per user × share of invites that convert. K above 1 means each user brings more than one new user. Viral cycle time is how long one loop takes. NPS (Net Promoter Score, Fred Reichheld, 2003) asks "How likely are you to recommend us, 0 to 10?"; promoters (9-10) minus detractors (0-6) as a percentage.

Why it mattersReferred leads usually arrive with more trust, and the loop compounds (see ). Few products reach K above 1, but even K of 0.2 lowers your cost of growth.

In practiceAsk when joy is highest: after a win, a delivered result or a high NPS answer. Make sharing one tap, with a pre-written message and a tracked link or code (). Offer a reward that fits: Dropbox's well-known programme gave extra storage to both sides. For B2B services, a simple ask after a project ends, a partner introduction or a referral thank-you works better than points. Disclose incentives clearly (), prevent abuse with limits, and track invites, joins and activated referrals.

Monetisation, pricing and expansion

Van Westendorp (1976); Kotler & Keller

DefinitionMonetisation is how a business turns value into revenue. Models: one-time, subscription, usage-based, freemium (free core, paid upgrade), free trial, tiered plans, and for services, project fees or retainers. ARPU (average revenue per user) = revenue ÷ users. MRR and ARR are monthly and annual recurring revenue. Expansion is more revenue from existing customers: upsell (bigger plan), cross-sell (another product), add-ons.

Why it mattersPrice is the strongest lever on profit and it signals quality. Free users still cost money, so the path from free to paid must be deliberate.

In practiceThree ways to set price: cost-plus, competitor-based and value-based (what the result is worth to the buyer). Value-based is usually strongest. Research it with Van Westendorp's Price Sensitivity Meter (four questions: too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive) or Gabor-Granger (willingness at set prices). Offer three tiers (good, better, best) so the middle looks sensible. Test prices on new visitors, never silently on existing customers. Track free-to-paid conversion, ARPU and expansion, and watch that new revenue does not raise churn (). Price is a hypothesis; log changes (). Tell customers clearly what they pay, and for India show prices with the right currency and payment options.

Lead to proposal: pipeline and CRM

BANT (IBM); MEDDIC; Lean Analytics

DefinitionThe sales pipeline is the set of deals at each stage between first contact and a decision. CRM (customer relationship management) software or a sheet records every contact, deal and next step. A stage has clear entry rules. Win rate = deals won ÷ deals closed. Sales cycle = days from first contact to close. Pipeline coverage = open pipeline value ÷ target.

Why it mattersFor B2B, most revenue is decided after the click. A lead that waits, or a proposal that never gets followed up, wastes all the marketing before it.

In practiceAgree stage definitions and write them down. Record the lead source on every lead (from and the form), so you can later link channels to revenue, not only leads. Respond fast: speed to first reply is a lever you control. Qualify with a framework such as BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline; from IBM) or the deeper MEDDIC (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion). Score leads on fit (matches the ideal customer, ) and intent (what they did). Always set a next step with a date. Log the reason for each lost deal. Free CRM options: a Notion or Google Sheets table, or the free tier of a CRM tool (check limits). Coverage targets such as 3 times are folk rules, so use your own win rate (see ).

DefinitionOutbound means you start the conversation: cold email, LinkedIn messages, calls. Inbound means they find you. Partnerships are structured relationships with others who reach your buyers: referral partners, complementary agencies, technology vendors, co-marketing. Why it mattersA new B2B brand has little search presence or reputation. Outbound and partners let you reach the right few buyers directly, and case studies are the proof that makes them reply.

Ethics, platform rules and compliance

DefinitionGrowth ethics means getting customers without deceiving or pressuring them. A dark pattern (term by Harry Brignull, 2010) is an interface that tricks users, such as hidden costs, fake urgency or a hard-to-find cancel button. In India, ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India) sets the advertising code, including its guidelines for influencer advertising (2021), and the CCPA (Central Consumer Protection Authority) regulates misleading ads and has issued guidelines on dark patterns (2023) under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

Make claims you can prove. Respect consent (, ). Avoid bought followers, fake reviews and click fraud. Get approval before spending on anything paid. See also and .
