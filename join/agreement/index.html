5Are you sure you want this?

Say yes only after thinking honestly. This is what joining Haritt really means.

This is the kind of company we are building, and you need to own it.

  • You are joining an early-stage company, not a finished one. Things change fast, and you help build them.
  • You are expected to think like an owner. Nobody will chase you for work.
  • There is no salary or fixed payment right now. Any payment is agreed separately, in writing.
  • No fixed hours. We look at attitude, honesty and results, not time spent.
  • What you put in decides what you become here. Growth is earned, never given by default.
If this excites you, read on. If not, that is completely fine. Just tell us honestly.

6Why everything is written here

We have learned from experience. These things have happened before, and we don't want them again:

People said "yes, I'll do it" on call, then did nothing.
People went silent for days or weeks without telling anyone.
People left without informing, leaving work half done.
Internal things were shared outside "casually", with friends.
Promises were made to clients that the team could not keep.
Later: "nobody told me", "it was not written", "I didn't read it".

So we wrote everything down, in simple words. Please read every page yourself before signing. If something is unclear, ask first. After signing, "I didn't know" will not be accepted.

7What we expect: 4 commitments

We don't ask for fixed hours. We ask for these four commitments. They are the base of everything at Haritt.

1. Communication
  • Reply to team messages within 24 hours, even if the reply is only "I'll get back by tomorrow".
  • If you are stuck, say it early. Don't wait until the deadline.
  • If you will miss a date, tell us before the date, with a new date.
  • Share short updates on your work: done, doing next, blocked.
2. Professionalism
  • Do what you say, when you say. If plans change, inform.
  • Be on time for calls and meetings, and come prepared.
  • Be polite and respectful with clients, the team and everyone you speak to as Haritt.
  • Keep your work neat, complete and checked before sharing it.
3. Ownership
  • Treat Haritt as your own company. Its success and its problems are yours too.
  • Finish what you start. Don't leave work half done.
  • If you see a problem, fix it or raise it. Don't think "this is not my job".
  • Take responsibility for mistakes, fix them, and learn from them.
4. Confidentiality
  • Everything inside Haritt stays inside Haritt.
  • Don't share company matters with friends, family or anyone outside the team. "Just one friend" is also a breach.
  • No screenshots, recordings or copies for personal use.
  • Full rule in section 17.

8Abilities we look for

Skills can be learned. These abilities matter more. In your 10 days, the founding team will look for them.

  1. Learning ability. You are ready to learn anything new, quickly. "I don't know this" is never the end. Say: "I don't know this yet, I will learn it."
  2. Honest about weak areas. Everyone has weak areas. Don't hide them, and don't use them as an excuse. Tell us, and work on them.
  3. Ownership. You take full charge of your work, from start to finish, without reminders.
  4. Problem solving. When something goes wrong, you look for a solution first, not someone to blame.
  5. Reliability. People can depend on you. When you commit, it gets done.
  6. Initiative. You don't wait to be told. You suggest ideas and start useful work yourself.
  7. Clear communication. You explain things simply, ask when unclear, and keep people informed.
  8. Honesty. You tell the truth, even when it is uncomfortable. No fake work, no fake numbers.
  9. Discipline and consistency. You keep showing up and keep working, not only when you feel like it.
  10. Adaptability. Plans change in a young company. You adjust quickly without complaining.
  11. Respect and teamwork. You respect everyone, help others, and put the team first.
  12. Attention to detail. You check your work before you share it. Small mistakes matter.

9What you can expect from Haritt

This works both ways. In return, the founding team will:

  • Explain clearly what is expected from you, and answer your questions.
  • Give you real work and real responsibility, not only practice tasks.
  • Give honest feedback on time, including at the Day 4 review and the final review.
  • Give access and tools step by step, as your role needs.
  • Keep your personal details private and use them only for onboarding and records.
  • Confirm every important decision about you (role, level, payment) in writing.

12Your 10 days are a learning curve

These 10 days are for learning and understanding. New ideas are always welcome at Haritt, but first you need to understand clearly:

  • What Haritt Group is, what our aim is, and where we are going.
  • What Haritt Developers does: a business solutions partner, mainly B2B. It works with businesses to understand their problem, find the right solution and make it happen, end to end.
  • What HV Verse does: a product venture, mainly B2C, building apps that help students and individuals. It is also open to B2B.
  • Why each venture exists, who its core audience is, and how they work together under Haritt Group.
  • How we work: our tools, our process, and how we talk to clients and users.

Once you understand this, your ideas become much stronger. Ask as many questions as you need.

17Confidentiality

What happens inside Haritt stays inside Haritt. Our ideas, clients and plans are our identity. If they go outside, we lose that identity, our clients' trust and our advantage. That is why this rule is strict.

You will never share
  • Client names, contact details, requirements and conversations
  • Prices, quotes, deals and contracts
  • Revenue, profit, losses, payments and any financial details
  • Internal problems, mistakes, disputes and decisions
  • Plans, ideas, products and launch dates not yet public
  • Designs, code, documents, tools, logins and passwords
  • Team chats, calls, meetings and screenshots
  • Anything about the founders and team members
With anyone outside the team
  • Friends and classmates
  • Family and relatives, even close ones
  • Other companies, clients or freelancers
  • Social media, groups, stories and posts
  • "Just one person" or "in confidence" is also sharing
What you can share

Only positive, public things that help the brand: our websites, public posts, launched products and approved work. Telling people you know about Haritt in a positive way, for marketing, is welcome. If it is not public, don't share it.

Not sure if something can be shared?Don't share it. Ask the founding team directly first. In your first 10 days, the founding team decides what can be shared.
After the final review, as Founding Team or Co-founderOnce your level is confirmed, you share the responsibility of deciding what can be shared, because you are an owner. Until then, always ask.
Breaking this ruleSharing confidential information is a serious breach. It means immediate removal from Haritt and all access, and Haritt may take legal action under Indian law, including claiming damages. This rule starts today and continues forever, even after you leave.

18Ownership: act like the owner

We are building this company with people who think like founders. Founding Team and Co-founder carry the same responsibility. At either level, you own your area completely, like it is your own company, and you act for the whole brand.

If you own it, you own everything in it.

  • You act as the owner of your area, not as someone helping from outside.
  • You act as the brand. How you talk, work and behave is how people see Haritt.
  • If your area or the brand gets into trouble because of it, it is your responsibility to handle it. No excuses, no blaming others.
  • You don't wait to be told. You see what needs doing and you do it, end to end.
The full details come in the full Team Agreement. We are telling you now, so it is clear from day one.
