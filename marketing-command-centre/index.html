THE CENTRAL RECOMMENDATION

One parent brand. Two clear business directions.

Use Haritt Group for the company story, Haritt Development for client software services, and HV World for owned digital products when the umbrella genuinely connects them.

THE OPERATING RULE

Proof before promotion.

Publish real demos, real project evidence and honest product limitations. Do not buy more domains, create too many social accounts or run paid ads before the conversion path works.

Decision guardrail: distinguish brand structure from legal structure. Do not imply that a service line is a separately registered company unless that is factually true. Verify public spelling, live product status, feature claims and contact details before publishing.
02 / Brand architecture

One parent. Two distinct routes.

Different audiences need different messages, but they do not automatically need separate domains, companies or social accounts.

Haritt GroupParent identity · Company story · Trust and partnerships
Haritt Development

Client services: websites, web applications and practical software solutions. Lead with the client's business problem, scope, delivery process and credible proof.

HV World

Owned digital products: HV Reset, HV Vault, HV Test and HV AI. Lead with specific use cases, product demos and a clear next action.

Keep the existing haritt.com domain and use clear paths/subdomains only where they improve the user journey. Do not buy new domains simply to make the architecture look larger.
03 / Positioning & messaging

Every brand needs one clear promise.

PARENT BRAND

Haritt Group

Audience: partners, prospective clients, collaborators and future team members.

Message: who we are, what we build, and how our services and products fit together.

CTA: Explore our work.

SERVICE LINE

Haritt Development

Audience: startups, SMEs, founders and businesses needing software.

Message: practical digital solutions built around a defined business problem.

CTA: Discuss a project.

PRODUCT ECOSYSTEM

HV World

Audience: students, job seekers, professionals and people who want useful everyday tools.

Message: plan your day, organise a job search and explore self-assessments in one product experience.

CTA: Try HV World.

05 / Content operating system

Make one useful thing. Repurpose it well.

Start with a cadence a small team can actually sustain. Content should demonstrate value or answer a real question, not exist just to fill a calendar.

MondayFounder lesson

One decision, lesson or challenge from building Haritt or HV World. Founder LinkedIn.

TuesdayProduct demo

A short screen recording showing one real workflow. HV World Shorts/Reels.

WednesdayProof or insight

A development insight or genuine case study, if ready. Avoid invented outcomes.

ThursdayTutorial

Answer one user question or teach one product workflow. YouTube/LinkedIn.

FridayFeedback loop

Release note, user feedback response or weekly lesson. HV World.

HV WORLD CONTENT PILLARS

Show the actual utility

  • HV Reset: daily planning, task changes, focus and progress.
  • HV Vault: job tracking, follow-up reminders and weekly progress.
  • HV Test: explain what each score means and does not mean. Never present it as a clinical diagnosis.
  • HV AI: one specific task, supported languages, confirmation controls and limitations.
  • Cross-product story: only describe integrations and benefits that work today.
HARITT DEVELOPMENT CONTENT PILLARS

Demonstrate competence

  • Portfolio walkthroughs labelled accurately as client work, internal product, prototype or experiment.
  • Case studies: problem, constraints, approach, deliverables, results and lessons.
  • Useful technical explanations without exposing client data or secrets.
  • Clear process: discovery, proposal, delivery, testing, handover and support.
Content quality test: one audience, one real problem, one useful takeaway, one CTA. Track clicks and downstream actions. Views and likes are signals, not the business outcome.
06 / Execution roadmap

90 days, in seven practical phases.

Each phase has an exit criterion. Move forward when the outcome is achieved, not simply because the date passed.

Days 1–7 · Decide

Resolve the fundamentals

Exit: one-page brand map and accurate claims.

  • Confirm brand spelling and hierarchy.
  • Define product status and Development service scope.
  • Audit the live website and hosting.
Days 8–14 · Structure

Map the user journey

Exit: approved sitemap and core copy.

  • Set page paths, CTAs, footer and contact routes.
  • Write audience-specific positioning.
  • Gather genuine screenshots and project examples.
Days 15–30 · Build

Make the core experience work

Exit: visitors can complete the intended action.

  • Publish accurate core and product pages.
  • Test mobile, links, forms, privacy and sign-up.
  • Fix broken or confusing journeys.
Days 31–45 · Measure

Instrument the basics

Exit: key traffic sources and actions are measurable.

  • Configure Search Console and analytics.
  • Check sitemap, titles and canonical URLs.
  • Secure social accounts and recovery methods.
Days 46–60 · Test demand

Talk to real people

Exit: useful feedback, enquiries or clear evidence of weak demand.

  • Publish product demos and founder posts.
  • Interview prospective users.
  • Contact relevant Development prospects.
Days 61–75 · Improve

Remove friction

Exit: improved activation or enquiry quality.

  • Fix confusing copy and onboarding.
  • Publish a genuine case study.
  • Repeat content formats that work.
Days 76–90 · Decide

Keep what earns its place

Exit: a measured next-quarter plan.

  • Review performance by channel and product.
  • Stop low-value activity.
  • Justify every new account or domain with evidence.
Always · Guardrails

Protect focus and trust

Do not mistake busyness for progress.

  • No unnecessary domain purchases.
  • No separate account for every product.
  • No paid ads before conversion paths work.
  • No unsupported claims about clients, AI or outcomes.
