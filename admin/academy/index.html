Is this what an MBA teaches?

The MBA brand-management syllabus, mapped

Yes. Brand management in an MBA is usually taught from Kevin Lane Keller's Strategic Brand Management, with Aaker, Kapferer and Kotler & Keller's Marketing Management alongside. Syllabi differ between schools, but the modules below are the common core. Newer research (Sharp, Romaniuk, Binet & Field) is now added in most courses too.

Tap any card number to jump to it. Concepts marked MBA core were added so the page covers the full syllabus.

01
Part 1 · Foundations

What a brand actually is

Before logos and colours, four ideas that every brand conversation in a company is built on.

01

Brand

American Marketing Association; Keller

DefinitionThe AMA defines a brand as "a name, term, design, symbol, or any other feature that identifies one seller's good or service as distinct from those of other sellers." Keller adds the "small b / big B" difference: the small-b brand is the logo and name; the big-B Brand is the awareness, reputation and prominence those create in the market.

Why it mattersPeople don't remember logos. They remember how you made them feel and whether you delivered. That memory decides whether they come back, recommend you or pay more. In a company, "brand" work means managing that memory on purpose.

At Haritt Group

Haritt had a brand long before it had a brand system. In 2020, when you, Pulkit and Rishi built websites for people From you, the people you worked for formed a memory of "Haritt": on time or late, good or average. That memory was the brand.

Today the name sits on a corporate brand that owns two ventures, Haritt Developers and HV Verse Verified.

02

Brand identity vs brand image

Kapferer; Keller

DefinitionBrand identity is the sender side: what the company decides to project (name, logo, colours, words, promise). Brand image is the receiver side: the set of associations people actually hold in memory. The gap between the two is called the identity-image gap.

Why it mattersDesign controls identity. Only research tells you the image. Most brand work is closing that gap.

03

Brand promise

DefinitionThe commitment the brand makes to every customer, every time: the experience they can count on. It is wider than a tagline. A tagline is how you say it in public; the promise is what you must deliver.

Why it mattersA promise sets the standard for every decision. If a feature, page or reply breaks it, it shouldn't ship.

At Haritt Group

"Useful technology, made simple."haritt.com homepage headline Verified

A good promise: short, clear and testable. HV Reset's "one task at a time" keeps it. A homepage button that still says "Watch the replay" for an event that has ended does not keep "simple" for a new visitor.

04

Touchpoints and the customer journey

DefinitionA touchpoint is any place a person meets the brand: website, Instagram, a WhatsApp reply, an email, the product itself. The customer journey puts touchpoints in order: aware, consider, try, use, return, recommend. Identity travels through touchpoints and becomes image, and image drives behaviour.

Part 2 · Purpose

Why Haritt exists

Purpose is the part of a brand that doesn't change when products change. It is also the part a young company can use before it has proof.

05

Brand purpose and the Golden Circle

Simon Sinek, Start With Why (2009)

DefinitionBrand purpose is the reason the brand exists beyond making money. Sinek's Golden Circle puts it in three rings: why (the belief), how (the way you work) and what (the product). His claim is that brands people love communicate from the inside out.

Why it mattersProducts change. Haritt went from client websites to AI apps. A clear why connects both, so the brand doesn't restart each time.

  • WHYPowerful tech is built for experts; everyone deserves simple tools that help
  • HOWUseful, simple, private, honest
  • WHATHV Verse apps and Haritt Developers services
At Haritt Group

The why already exists on the About page: "Powerful technology is everywhere, but most of it is built for experts" Verified. The homepage, though, leads with what (products, stats, "Try HV Verse"). That is normal for a product site, but the parent brand is the one place where the why should come first.

06

Mission and vision

DefinitionA mission statement says what you do today, for whom and why. A vision statement says what you want to become. A good mission is specific enough to say no to things. A good vision is ambitious but believable.

Mission Verified

"To make useful technology simple and accessible for everyone, from individuals to growing businesses."

"Everyone" is broad. It says yes to almost anything.

Vision Verified

"To become India's most trusted home for simple, useful products that people rely on every day."

Strong: clear market (India), clear test (trust, daily use).

07

Brand values and principles

DefinitionCore values are the beliefs that guide behaviour. Operating principles turn them into decision rules. A real principle forces you to say no to something. If nobody can name what a principle stopped, it is decoration.

Why it mattersYou told me Haritt has no principles From you. The About page lists four Verified. When the founders don't know them, they are copy, not principles. Tap a card to see what each one should stop.

Part 3 · Strategy

Who it's for and why it's different

Strategy is choosing. A brand that is for everyone is remembered by no one.

09

Segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP)

Kotler & Keller, Marketing Management

DefinitionSTP is the core marketing strategy sequence. Segmentation splits the market into groups with the same need (by demographics, behaviour, need or firmographics for B2B). Targeting picks the segments you will serve first. Positioning decides how you want those segments to see you (next card). For a parent brand, the target audience is often not the end user.

Why it mattersThe homepage speaks to three groups at once: individuals, professionals and startups Verified. Those are HV Verse's and Developers' customers. The stakeholders who actually judge a parent company are different.

10

Positioning statement and frame of reference

Ries & Trout (1981); Moore, Crossing the Chasm (1991); Keller

DefinitionPositioning is the place you want to hold in the target's mind compared with the alternatives. Keller's first step is the competitive frame of reference: which category you belong to and who you are compared with. The standard template: For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [reason to believe]. The last part is the RTB.

11

Points of parity and points of difference

Keller, Sternthal & Tybout, HBR (2002)

DefinitionPoints of parity (PoP) are associations every credible brand in the category must have just to be considered. Points of difference (PoD) are associations customers value that only you deliver. A PoD must be desirable, deliverable and differentiating.

Why it mattersMost young brands advertise parity ("fast, secure, modern") as if it were difference. Customers don't notice.

Parity (must have)

Working products, a clear website, contact details, privacy policy, social presence Verified

Difference (only Haritt)

Hinglish AI across apps, privacy without cookies, a services studio that runs its own product, a founder story from 2020 Our reading

12

Perceptual map

DefinitionA perceptual (or positioning) map plots brands on two attributes customers care about. You place yourself and the alternatives and look for white space: a position nobody owns.

Why it mattersIt shows at a glance whether you are crowded or own a corner. Hover or tap a dot.

Tap a dotEach point is a type of alternative, not a named company.

Our reading Illustrative placement, not survey data. A real map comes from asking customers to rate each option.

13

Value proposition and USP

Reeves, Reality in Advertising (1961)

DefinitionThe value proposition is the full answer to "why should I choose you?": the benefits, for whom, against which alternative. The USP is the single claim no competitor makes. Today many teams say UVP for the same idea.

At Haritt Group

The group's strongest unique claim is not a feature. It is the combination: a team that builds services for clients and runs its own live products. Developers says it directly: "We show work we built and operate ourselves" Verified. Few small studios can say that.

14

Brand mantra

Keller, Strategic Brand Management

DefinitionA three to five word internal phrase that captures the heart of the brand: emotional modifier, descriptive modifier, brand function. It is for the team, not for ads. Keller's textbook examples: Nike, "authentic athletic performance"; Disney, "fun family entertainment".

Why it mattersWhen a decision is unclear, the mantra settles it. It also makes every new product easier to judge.

"Useful tech, made simple."Draft mantra for Haritt Group, taken from the existing promise Draft
15

Tagline and messaging hierarchy

MBA core

DefinitionA tagline (slogan, strapline) is the short public line used with the logo. A messaging hierarchy, also called a messaging framework or message house, orders everything else the brand says: one core message at the top, three or so message pillars under it, and proof points under each pillar. Every page, post and pitch picks from it.

Why it mattersWithout a hierarchy, every page invents its own message and the brand sounds like three companies. With one, a new writer can be on-brand on day one.

MBA coreKeller; Aaker, Brand Portfolio Strategy (2004)

DefinitionThe brand portfolio is every brand the company owns, each with a role: flagship, flanker, fighter, silver bullet or cash cow. The brand hierarchy shows the naming levels, from corporate brand to family brand to individual brand to modifier. A naming system (nomenclature) is the rule for naming anything new.

Why it mattersWithout a naming rule, every new product gets a name by mood, and the family stops looking like a family.

18

Brand extension, dilution and cannibalisation

Aaker & Keller (1990)

DefinitionA brand extension uses an existing brand name for a new product. A line extension stays in the same category; a category extension moves into a new one. Extensions succeed when there is fit with what people already believe. Bad fit causes brand dilution, weakening what the brand stands for. Cannibalisation is when a new product takes users from your own existing one.

At Haritt Group

The About page plans "new products under the Haritt name" Verified. That conflicts with the house-of-brands model, where products live under HV Verse, not Haritt. Decide the rule now: new consumer apps join the HV family, and new business offers carry the Haritt name. Draft

19

Co-branding, ingredient branding and licensing

Keller: leveraging secondary brand associations

DefinitionKeller calls these "leveraging secondary associations": borrowing meaning from something else. Co-branding shows two brands together so each borrows the other's trust. Ingredient branding shows one brand as a part inside another product ("Intel Inside"). Licensing lets another company use your brand for a fee. Endorsements, events and causes are other sources.

For later

A small "Built by Haritt Developers" credit in the footer of every client site is ingredient branding. It is free and turns every client site into a touchpoint. Ask each client first. Draft

20

Rebranding, refresh and revitalisation

Keller: managing brands over time

DefinitionA refresh updates the look and keeps the meaning. A rebrand changes the name, positioning or identity. Keller's "managing brands over time" adds brand reinforcement (keeping a brand consistent and relevant) and brand revitalisation (bringing a fading brand back).

Why it mattersThe cost of renaming grows with every user, link and search result. If a name is going to change, earlier is cheaper.

At Haritt

A new name for HV Verse is already being discussed in its own thread. Rule of thumb for that decision: rename only if the current name blocks growth, and do it before the user base grows. Haritt Group's own name doesn't need a rebrand. The double-T spelling just needs consistency (Part 6). Haritt itself is a revitalisation case: a 2020 brand brought back in 2026.

People choose brands the way they choose friends. Character decides who trusts you and who ignores you.

21

Brand personality

Jennifer Aaker, Journal of Marketing Research (1997)

DefinitionBrand personality is the set of human characteristics associated with a brand. Aaker's Brand Personality Scale grouped them into five dimensions: sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication and ruggedness. Strong brands lead with one or two.

Brand archetypes

Brand voice and tone of voice

DefinitionVoice is the brand's constant personality in words. Tone of voice is how that voice adjusts to the moment: an error message, a launch post and an apology sound different, but come from the same voice. Teams write it down as "we are / we are not" pairs plus examples.

Haritt Group is

Distinctive brand assets

Jenni Romaniuk, Building Distinctive Brand Assets (2018)

DefinitionDistinctive brand assets (DBA) are non-name elements that trigger the brand: a colour, shape, character, sound or phrase. Romaniuk grades each on two measures: fame (what share of category buyers link it to you) and uniqueness (whether it points only to you).

Why it mattersAssets are built by repetition over years. Picking them early and using them everywhere is the cheapest brand building there is.

Why it mattersSmall inconsistencies cost recognition. Each one is cheap to fix and expensive to ignore.

Part 7 · Growth

How brands actually grow

How the brand is communicated, and the evidence from the last fifteen years that changed how brand teams spend time and money.

32

Integrated marketing communications

MBA coreKeller: designing and integrating communications

Mental availability, physical availability and category entry points

Byron Sharp, How Brands Grow (2010); Romaniuk

DefinitionSharp's research at the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute argues that brands grow mainly by reaching more buyers (penetration), not by deepening loyalty. Mental availability is how easily you come to mind in buying situations. Physical availability is how easy you are to find and buy. Category entry points (CEP) are the moments and needs that start a search.

Why it mattersA brand is remembered for specific moments, not for being "good". The job is to link Haritt to the moments that matter.

Brand building vs performance marketing

Binet & Field, The Long and the Short of It (2013)

DefinitionBrand building creates memory and preference over time. Activation (performance marketing) captures people who are ready to act now. Binet and Field's analysis of the IPA effectiveness databank found a balance of roughly 60% brand and 40% activation worked best on average for consumer brands. For B2B, LinkedIn's B2B Institute, working with them, suggested about 46:54. They also link growth to ESOV.

Why it mattersSpending only on performance gets quick results that fade. Spending only on brand builds memory nobody acts on.

Brand equity: the CBBE pyramid and Aaker's model

Keller (2001); Aaker, Managing Brand Equity (1991)

DefinitionKeller's CBBE model defines equity as the different response a brand gets because of what customers know about it. The pyramid builds it in four steps: salience (identity: who are you?), performance and imagery (meaning: what are you?), judgements and feelings (response: what do I think of you?) and resonance (relationship: what about us?). You can't skip a step. Aaker's model lists the assets instead: awareness, perceived quality, associations, loyalty and other proprietary assets.

Brand Asset Valuator

MBA coreYoung & Rubicam, BAV

DefinitionThe BAV is one of the largest brand research databases. It scores four pillars: differentiation (how different), relevance (how useful to me), esteem (how well regarded) and knowledge (how well understood). Differentiation and relevance together are brand strength, which predicts future growth. Esteem and knowledge are brand stature, which shows the current position. New brands typically rise in strength first.

Brand valuation

MBA coreInterbrand; Brand Finance; ISO 10668

DefinitionBrand valuation puts a money value on a brand. Interbrand's method combines financial performance, the role the brand plays in the purchase decision, and brand strength. Brand Finance uses "royalty relief": what you would pay to license the brand if you didn't own it. ISO 10668 sets the standard for doing it. Brand value is a money figure; brand equity is the customer-side strength behind it.

Why it mattersIt matters in fundraising, mergers and licensing. For now it matters to you as vocabulary.

At Haritt Group

Brand loyalty, brand community and advocacy

MBA core

DefinitionBehavioural loyalty is repeat use; attitudinal loyalty is preference and attachment. A brand community is users connecting with each other around the brand. Why it mattersSharp's research says loyalty mostly follows size, so don't over-invest early. But for a B2B studio, a few loud happy clients are worth more than any ad.
