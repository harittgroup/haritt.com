1
Haritt Developers earns money by building websites, software, brand work and automation for small businesses. The site lists seven services. Nobody buys on the first visit. An owner reads your pages, perhaps looks at the work, sends the form on "Start a project", talks to you, gets a quote, and only then signs. The funnel: from first visit to signed project

Seven steps of one path, and the ones nobody records yet.

View services or workOpen contact pageStart the formSend enquiry
Discovery call heldProposal sentProject wonClient refers another

A funnel is the path people take towards a goal, and it is called a funnel because each step loses some people. Your admin dashboard already draws the first half for developers.haritt.com: Visitors, then Services or Work, then the Contact page, then Started form, then Sent enquiry. The second half does not exist anywhere. Nothing records that a call happened, that a proposal went out or that you won.

Look at the numbers you do have. For the 30 days to 13 Oct, with your own devices removed, the dashboard showed 301 sessions (visits) and 633 page views, which is about two pages per visit. Be careful with the tile called Visitors, 211 for that period: it adds up the different devices seen each day, so it is not 211 separate people. One person on two days counts twice. And calls that come from Google Business Profile or Justdial never touch your website, so they never enter this funnel at all.

AExtend the funnel in a sheetOne row per enquiry, one column per stage.

Make a Google Sheet with columns: date, name of business, service asked for, source (how they found you), budget band, then date of call, date of proposal, project value, won or lost, and a notes column. This is your funnel after the enquiry. It takes five minutes per enquiry and needs no change to the website.

BLog what happens outside the sitePhone calls and messages from listings count too.

Add a row for every call, message or introduction that comes from Google, Justdial, LinkedIn or a friend, even if no form was sent. Put the source in the source column. This is the only way to see how much of your work comes from places the counter cannot see.

CWrite one definition per stageQualified, call held, proposal, won, lost.

Write one line for each stage and agree it with your co-founder. For example: qualified means the enquiry is for a service you offer and has a real brief. Call held means you actually spoke. Lost means they said no or went silent after a reminder. If the definitions are loose, the numbers will be too.

Five stages for a business that sells projects

Acquisition, activation, revenue, retention and referral, in your words.

AcquisitionAn owner visits
ActivationA real call is held
RevenueA project is signed
RetentionSupport or repeat work
ReferralA client sends another

The five stages growth teams use are usually called AARRR, said like a pirate. They are acquisition, activation, revenue, retention and referral. They are only useful if you put them in your own words, so here they are for a studio. Work on them in the order below, because a later stage is hard to improve when the earlier one is blank.

1Acquisition: an owner visitsMeasured today by visits and enquiry_sent.

Someone lands on the site and looks around. You already count this. The question to answer is which route they came from, and today you cannot answer it because enquiries carry no source.

2Activation: a discovery call is heldDefined here, not recorded yet.

For a service business an enquiry is not yet a customer. The first moment of real value is the first proper conversation. Your files suggest "discovery call held" as the activation event. Record the date in the sheet.

3Revenue: a project is signedNeeds value and won or lost.

Add the project value and whether it was won or lost for every enquiry. If you can only add one thing this month, add this. It is the only stage that pays the bills.

4Retention: support or repeat workLater, once there are clients.

You already offer Consulting and Support as a service. There are no client records yet, so this stage has nothing to measure. Revisit it after your first finished project.

5Referral: a client sends anotherAsk, then count.

Referrals do not happen by themselves. Near the end of each project, ask for one introduction and write down the answer. Count introductions in the sheet.

1
An ideal client (marketers say ideal customer profile, or ICP) is the one type of business you serve better than anyone else and who also gets real value from you. Your site today speaks to every business. It lists seven services: Development and Software, Branding and Creative, Digital Marketing, Lead Generation and Conversion, AI and Automation, E-commerce Solutions, and Consulting and Support. When a promise fits everyone, a visitor cannot tell whether it is for them, and they leave to find someone who says "we build sites for coaching centres" or "we fix slow follow-up for shops".
2
Three ideas belong together here. The ideal client is who you aim at. The offer is what exactly you promise them and what they can say yes to first. Fit (product-market fit, in the usual phrase) is the evidence that the right people actually want that offer. For a studio with few clients, fit does not come from big surveys. It comes from conversations and from what your first clients say.
3
Your social plan lists three audiences: small-business owner, founder or startup, and referrer (people like accountants, designers and printers who know many owners). The choice between them is open. My reading: a referrer is a route to clients, not a client, so the real choice is between small-business owners and founders.
4
There is also a cost to waiting. Posts, listings, the Google profile and the intro emails are all about to be written. If they are written before this choice, they will all say the same broad thing, and you will have to rewrite each of them later. Choosing first means every piece of writing you do from now on has one reader in mind. That is why this lesson sits ahead of the channel work, and why it is worth a couple of focused evenings rather than a long project.
5
Do you need this now? Yes, and it comes before choosing any channel. It is free. If you skip it, every post, listing and email will be written for "businesses" and will read like every other studio. What to do instead of waiting for more traffic: use the enquiries and conversations you already have.

1
Fit means people want what you offer. A studio will not have thousands of users to survey, so test it with a small, honest series of conversations. Say you speak to 10 owners of your chosen kind of business (a made-up number). Count how many describe the problem in their own words, how many want the first step you offer, and how many ask for a proposal. These counts tell you far more than likes on a post.
2
Keep one sheet with a row per conversation. Columns: date, business type, the problem in their words, reaction to the offer, what they said about price, and the outcome. Write the words people use. After a few rows, you will see phrases repeat, and those phrases belong in your headline.
3
Look for three signs of fit: owners describe the same problem unprompted, they ask about price or timing quickly, and finished clients would recommend you. At the end of each month, count the rows in each outcome column and write the counts at the top. Do not build charts yet. Three or four numbers and your own notes are enough. If you have only a few rows, say to yourself that it is too early to conclude anything and keep collecting. That is a fair answer, and better than a confident wrong one.

What testing means, and why a formal A/B test is not for you yet

A test is a fair comparison. At about 10 visits a day, you test bold changes and talk to people instead.

Formal A/B testNeeds thousands of visits

vs
Your kind of testBold change, careful read

Before and after, plus real conversations.

1
Say you change your Start a project headline and the next week brings three enquiries instead of one. Did the headline work? You cannot tell. A festival, a good post or plain luck could have done it. A test is a way of making the comparison fair. In an A/B test, visitors are split at random: half see the current page (the control) and half see the changed one (the variant), at the same time. Because the split is random, a gap between them can be blamed on your change, and statistics tells you whether the gap is bigger than chance.
2
The catch is volume. For the last 30 days to 13 Oct you had 301 sessions, about 10 a day, and your enquiry rate is not on file. Here is a worked example with made-up numbers. Say your enquiry rate is 5 percent and you want to detect a rise to 7 percent. A common rule of thumb for the visitors needed per version is 16 times the variance divided by the square of the smallest change you care about. The variance of a rate is p times (1 minus p), so 0.05 times 0.95 is 0.0475. The smallest change is 0.02, squared 0.0004. That gives 16 times 0.0475 divided by 0.0004, which is 1,900 visitors per version, so 3,800 in all. At 10 a day that is about 380 days, more than a year.
Do you need formal A/B testing now? No, later. At this traffic a classic test cannot finish in a useful time, and calling noise a win would mislead you. But the habit behind testing is needed now: write down a guess, change one thing, compare fairly, and keep notes. The rest of this lesson shows how to do that with small numbers, so every change you make teaches you something.

Paid ads mean paying a platform such as Google or Instagram to show your message to people. Paid search puts an ad above the results when someone types a word you chose. Paid social puts an ad in a feed, often to people who match an age or interest group. Retargeting is showing ads to people who have already visited your site, using a small tracking snippet (a pixel) from the ad platform.

PPaid search: the one idea that could fitDefending the name, and only if the name is searched.

No Google Ads account or conversion tag exists in your files, and both the HV Verse and Group plans say organic only for now. The only paid search idea that makes sense for the Group is an ad on the exact name Haritt, so a look-alike company cannot take the click. But that only helps if people type the name. If Search Console shows near zero impressions for the name, the ad would be a bill for nothing. So the trigger is data you will already have: steady name impressions, and look-alikes above you for them.

PPaid social and retargeting: blocked on purposeYour privacy choice is the reason.

GA4 on haritt.com is set with ad storage, ad user data and ad personalisation denied, and Google signals are off. This means GA4 cannot build lists of past visitors for ads, by design. There are no ad pixels either, and no cookie banner. Starting retargeting would need a consent change and a privacy text update first. Also, there is nothing to retarget people towards: the Group sells nothing. A boosted post could be tested later, but only a post that already did well without help, tagged as paid so you can separate it in analytics.

TThe cost of doing it anywayMoney is the small part.

Beyond spend, a paid test costs setup time, a privacy change you would have to explain to visitors, and the risk that the numbers mislead you. A page with thin proof turns paid visits into quick exits, and you would learn only that the page needs work, which you can find out free with five people and a phone.

A made-up example to show the sum. Say Search Console shows 40 impressions a month for the name, and you would pay ₹3,000 for a month of brand-name ads. Even if every ad click were free of waste, 40 searches is a very small audience, and most of those people would already have clicked haritt.com in the normal results. The ₹3,000 would mostly buy clicks you were getting for free. Now say instead the figure were 4,000 impressions and a look-alike company sat above you. The sum looks different, and a small capped test makes sense. The same money, two different answers, and the data is what decides.

When the trigger arrives, do not skip the preparation described in the earlier paid ads lesson for the apps: write the goal, the cap and the stop rule first, and get your OK before any account is created.

Retention means how many people come back after their first visit, usually measured after a day, a week and a month. For an app like HV Reset, it is a key sign of whether the product is useful. For a corporate page, it is mostly beside the point. A client does not need to visit haritt.com every week. They visit it once or twice to check that you are real. Chasing return visits there would push you to add reasons to come back that do not match the page's job.

The dashboard does show cohorts (groups of visitors from the same week) at one, seven, fourteen and thirty days, plus weekly and monthly active users. It counts devices, so a new browser counts as a new person. A reading on another tab showed Returning 0, WAU 0 and MAU 0, which may be a bug. Until someone checks that, do not read anything into these figures for haritt.com.

TThe signal that would change the answerContent that people come back to.

If Playbooks grows to several useful articles, return visits become a fair measure of whether readers find them worth it. At that point, look at returning readers on the Playbooks pages only, not on the whole site. Until then, skip retention targets entirely.

There is a wider lesson here about app-style retention: streaks, reminders, notifications and loyalty programmes belong in products that people use repeatedly, like HV Reset or HV Hunt, and they are covered in the HV Verse lessons. Copying them onto a company page would add effort and confuse the page's job, which is to be understood and believed in one visit.
