What growth means for Haritt Developers

You sell projects to businesses, so growth means good conversations, not clicks.

CatchingPeople already looking

Name searches, listings, the enquiry form.

vs
CreatingPeople who have not heard of you

Posts, shown work, introductions.

1
Haritt Developers earns money by building websites, software, brand work and automation for small businesses. The site lists seven services. Nobody buys on the first visit. An owner reads your pages, perhaps looks at the work, sends the form on "Start a project", talks to you, gets a quote, and only then signs. So growth marketing for you means improving every step on that path, from the first time an owner hears your name to the day a client sends you another client. It does not mean "more visitors" by itself. A hundred visitors who are students looking for a free tool are worth less than five owners with a real brief.
2
It helps to split the work in two. Capturing means catching people who are already looking for you. Today you are mostly set up for this: on 14 Oct a search for "Haritt Developers" showed developers.haritt.com at #1 and #2 and the Justdial listing at #3 (from your files). But only people who already know the name type it, so this tells you little about growth. Creating means making new owners aware of you through posts, shown work and introductions. The LinkedIn and Instagram plan is a draft, and nothing is recorded as posted.
3
Brand marketing is the slow part of creating: it makes a name feel trusted. For a studio with no client case studies, no testimonials and no client logos on the site, the biggest brand job is proof. Paid ads are not part of this lesson. The Developers plan says no paid ads for the first 90 days, and that point is still marked open, so it is a plan and not a final decision.
4
Do you need this now? Yes. This lesson costs no money, only a few hours and one sheet. Every later choice (which channel, which message, whether to run ads) depends on knowing where your path leaks. Without it you would pick LinkedIn or Google by taste. There is nothing else you should do instead: this is the base.

Before you change anything, here is what is true today, from your files.

AWhat already worksThe form, the database and the counter are in place.

The form on Start a project saves each enquiry to your database (Firestore) and emails both your team and the sender. If the save fails, a pre-filled email draft takes over. The site also has its own counter, which records visits and actions without cookies: view_services, view_work, view_contact, cta_contact, form_start, enquiry_sent, email_click and a click on each service button. A GA4 property (G-ERL2FMV2TG) was added on 20 Oct, but whether the tag and key events are set up cannot be checked from the files.

BWhat is missingNo source, no stages, no client, no proof, no enquiry count.

Each enquiry is saved with a fixed label, developers.haritt.com/contact, and no UTM tag (a small label added to a link that says which post or listing sent the visit). So if an owner found you on LinkedIn, Google or through a friend, the record cannot say. The inbox has three states, New, Contacted and Closed, so you cannot see which enquiries were good. No ideal client is chosen. The only real numbers on file are one screenshot of the 30 days to 13 Oct. How many enquiries came in is not on file.

CThe readiness checkFour questions. Three

Ask yourself: can I say how many enquiries came in over the last 30 days? Can I say where each came from? Do I know how many turned into a call? Is there a written rule for what a good enquiry is? If the answer to the first three is no, starting any channel is premature, because you could not tell whether it worked. Do this instead: spend a week building the tracking sheet in the next part and filling in what you remember. That is real work and it is free.

Made-up example row, only to show the columns. Export your real enquiries from the admin inbox CSV.

A funnel is the path people take towards a goal, and it is called a funnel because each step loses some people. Your admin dashboard already draws the first half for developers.haritt.com: Visitors, then Services or Work, then the Contact page, then Started form, then Sent enquiry. The second half does not exist anywhere. Nothing records that a call happened, that a proposal went out or that you won.

Look at the numbers you do have. For the 30 days to 13 Oct, with your own devices removed, the dashboard showed 301 sessions (visits) and 633 page views, which is about two pages per visit. Be careful with the tile called Visitors, 211 for that period: it adds up the different devices seen each day, so it is not 211 separate people. One person on two days counts twice. And calls that come from Google Business Profile or Justdial never touch your website, so they never enter this funnel at all.

AExtend the funnel in a sheetOne row per enquiry, one column per stage.

Make a Google Sheet with columns: date, name of business, service asked for, source (how they found you), budget band, then date of call, date of proposal, project value, won or lost, and a notes column. This is your funnel after the enquiry. It takes five minutes per enquiry and needs no change to the website.

BLog what happens outside the sitePhone calls and messages from listings count too.

Add a row for every call, message or introduction that comes from Google, Justdial, LinkedIn or a friend, even if no form was sent. Put the source in the source column. This is the only way to see how much of your work comes from places the counter cannot see.

CWrite one definition per stageQualified, call held, proposal, won, lost.

Write one line for each stage and agree it with your co-founder. For example: qualified means the enquiry is for a service you offer and has a real brief. Call held means you actually spoke. Lost means they said no or went silent after a reminder. If the definitions are loose, the numbers will be too.

Five stages for a business that sells projects

Acquisition, activation, revenue, retention and referral, in your words.

AcquisitionAn owner visits
ActivationA real call is held
RevenueA project is signed
RetentionSupport or repeat work
ReferralA client sends another

The five stages growth teams use are usually called AARRR, said like a pirate. They are acquisition, activation, revenue, retention and referral. They are only useful if you put them in your own words, so here they are for a studio. Work on them in the order below, because a later stage is hard to improve when the earlier one is blank.

1Acquisition: an owner visitsMeasured today by visits and enquiry_sent.

Someone lands on the site and looks around. You already count this. The question to answer is which route they came from, and today you cannot answer it because enquiries carry no source.

2Activation: a discovery call is heldDefined here, not recorded yet.

For a service business an enquiry is not yet a customer. The first moment of real value is the first proper conversation. Your files suggest "discovery call held" as the activation event. Record the date in the sheet.

3Revenue: a project is signedNeeds value and won or lost.

Add the project value and whether it was won or lost for every enquiry. If you can only add one thing this month, add this. It is the only stage that pays the bills.

4Retention: support or repeat workLater, once there are clients.

You already offer Consulting and Support as a service. There are no client records yet, so this stage has nothing to measure. Revisit it after your first finished project.

5Referral: a client sends anotherAsk, then count.

Referrals do not happen by themselves. Near the end of each project, ask for one introduction and write down the answer. Count introductions in the sheet.

Open your admin, go to Analytics and pick the developers.haritt.com tab. Menu names can change, so follow the idea if a label moves. Set the range to 30 days and choose Real visitors only (without me). Then read from the top, one number at a time. The first screen uses the real figures from 13 Oct. The second uses made-up numbers to show how the funnel reads.

1Sessions and page viewsVisits and pages.

Sessions are visits: 301 in the 30 days to 13 Oct. Page views are pages opened: 633, about two per visit. A rise in sessions means more people arrive, not that they are right. A fall with no change from you points to a listing or ranking change. Action: look at Where sessions come from for the day it moved.

2Engaged sessions and engaged timeDid they stay and look?

An engaged session is one where the page stayed visible for 10 seconds, or two pages were opened, or an action happened. You had 70.1 percent and 38 seconds on average. That looks healthy, but daily visitors ran from roughly 2 to 15, so one good day swings every percentage. A sharp fall in one week points to a broken page or a source sending the wrong people. Action: judge by weeks, then test the page on a phone.

3The funnelWhere visitors drop.

Compare each step with the one before it. Few reach the Contact page: the path or message is unclear. Contact seen but form not started: trust or proof is missing. Started but not sent: the form is too long. Action: fix only the biggest drop, one change at a time.

4Channels and conversionsWho brought the enquiries.

The channel table shows sessions and enquiries by channel, and Attribution switches between last touch and first touch. Direct hides WhatsApp and Instagram visits that carried no tag. Many sessions but no enquiries from a channel means the wrong audience or message. Action: tag your links, then compare again.

5Enquiry rate and form completionTwo rates at the bottom.

Enquiry rate is enquiries per visitor day. Form completion is sent divided by started. Neither is on file yet, so write down the first real values. With so few visitors, one enquiry more or less changes both a lot.

One warning. The 13 Oct screenshot shows Returning, weekly users and monthly users at zero. Yet 211 visitor days minus 151 new devices leaves 60 that were not new, so returning cannot be zero. This looks like a counting fault, so do not quote those tiles until the cause is found. Your GA4 property runs beside this counter and the two are not reconciled, so their numbers will differ. Use the own counter as your record for now.

Choosing one number, and finding the leak

The number to judge growth by, and a way to find where the path loses people.

Where is the path leaking?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyNot enough people arriving

Read Where sessions come from. This is a channel problem (lesson d03).

Most likelyMessage or offer unclear

Compare Services and Work views with Contact views and the service buttons.

Most likelyTrust or page problem

No proof and no price signal are on the site. Test those first.

Most likelyForm too long or worrying

Check form completion. Eight fields plus a consent tick is a lot on a phone.

Most likelyWrong people or slow reply

Check budget bands and how fast you replied.

Most likelyOffer, price or fit

Read proposal notes. Ask lost leads why.

1
Your files have no North Star yet. That is the one number that best shows the business is really growing. My reading, and a draft for you to confirm, is qualified project conversations per month: an enquiry for a service you offer, with a real brief, that leads to a call. Counting enquiries alone is not enough, because it counts every enquiry, good or not. The inputs that feed it are enquiries sent, the share with a budget band chosen, replies within one working day, calls held and proposals sent. The form already collects budget band and timeline.
2
When the number looks low, do not guess. Walk the path from the bottom up and find the biggest drop between two steps. Tap the symptoms below. Change one thing at a time, for example only the form, then wait until you have enough enquiries to compare. With a few visitors a day that means weeks, not days. Write down what you believed, what you changed and what happened, and keep that log.

What to do first, and when to look again

The first jobs, and the moments to come back to this lesson.

Write definitionsQualified, call, won
Build the sheetOne row per enquiry
Fill the last 30 daysFrom memory and inbox
Review each weekFind the biggest drop
1
Start with the cheapest actions. First write the definitions and agree the North Star with your co-founder. Second, build the sheet and export your current enquiries from the admin inbox (it has a CSV export) to fill the first columns. Third, fill in what you remember about calls and sources for the last month. Fourth, put a short weekly review in the calendar: open the dashboard, open the sheet, and write down the one step that loses the most people.
2
A small habit makes the sheet work: update it the same day an enquiry arrives or a call ends, while you still remember the details. A sheet filled in once a month from memory is mostly guesses. Also keep the source column honest. If you do not know where someone came from, ask them in your first reply: "How did you find us?" It is a normal question and it costs nothing, and it fills the gap that the form leaves today.
3
Come back to this lesson at three moments: after you have one full month of sheet data, after your first project is delivered, and before you decide to spend any money on ads. Until then, do not add new platforms. If the sheet shows that enquiries come but calls do not, the next lesson on ideal client and offer matters more than any channel. If few people arrive, go to the lesson on choosing where to spend your effort.
