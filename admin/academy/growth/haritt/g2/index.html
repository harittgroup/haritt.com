1
Credibility means that a stranger who lands on haritt.com believes the company is real, capable and worth a conversation. For a company with a product, the product page does much of that work. Haritt Group has no product on its page, so the page itself has to carry the belief. A client considering Haritt Developers, a partner thinking about a link, or a student wondering who is behind HV Verse may all open haritt.com first, to check that the company exists before they do anything else.
2
Your files show where this stands. The site shows no team, because the leadership page was deleted under your rule of no personal names on public pages. It shows no numbers from real use, no press and no client logos. The strip on the home page says 1 product live, 14 actions HV AI can take, 3 languages and 3 apps. Those are your own claims, and they are fine as claims, but they are not proof. A claim becomes proof when a visitor can check it. And one fact is working against you: the founding year appears as 2026 on About, 2020 in your own story and 2018 on a Justdial listing.
3
PR in the old sense, meaning press coverage, is not realistic for a company this young, so this lesson is mostly about proof you control: your own work, shown honestly, and the same story told everywhere.
Do you need this now? Yes. This is the biggest gap on haritt.com, it costs nothing but care, and it makes every later step (partners, hiring, client enquiries) easier. It comes before any outreach.

Because you chose not to show people, the proof has to come from the work. You do have real work: the HV Verse apps went live on 1 October 2026, and Haritt Developers builds websites and apps for small businesses. What you do not have, today, is a page that lets a visitor look at that work and verify it. Before you build anything, go through the checks below.

ACheck each claim you already makeIs every number on the home page true today?

Open the home page strip and test each claim against the live products. Is it really one product live, three apps, three languages? Does HV AI really offer 14 actions? If a number has changed, update it the same day. Counts that were true when written and later became wrong are worse than no count, because a sharp visitor will notice.

Ready when: you can point to where each claim can be seen, in the product or on a public page.

BChoose the founding lineOne story, used everywhere.

The conflict between 2026, 2020 and 2018 is public, and it is easy for a client or a search engine to spot. Choose one honest line. For example, if the company began informally earlier and was relaunched in 2026, you can say exactly that in one sentence. This is your decision, because only you know what is true. Whatever you pick, use it on About, on Justdial and in every profile bio.

Ready when: the same sentence appears in all three places.

CDecide what you may showThe no-names rule, and client approval.

Your public rule is no personal names. Client work can only be shown with the client's written approval, which your Developers site already promises. So the proof you may show freely is your own: the apps you built, their screens, launch dates and honest counts. Write that down as the rule for what goes on the Group page.

Ready when: the rule is written in one place that anyone adding content can read.

Also write down one thing you will not do: you will not invent a client, a number, a review or a press mention to fill the gap. Visitors can usually tell, and one found invention damages every true claim next to it. If you cannot do check B, stop and do not add more proof yet. Adding screens to a page whose founding story contradicts itself makes the contradiction easier to notice.

The page does not need to look bigger than the company is. A small, honest, checkable page persuades more than a large one with claims nobody can test. Here is how each kind of proof works for Haritt Group.

SShow the work itselfReal screens and a link to the live product.

For each venture, add a short block on haritt.com with a real screenshot, one sentence about what it does, the launch date, and a link to the live site. A visitor can click through and see that it exists. This also fits your "Try HV Verse" button, because the page then explains what they will find on the other side. Keep HV Verse text on the Group page about HV Verse as a product, and follow its own brand rule of only using its own name in the apps.

PPut a date and a source beside every numberSo a number is checkable.

If you write "3 apps", add "checked on" and the date, or link the number to the page where the apps are listed. If you cannot link it, think about whether the number belongs on the page. Never round up. A smaller number you can defend is better than a larger one you cannot.

BBe clear about who is responsibleA company without a founder photo can still be accountable.

Without names, accountability comes from other signals: a working contact address shown as plain text (not only a link that may fail to open an email app), a response promise (the page already says replies come within two working days), a privacy notice that matches what the site does, and a stable company name. These are small but they tell a visitor that someone will answer.

HHow to build the block, step by stepA small piece of work, done once.

Take one venture at a time. Open the live product on a phone and take two clean screenshots of the screens that explain it best. Write one plain sentence beneath them saying what the product does and who it is for. Add the launch date and the link. Then ask someone who has never seen it to read the block and tell you what it is. If they hesitate, shorten the sentence. When both blocks are done, check that the Products page and the home page do not repeat each other in different words, since two versions of one claim invite doubt.

CClient proof, when it existsWritten approval first.

When Haritt Developers finishes a client project, ask the client for permission to show a short case study. Ask in writing, say exactly what you will show, and keep the reply. Until then, the Group page should not imply clients that do not exist.

How the ventures prove themselves

Each venture earns trust in its own way, and the Group borrows it.

Venture does real workApps live, builds delivered
It is shown honestlyScreens, dates, approvals
Others repeat itProfiles, links, mentions
Group page links to itVisitors can verify
1
The Group page should not try to make up trust of its own. It should collect what the ventures have earned and point at it. HV Verse earns trust by being a free product people use. Haritt Developers earns it by delivering sites and apps for small businesses and, with approval, showing them. When either venture has something worth showing, the Group page links to it with a short line and the date.
2
Think of it as a chain. Nothing on the Group page is a first-hand source of truth. The apps are the source, and the page is a signpost with a date on it. That is why the page needs to be updated whenever a venture changes: a signpost pointing to an old screen or an old count quietly undoes the trust it was built to create. Make updating the Group page part of finishing any venture release, the same way you would update the changelog on your admin page.
3
There is also a plain, free kind of PR that fits: being listed accurately in places that journalists, clients and partners look. That is the profile and directory work from the first Group lesson. A LinkedIn company page that matches haritt.com, a directory entry with the same one-line description, and a Developers listing that says it belongs to Haritt Group all add up to the impression of a real, consistent company. Press releases and paid mentions are not needed. If someone writes about the apps, save the link, and add it to the page with the date.
4
A worked example, with made-up numbers. Say a month after adding the venture blocks, 400 people opened the home page, 120 opened Products, 14 opened Contact and 5 clicked the email. Nothing says those are good or bad. What matters is the next month: if you add one real screen block and the Contact figure moves from 14 to 25 while Products stays near 120, that is a sign proof helped the later step. If nothing moves, the problem is probably further up, in who is arriving.
5
Playbooks, the Group's article section, is another source of proof, because a clear and useful article shows that the people behind the company know their subject. There is one published article now (about organising a job search) and five drafts. Publish them only if each fits the Group's job, and link each article to the relevant venture.

1
A short reminder of how to read the analytics here. GA4 and your own counter will not always agree, because the counter is built to be simple and GA4 respects the privacy signals visitors send. Do not try to make them match exactly. Pick one as the main source for monthly comparisons, note which, and use the other as a sanity check: if one doubles and the other does not move, something is broken in the tracking, not in the page.
2
There is no sign-up on haritt.com, so success here is softer. Look at three things in GA4 (your analytics) and your own counter: how many visitors open the venture pages and the Contact page, how many click the email, and how many say something like "I saw your site" when they write. The dashboard funnel already has the steps Visitors, Products page, Contact page and Clicked an email. Use it as your measure.
3
The best test costs nothing. Ask five people who do not know Haritt to open haritt.com on their phones for ten seconds, then close it and tell you what the company does, whether they would trust it, and what they would click next. If three or more cannot say what the company is, the page message is the problem. If they understand but hesitate to trust, the evidence is the problem.
4
Write the result of each test in a plain learning log: the date, what you changed, what you expected, and what happened. After a few entries you will see which kind of proof moves people on your site and which kind is only decoration. This log is also what you show a partner later, when they ask how the company works.
5
To fix, change one block at a time. Add real screens first, then correct the number claims, then the founding line, and rerun the five-person test with five new people. If your analytics figures are tiny, do not rely on percentages; rely on the test and on actual replies in the inbox.
When to revisit: every time a venture ships something new, or a client gives approval. Check every number on the page once a month, since a stale count is a credibility problem.
