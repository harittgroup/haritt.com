1
Think of two people at the shop door. One keeps a tally on paper: how many came in, how many asked for a quote. The other also notes how each person arrived, whether they walked, drove or were sent by a neighbour. Your site already has the first person: a first-party counter that records visits on your own domain and events like view_services, cta_contact, form_start and enquiry_sent, without outside tools. GA4 (Google Analytics 4, Google's free analytics) is the second person.
2
GA4 matters for three reasons. It sorts visits into channels such as Organic Search, Direct, Referral and Organic Social. It connects to Google Search Console, so you can see which searches lead to which pages. And it feeds Looker Studio, the free report builder the next lesson uses. GA4 is event based: every action, from a page view to a click, is stored as an event with extra details. It is not perfect. Ad blockers and declined consent hide some visits, and small sites can have data hidden by thresholds, so its totals will not match your counter exactly. Compare trends, not exact totals.
3
One thing about your own situation. The changelog says a GA4 property with ID G-ERL2FMV2TG was added on 20 Oct. Whether the tag is on the live pages, whether any key events exist and whether enquiry_sent reaches GA4 cannot be checked from your files. So this lesson treats GA4 as installed but unproven, and shows you how to prove it.
Do you need this now? Yes. The property already exists, so the cost is a few hours of checking, not new software. With about 10 sessions a day, every week without correct tracking is a week of lost history, and you cannot get it back. You also have no Search Console data and no source on any lead, so GA4 plus tagged links are the cheapest way to start learning which channel brings real enquiries.

Starting with the tool is how tracking goes wrong. A measurement plan is a small table that goes from a business goal, to the number that shows progress (the KPI), to the exact events that record it. For Haritt Developers the goal is more qualified project enquiries, the KPI is the enquiry conversion rate, and the main event is enquiry_sent. Your counter already uses lowercase names with underscores, which is the right style. Keep it for GA4 so the two tools speak the same language.

The plan has five columns: the event name written exactly, when it fires, the decision it supports, the owner, and whether it has been tested. Keep it in a Google Sheet. Add an event only when a decision needs it. Today the useful ones are cta_contact (someone clicked toward the form), form_start (they began typing), enquiry_sent (the lead) and email_click (they chose email). Phone and WhatsApp taps and anything after the enquiry are not tracked, and there is no point adding them until those routes exist.

1Check the privacy noticeThe files show a contradiction.

The 19 Oct Developers privacy text says "no cookies and no third-party analytics". GA4 uses cookies and is third party. Whether the text was updated live is not on file. Read the live notice, and rewrite it to say what the site really uses, for what purpose, and how to opt out. Under India's DPDP Act, the notice and consent are your responsibility, so check current official guidance, and take advice for anything unclear.

2Decide on consent and Google signalsTwo settings, two decisions.

On haritt.com, GA4 loads with analytics storage granted by default and ad signals off by design. For Developers, the choice is yours. My reading: keep Google signals off, because you run no ads and signals add cross-device data you do not need. Decide about a cookie banner separately. A banner is stricter, and it will hide some visitors, so with small numbers that cost is real.

3Agree names for tagged linksDo it before you create the first link.

Lowercase, hyphens, no spaces, no personal data. A short fixed list for medium. Write it in the same sheet as the plan. the UTM part below shows the list.

In GA4 an account holds properties, and a property holds data streams. A data stream is one source of data, such as your website. The stream gives you a Measurement ID that begins with G-, and the site's tag must carry that same ID. Menu names change, so use the paths below as a guide and look for the nearest match.

1Check the streamOpen the stream and match the ID.

Open GA4, click Admin (the gear at the bottom left), pick the Haritt Developers property, then open Data streams and click your web stream. Confirm the website address is developers.haritt.com and the Measurement ID is the one in your files. If the tag was added to the site by another person, ask which pages carry it. A tag missing from just the contact page would break the most important number.

2Open Realtime and visit your own siteProof that the tag fires.

In the left menu choose Reports, then Realtime. On a phone with mobile data (not your office network), open the live site and click around. Within a minute you should see a user, the pages you viewed, and events like page_view. If you see nothing, the tag is not firing, or you are using an ad blocker, or the tag has the wrong ID.

3Use DebugView for the exact eventThe detailed check.

DebugView (under Admin, in the display area of the property, or in the left menu) shows each event in order, with its details, for a device in debug mode. You can switch debug mode on with Google's Tag Assistant or the GA Debugger browser extension. Send a test enquiry with a name like Test Please Ignore and watch for enquiry_sent. If it appears, the link between the site and GA4 works. If it does not, the site sends the event to your counter only, and the website thread needs to add a GA4 event call after a successful save. Ask for it with the exact event name.

4Mark enquiry_sent as a key eventThe one event that matters.

A key event (once called a conversion) is an event you have chosen as valuable. After enquiry_sent has appeared at least once, go to Admin, then Events (or Key events), and switch on Mark as key event next to it. If it is not in the list yet, use the New key event button and type the name exactly. Keep to this one for now. Marking many events as key makes every conversion report noisy.

5Keep yourself out of the dataYour own visits are not customers.

In the stream's tag settings, look for Define internal traffic and add your IP address or range, then create the matching data filter in the property settings and switch it from Testing to Active once it shows correct. Home and mobile IPs change, so your own counter's owner exclusion remains the most dependable record.

UTM tags: label every link you control

Without tags, LinkedIn, WhatsApp and email visits fall into Direct and you learn nothing.

Your page URLAdd source, medium, campaign
Visit labelledChannel in reportLead tied to channel
1
A UTM tag (short for Urchin Tracking Module) is a label added to the end of a link. It tells analytics where the click came from. Without it, visits from a LinkedIn post, a WhatsApp message or an email signature often show as Direct, because the app does not pass the origin on. Your own counter already reads utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content. But the enquiry record saves a fixed text instead, so no lead can yet be tied to a channel. Tagged links fix half of that. A hidden-field change to the form, asked for in the website thread, fixes the rest.
2
The five parts are source (who sent it), medium (the type of route), campaign (the initiative), and the optional term and content. Keep names lowercase, with hyphens, and never put a person's name, email or phone in a tag. Never tag links between pages of your own site, because that breaks the original source. Your admin Links tab already has a link maker for LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp and email, so use that rather than typing by hand. Google's Campaign URL Builder also works.

Check it every week: the habit that keeps the data honest

A five minute check stops a broken tag from costing you a month of data.

LookRealtime and last 7 days
CompareGA4 vs own counter
Countenquiries vs inbox
Fixask website thread
Notelog the change

Tracking is not finished once it works. It breaks quietly: a page is edited and the tag vanishes, a form is changed and the event name differs, a new link is posted without a tag. The way to catch this is a small weekly routine, done on the same day, written in the plan sheet.

Open Reports, then Realtime, and visit the site yourself to confirm the tag is alive. Open the last 7 days and compare sessions with your own counter's sessions. They will not match exactly, since GA4 loses visits to blockers and consent, but they should move together. If GA4 shows far fewer than your counter, or a week with zero, something broke. Then compare the number of enquiry_sent events with the number of real enquiries in the inbox. They should match within one or two. A bigger gap means a missing event, a double count or the mailto fallback being counted wrongly.

If a tagged link shows up as Direct, the tag was lost somewhere, often when an app or a redirect strips it. Test every new link by clicking it yourself in a private window and checking Realtime. Record each change you made to tracking, with the date, in a short log. When the numbers look strange a month later, that log is what tells you why.

WWhat GA4 cannot do for youKnow its limits so you do not trust numbers too far.

GA4 will not tell you which leads were good, who won a project or how long a deal took. It stops at the enquiry. The inbox statuses New, Contacted and Closed have no qualified, proposal or won stage, so add those in your own sheet. GA4 also shows nothing about calls from Google or Justdial listings. For those, ask every enquirer how they found you, and write down the answer.

1
Most tracking problems fall into a small number of patterns, and each has a quick test. Use the examples above, and follow the same order every time: first confirm the tag, then confirm the event, then confirm the label, then compare with your own counter and your inbox.
2
When you find a problem, fix one thing, then test it the same way you found it. For a missing event, that means a test enquiry in DebugView. For a lost label, that means clicking the tagged link in a private window and watching Realtime. Write the result in the log. If a fix needs a change to the site, such as an event call, hidden form fields or a privacy text edit, write the request in plain words with the exact event names, and ask for it in the website thread. Do not edit the live site yourself without agreeing the change.
3
If after all this the numbers still do not add up, do not stop using the data. Use the weaker but steady signal: your own counter and the inbox for counts, GA4 for channel mix and trend. Together with the question "How did you hear about us?", they are enough to make good decisions at your size. Revisit consent and Google signals when you plan paid ads or retargeting, since both need a new privacy and consent decision.
