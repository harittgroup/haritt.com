Two moments that decide your growth: the first reply and the aftercare

A fast, useful first reply turns an enquiry into a conversation. Good aftercare brings repeat work and introductions.

After the saleKeeping the client

1
Marketing people talk about activation and retention. In an app, activation is the first time a user gets value. For Haritt Developers there is no app, so the idea needs translating. Activation here is the moment an enquiry becomes a real conversation: the first useful reply, and the first call. Retention, in an app, is people coming back. For you it means a client who buys more work, pays for support, or sends you someone else. Both moments are cheap to improve and both cost almost nothing.
2
Today, when someone sends the form, two automatic emails go out. The sender gets an enquiry received message (once per 6 hours per address, at most 30 an hour), and your team gets a new enquiry alert. In the admin inbox you can reply and mark the lead as Contacted. What is missing, from your files: a target for how fast you reply, a booking link, a record of calls held, and a standard first reply beyond the manual welcome email. Also missing: any client list, any repeat-work record, any support plan on file.
3
This matters more than it seems. A small business owner who sends a form often sends it to two or three studios on the same day. The one who replies first, clearly and kindly, usually gets the call.
Do you need this now? The first reply, yes, this week. It is free and you control it. Aftercare you can set up in a simple way now, and it will only pay off once you have clients. Do not wait for the first client to write the sheet and the offer, because the first delivery is the best time to ask for a quote and an introduction.

Four things stand between a lead and a good first conversation.

The two alert emails already work, which is a solid base. The rest is about habits and small pieces of text. Check each one below.

AA reply-time ruleOne working day is a fair start.

Agree with your co-founder how fast you answer. A plain rule is one working day. How to check: look at your last few enquiries and count the days between the enquiry and your first human reply. Not on file: those dates. If you cannot find them, start recording them in the pipeline sheet from now.

BAn owner for the replySo it is never

The team has two founders and no set roles. Pick one person to send the first reply and the same person to update the sheet. Name a back-up for days they are away.

CA first-reply templateThree questions and a call link.

Write one reply you can adapt in two minutes. It should thank them, repeat in one line what you understood, ask three short questions (what they want to achieve, what exists today, by when), and offer a call with a booking link. Keep it plain and warm, with the tone of your site.

DA booking linkLet them choose a time without back and forth.

A booking link is a web address that shows your free times and lets the person pick one. Free scheduling tools exist, and some calendars include one. Check that the tool you pick is free for your needs today, and that it sends a confirmation. Put the link in the template and in the thank-you email.

How to tell you are ready. Do a dry run. Send a test enquiry to yourself through the form, and watch what happens: the automatic email, the team alert, your reply, the booking link, the note in the inbox, the row in the sheet. If any step is awkward or takes more than a few minutes, fix it before a real lead arrives. If you are not ready, the safe action is still to reply within one working day with a plain message, because a quick, human answer is already a strong start.

Open the admin, go to the enquiries page, and choose the newest enquiry. Menu names can change, so look for the page that lists project enquiries. Read the whole form before you reply, especially the project text, the budget band and the timeline. A reply that shows you read it is worth more than a polished one that does not.

RReply wellShort, specific, one ask.

Start with one sentence that shows you understood their need, using their own words. Then ask your three questions and offer the booking link. Avoid listing all seven services or sending a long brochure. If the budget band looks very low for the request, do not argue about it in the first reply. You can discuss scope on the call, or offer a smaller starting scope, which your FAQ already mentions.

RRecord itMake the speed visible.

After you reply, mark the enquiry as Contacted, write the date in the notes, and update the stage in your pipeline sheet. When a call is held, write the date and the outcome. This is your only record of speed, since the system keeps no call or reply time of its own. Leads that come through the email fallback, Justdial or direct email are not in the inbox, so type them into the sheet.

HHow to read these screensWhat the dates and shares mean and what to do.

Days to reply and share answered in time. Any enquiry above your rule is a visible fault. A low share means the habit breaks on busy weeks, so name one owner and a backup. Late replies explain more lost leads than almost anything, because the owner may have written to two or three studios on the same day.

Status in the inbox. Rows left on New past your rule are unanswered. Rows on Contacted for weeks with no call date in the note have stalled, so follow up once and then close.

Budget and timeline columns. Use them to shape the reply. A very low band for a large request is not an argument for the first message. Offer a smaller starting scope on the call. A short timeline is a reason to reply faster.

When you win: onboarding the client

A clear start prevents most later disagreements.

Written scopePrice and datesWho approvesAccess needed
Regular short updateShow progress earlyLog changes
Walk throughAsk for a quoteOffer support

Onboarding means getting a new client started well. You have no client yet on file, so this section is a plan, not a record of what you do. It is written so you can use it on the first project and adjust it afterwards.

AAgree the scope in writingWhat is included, what is not.

Before work begins, send a short written scope: what you will build, what you will not, the price, the timeline and who approves what. Unclear scope is the usual root of unhappy clients, and writing it down costs an hour. Keep the signed or confirmed version in your client record.

BSet the rhythm of updatesSo the client is never left guessing.

Agree how often you will update them and how. A short message every week, saying what is done and what is next, is enough for most small businesses. Show something early, even if it is rough, because early feedback is cheaper than late changes.

CAsk at the right momentProof and introductions.

At handover, when the client is happiest, ask three things: may we use a short quote, may we write a case study with your approval, and do you know anyone else who needs this. The site already promises that case studies are published only with approval, so ask in writing and keep the answer.

When the client asks for more. Mid-project requests are normal. Do not say yes or no on the spot. Write the request down, say what it adds in time and cost, and let the client choose. Record the change in your client sheet. Done calmly, this protects your margin and the relationship together.

1
Retention for a studio is quiet work. Start with a client record sheet: one row per client, with the project, the delivery date, the support or repeat work that followed, and whether they referred anyone. Fill it with every project done so far, and add each new one the day it ends. Without it, you cannot tell which clients are likely to come back, and you will forget to follow up.
2
A care plan is a simple offer of ongoing support after launch. Consulting and Support is already one of your seven services, so this is a way to package it. Describe what is included, how the client reaches you, and the price, after you decide it. Do not set a price from this lesson, since none is on file.
3
For referrals, there is no mechanism today. Draft a two-line ask: thank them, and ask whether they know anyone who needs a similar site. List five clients or partners to ask. Give partners a tagged link so any visit or enquiry shows as a referral in your analytics, and count the enquiries that say referred by. Keep any reward free at first, for example introducing clients back to them. A paid referral fee needs your approval.
4
How to read the client sheet. Count by name. If delivered clients have no support plan and no repeat work, you did not offer it at handover. If clients are happy but nobody referred anyone, you did not ask. A blank in a column is a to do, not a zero. Compare each quarter with the last, and write what you will ask next time.

Read the first two numbers together. If you reply fast but few leads reach a call, the reply or the booking step is the problem. If you reply slowly and few reach a call, speed is the first thing to fix, since it is free. The made-up chart above is only a picture of this: eight in ten answered in time, four in ten reaching a call. Your own numbers will be different and will move a lot while the count of enquiries is small.
2
A warning about the admin dashboard. It shows visitor retention (people who return to the site after 1, 7 and 30 days). For a lead site this is not meaningful, because a business owner does not come back daily to a services page. Its zeros look like a counting problem. Ignore that panel for this purpose and track retention in your client sheet instead.

When leads go quiet or clients leave

Find whether the break is speed, clarity, fit or delivery.

What do your numbers show?

1
When the follow-up part breaks, the cause is usually small and found by looking at dates. Work through it in this order. First, check your own records: is the date of the first reply written, and was it inside your rule? Late replies explain more lost leads than almost anything. Second, read the reply you sent. If it is long, generic or full of questions, shorten it and test the new version on the next ten leads. Third, check friction: click your own booking link on a phone and see if it works.
2
If the leak is after the sale, ask the client plainly what would have helped. A short, kind message after delivery, such as "What worked and what could we do better?", gives you facts no dashboard can.
3
Retest. Change one thing, such as the reply template, for the next ten to fifteen leads. Compare the share that reach a call with the earlier group. Keep it if it improved and write what you learned in your notes. If not, try the next cause.
