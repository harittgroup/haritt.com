Why you pick channels on purpose

One route that works beats five half-tried. Choose two, test, then decide.

Spread outEvery platform a little

Many posts, no clear learning, tired by week three.

vs
ChosenTwo channels, tested

One number each, a stop point, a clear winner.

1
A channel is simply a route to your audience: Instagram, a WhatsApp group, a college poster, a search result, an email. Marketing writers count about nineteen of them. Think of a bakery that could hand out flyers, post on Instagram, sponsor a college event or wait for walk-ins. It could try all four, but a small shop with one pair of hands would end up doing each badly. The sensible move is to choose the one or two that fit its customers and its time, run a cheap test on each, and keep what works.
2
HV Verse is in the same position, with an even smaller team. Your social plan already lists Instagram as primary, YouTube and LinkedIn as essential, X as light, Facebook on hold, plus WhatsApp and college groups through the Student Beta and the welcome emails inside the apps. That is seven routes, all unpaid. The plan also expects about 30 minutes a day of outreach (comments and answers in communities) and one collaboration a month. The question this lesson answers is which of these routes should get your first real effort, and how you will know.
Do you need this now? Yes. The Student Beta is live, you have limited time, and no file shows which route brings students who actually use an app. A short, deliberate test of two channels now will save months of scattered posting. It costs only your time.

A channel decision also rests on money and speed. Paid ads are fast but you have chosen not to use them for now. Search takes months to build. Earned routes like press and directory listings are uneven. Owned routes like email and your WhatsApp contacts are slow to grow but they belong to you. Free apps rarely sell by phone and rarely suit expensive channels, which is why your plan leans on social, groups and sharing inside the apps.

A channel test is only fair when you can see what each channel brought, and what those people did next. Four things need to be in place before you start. For each, here is how to check and where HV Verse stands.

AA clear first group and promiseWithout this, every channel carries a different message.

How to check: write the sentence from the previous lesson. If you cannot say who it is for in one line, stop here and finish that first.

HV Verse today: two age groups are named in the draft plan but no first group is chosen. Do that first, because a channel is only a route to a person.

BTagged links on everythingSo visits are named, not just counted.

A UTM tag is a small label added to the end of a web address, such as a source (where the link was posted) and a medium (how). When someone clicks, your analytics reads the label and files the visit under that source. Without it, a visit from an Instagram bio often appears as Direct, which tells you nothing.

How to check: open your link maker, make one link, click it and see whether the visit appears under the right channel in your dashboard.

HV Verse today: eight short links (for WhatsApp, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, X, Facebook, email and QR) already tag sources. The Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, X and Facebook short links still land on the home page, not on the profiles, so decide where each should point.

COne meaning of successA visit is not a win. A first use is.

How to check: ask what one student must do for you to call a channel good. For HV Reset it could be finishing a first task. For HV Hunt it could be adding a first job. For HV Assess it could be completing the test.

HV Verse today: the apps already record these first actions, but you have not agreed which counts as your target. Pick one per app and write it down.

DTime and a stop pointEvery test needs a date and a number.

How to check: before you start, write one line: "I will post or share on this channel for four weeks, and I will keep it if it brings at least N activated students." Choose N yourself, based on how many students you want per week.

HV Verse today: no plan has stop points yet. Add them, or tests will drift forever.

If B and C are still missing, do those two before choosing channels. Posting without them means you will not learn from the posts.

1
The bullseye method works like a dartboard. First write down one idea for every channel, even silly ones. Then place the channels in rings: the centre for the few you believe will work best, the middle ring for ones worth trying, the outer ring for the rest. Finally, test only the centre. Writers who popularised it also suggest spending about half your effort on the product and half on getting it to customers, which is a useful reminder when the apps still need work.

HHow to score a channel yourselfThree questions, one number each.

For each channel, answer three questions out of ten. How many of your chosen students can this reach? What would one activated student cost you in time or money? Can you start within two weeks? Add the numbers. Pick the top two, and prefer channels you can finish a test on within four weeks. Do not put Facebook in the first round, since your plan already holds it.

PPick complements, not twinsOne channel to reach, one to convert.

A good pair is one channel that spreads awareness and one that sits close to action. Instagram reels reach people who have not heard of you. A WhatsApp group message gives a direct link to the beta page to people who already trust the sender. Test them together so the two numbers can be compared.

Here is a simple routine that uses tools you already own. Menu names can change, so look for the nearest label if something moved.

1Write the test cardHalf a page, written before you post.

Channel name, who it targets, what you will post and how often, the one link you will use, the number you will watch, the date you will decide. Example: "Instagram, college students, three carousels a week, link /ig, I watch activated students from Instagram, decide on 14 November." Dates here are only an example.

2Make the tagged linkOne link per place, one campaign name.

Open your link maker and choose the destination, such as the beta page. Set the source (instagram), the medium (social or bio) and a campaign name (for example, studentbeta). Copy the full link. Use a different utm_content value for each poster, post or group, so two posters can be compared. A QR code should carry its own tag.

3Post or share, and keep a logCount what you did, not just what you got.

Keep a simple sheet: date, channel, what you posted, who you messaged. After a week you can see whether you did the work you planned. If you posted only half as often, a poor result says nothing about the channel.

4Read it weekly in the dashboard and in GA4Same time each week, same two numbers.

In your admin, open Analytics, choose the HV Verse tab, set the last 7 days, tick Real visitors only, and read the Channels panel. In GA4 (the Google tool added on 20 October), open Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition, and look at sessions by source and medium. The two tools count differently, so use them to compare shape, not exact totals.

Run each test for the length you wrote down. Short tests mislead, because a post can have a lucky day or an unlucky week.

Read the results and spot a loop

Compare activated students per channel, and look for what feeds itself.

Finish the testStudent gets a result
Share the scorecardLink or QR is tagged
Friend visitsCounted as Shared by users
Friend takes the testNew completion
New shareThe loop turns again

When the test period ends, resist the urge to read followers or likes. Read one table with one row per channel: visits, first actions (activated students), and the effort you spent. A channel that brought 200 visits but two first actions is weaker than one that brought 40 visits and ten first actions. Divide first actions by visits to get a rate, and compare rates before comparing totals. Add your time: if a channel took ten hours a week and brought two students, that is a cost even though no money moved.

Look at the shape over the weeks, too. A channel that grows slowly but steadily deserves more time than a spike from one forward. If numbers are small, say fewer than twenty visits, treat the result as a hint and extend the test.

FFunnels versus loopsA funnel needs refilling. A loop refills itself.

A funnel is a tank you refill daily with posts. A growth loop is a path where what one round produces feeds the next. Your clearest loop is the HV Assess scorecard: someone finishes the test, gets a scorecard with a unique ID, QR code and PDF, shares it, and a friend takes the test. Today that loop is only half built, since shared visits count only when the link carries a tag like utm_source=scorecard. Tag it, and the Channels panel will show visits under Shared by users.

WWhat to measure in a loopConversion at each step and time per round.

Count how many finishers share, how many friends visit, how many take the test, and how long one round takes. Improve the weakest step first. There is no invite step yet in HV Reset or HV Hunt, so those apps do not have a loop. That is a later project, covered when you work on referrals.

When a channel does not work

Check the audience, the message, the link, the page and the product, in that order.

What do your numbers show?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyReach or hook problem

Check views per post. If views are low, change the first line or image. If views are fine but clicks are rare, the call to action is weak.

Most likelyPage, promise or product

Open the landing page on a phone as a new student. Does the first screen match what the post promised?

Most likelyMissing tags

A link without a tag hides the source. Add tags and rerun for two weeks.

Most likelyOne-off spike

Look at what that week had: a forward, a festival, an exam break. Do not copy it blindly.

Most likelyGroup or offer problem

Go back to the earlier lesson. Re-check the promise with five students.

When a channel does not work, you first need to know why. A weak result can come from the audience (wrong people), the message (they do not get it), the link (visits are not recorded properly), the page (they land and leave), or the product (they use it once and vanish). Each has a different fix, so check them in order. The cheapest check is the link, then the page, then the message.

1Check the tracking firstIs the result real?

Click your own tagged link from a phone. Does it appear in the Channels panel within a short time? If not, fix tagging before judging anything.

2Check the page nextDo students understand it in five seconds?

Open the destination on a phone and read only the first screen. Ask a friend what they think HV Verse is. Fix confusing headlines before changing the channel.

3Then the message and the audienceIs the hook or the group wrong?

Change one thing at a time. A different first line, then a different group, then a different format. Run each variant for the same length of time and compare first actions per visit.

4Decide: scale, change or stopUse the stop point you wrote.

If the channel meets your number, give it more time. If it nearly meets it, change one thing and retest once. If it misses twice, stop and move to the next ring. Write what you learned so you do not repeat the idea.

Not needed now: pipeline and outbound

Two ideas from B2B selling. Here is why you can skip them, and what to do instead.

Pipeline and outboundFor selling to businesses

Deals, proposals, cold emails, case studies.

vs
HV Verse todayFree apps for students

No sale, no deal stages, no buyers to chase.

Your channel list could also include a pipeline (a path from first contact to a signed deal) and outbound (starting the conversation yourself by cold email or call). Both come from B2B selling. For free student apps, neither belongs in your first round. Here is the short reason for each, and what to do instead.

APipelineNot needed now: nobody is buying.

A pipeline sorts open deals by stage, such as lead, call and proposal. HV Verse has no deals. The closest thing is the Signups page, which already moves Student Beta applicants through new, contacted, joined and closed. Use that weekly and stop there. If you start pitching institutes, a simple sheet with one row per institute and a next step is enough.

BOutboundNot needed now: it suits businesses, not students.

Cold messages make sense when a few named buyers matter. For students, group posts and community answers do the same job in a friendlier way. A pitch deck for coaching institutes and intro email designs exist, but no send is recorded, and each one needs your own go. Keep them for later, after you have one proof story and an answer on parental consent.

What to focus on instead: finish the first-group decision, tag your links, run a two-channel test, and close the scorecard loop. Then revisit the channel list after the Student Beta ends, and again after three months of real numbers, when the question of paid ads comes back.
