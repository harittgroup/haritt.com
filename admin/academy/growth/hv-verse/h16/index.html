Why pricing is not your job yet

HV Verse has no revenue model, and that is a choice, not a gap to hurry past.

TodayFree for everyone

HV Reset, Hunt, Assess and AI cost the student nothing.

vs
LaterA price on a proven habit

Only after students return and say it helps.

1
Monetisation is the plain word for how a product turns value into money. A neighbourhood bakery can sell loaves one at a time or a monthly bread box, and picking between those is a pricing decision. Software has its own menu: a one time sale, a subscription, pay by use, freemium (a free core with a paid upgrade), a free trial, tiers of plans, or project fees for services. Price also tells people something about quality, and it is one of the strongest levers on profit.
2
For HV Verse, none of that applies yet. All four apps, HV AI included, are free for everyone, and your files say there is no revenue model and no pricing. That is a clear decision, and it fits your stage: the name is only a few weeks old, the apps launched on 1 October, and you do not yet know how many students come back. Putting a price on an app before you know whether people return is like pricing a restaurant's food before anyone has eaten a second meal there.
3
There is one money item on file, and it is a cost, not a price. If HV Reset goes into the phone stores, a Google Play developer account costs 25 US dollars once and an Apple developer account costs 99 US dollars a year. Those accounts do not exist yet and the plan covers HV Reset only. Paying for a store account is not the same as charging users.
Do you need this now? No. This lesson is marked for later. Charging, or even choosing a price, is not a growth step today. What to do instead: keep the apps free, prove that students come back, and write down what a returning student is worth to you. The next parts explain what would have to be true before pricing becomes a real task, and what to do on that day.

What must be true before you charge

Think of these as a gate. If the answers are not yes, pricing now would be guesswork, and it could hurt the free growth you are working on. Here are the checks, and your position on each.

ADo students come back?A price on a leaky product only reveals the leak.

Retention is the proof that the apps matter. You have cohort reports in the dashboard, but they are not reliable yet, because the dashboard shows weekly active users as 0 even though returning visits clearly happened. How to check: once the counter is fixed, look at how many new users are still active after seven days and after thirty days. Until that number exists, there is nothing to price.

BWho would pay, and for what?Different groups value different things.

Your audience draft names students aged 16 to 22 and freshers aged 21 to 26, but you have not chosen a first group. Students with no income and freshers who have just started earning behave very differently around money. The same app may be free for one group and worth paying for by another. How to check: choose the first group and talk to five of them about what they would pay for, without naming a price yet.

CIs there a natural paid layer?A paid layer must sit on something extra.

Freemium works when the free part is enough to build the habit and a paid part adds something clearly more useful. Your files say a paid layer should be planned only after retained users exist. HV AI is a candidate to think about because running it may cost money to serve, but the cost per use is not on file, so do not assume.

DPayments, age and disclosureRules come before money.

Charging brings rules: how you take payment in India, what you must tell the user before they pay, and what you do for users under 18, where parental consent is already an open question. Check these on official sources when the day comes. Do not build any of it now.

1
You have already sketched one route in your files: a ten slide pitch deck for coaching institutes, proposing a pilot of HV Reset and HV Assess for students up to Grade 10. It is not known whether the deck was sent. That route is a kind of project or plan fee paid by an institute, not by a student, and it has its own risks, such as parental consent and a long decision time. It is worth keeping separate from student pricing.
2
To choose between models, there are three ways to set a price. Cost plus adds a margin to what it costs you. A competitor based price looks at what similar tools charge. A value based price asks what the result is worth to the buyer, and is usually the strongest. For a student, "worth" is small in rupees, so research matters more than guessing. Two simple methods exist: Van Westendorp asks four questions (too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive) and finds the range students accept; Gabor-Granger asks whether they would buy at a few set prices. Both are just short surveys, and a free form tool is enough.

Run a first price test safely

New visitors only, a written guess, and a clear way to stop.

Retention countedGroup chosenPaid layer defined
Show to new visitorsRecord price seenCount who pays
Compare with freeCheck day 30 returnKeep or revert

Suppose the day comes. Here is how to test a price without damaging what you built.

1Write the guess firstPrice is a guess until a student agrees to it.

Write one sentence before the test: "We think students in the first group will pay a certain amount a month for this paid layer, and at least a stated share of active users will take it." Decide the amount and the share before you look at results. Any numbers you choose are yours to set, and none are on file today.

2Test on new visitors, not current usersSurprising existing users is the fastest way to lose trust.

Show the new price only to people who have not used the apps yet, or to a small, clearly announced group. Never change what existing free users get without telling them. Your Beta already says perks are not guaranteed, which gives you a little room, but honest notice is still the rule.

3Log every changePricing changes need a record.

Keep a simple sheet with the date, the price shown, who saw it and what happened. Without a log, a rise in sign-ups next month will be impossible to link to the price.

4Track more than paymentsRevenue is not the only result.

Watch the share of free users who pay (free to paid), the revenue per user, and whether people leave after the paywall appears. Check the day 30 return of the paying group, because a payment that disappears next month is not growth.

The money numbers, explained early

Four terms you will meet, and how they would read for HV Verse.

200Made up
20Made up
16Made up

Made-up example, not your numbers. Free to paid is 20 of 200, which is 10%. Churn is 4 of 20, which is 20% a month.

1
It helps to know the vocabulary before you need it, so that a report does not confuse you later. ARPU is average revenue per user: total money divided by the number of users, free users included. MRR is monthly recurring revenue, the money you can expect each month from plans that renew, and ARR is the same for a year. Free to paid is the share of free users who become paying. Churn is the share of payers who leave in a month. Expansion means a user moving to a bigger plan or buying another app from you.
2
Take a made up case: 200 active free users, of whom 20 pay a monthly amount you choose. Free to paid is 10%. If 4 of those 20 leave next month, churn is 20% a month, which is high and would tell you the paid layer is not valuable enough. None of this is a forecast for HV Verse. The point is to see how the numbers connect, so that when real counts exist you can read them without help.
3
Notice what is missing today: you have no paying users, so none of these figures can be calculated. Your own budget file says the cash cost per user is zero and the revenue per user is zero. That is why the right numbers to watch now are the free ones: activation, seven day return and shares.

What to do instead, now

Spend the effort on the things that make a price possible later.

1 Stay freebuild the habit
2 Count7 and 30 day return
3 Listenfive students, no price
4 Notewhat a returning user is worth
1
Because pricing is not the job now, the useful question is what to focus on so that it can be the job later. There are four things, and none costs money.
2
First, stay free and aim at habit. Your own file says to prove retention before any price. Second, fix the counting, so that returning users and weekly active users show real numbers instead of zero. Third, talk to students. Ask five of them what they would miss if the app disappeared. If they name something specific, that is the seed of a paid layer. If they shrug, you have learned that the product needs work first. Fourth, write down in one paragraph what a retained student is worth to you. It does not need to be money. It might be a recommendation, a testimonial with permission, or a partnership with an institute.
3
If you decide to go to the phone stores, finish the Android plan as a separate task: choose how to wrap the web app, decide the account type and the package name, and note that new personal Play accounts may need testers for a set period before release (re-check the current rule at the time). Your Beta users could fill that tester group if they agree. That is a launch task, not a pricing task.

When it goes wrong, and when to come back

How to read a failed price test, and the signals that say it is time.

What do your numbers show?

Tap what you see. The likely culprit shows here.
Most likelyOffer or price

Ask the few who saw it why not. Is the paid part clear, or just a locked door?

Most likelyPayment step

Check mobile checkout, steps and payment methods students actually have.

Most likelyTrust or free value cut

Did you take away something free that students used?

Most likelyProduct value

Look at day 30 return of payers before changing price.

Most likelyDisclosure

Make the price and terms clear before the student commits.

1
A price test fails in a few predictable ways. If almost nobody pays, the issue is usually the offer, not the number: students cannot see what the paid part gives them. If people start paying but do not finish, the problem sits in the payment step, so test it on a phone with the methods students really use. If sign-ups fall after you show a price, you may have taken away something free that they relied on. If payers leave quickly, price is not the cause; the product is not valuable enough for repeated use. Find which pattern you see before changing the price.
2
Revert quickly when a test hurts. Then change one thing, such as the wording of the paid layer, and run it again on new visitors. Do not stack tests.
When to come back to this lesson: after the three month review, if you can show that a clear group of students return week after week, that you know the one thing they value most, and that the privacy, age and payment rules are checked. Until then, the right pricing decision is to keep it free.
