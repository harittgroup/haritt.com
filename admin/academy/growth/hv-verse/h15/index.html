What a referral loop means for HV Verse

A student tells a friend because the app helped, and the friend arrives already trusting you.

vs
Referred visitorArrives warm

A friend said it helped, so they try it.

1
Picture a student who finishes the HV Assess self-assessment, likes the result, and sends the scorecard to a friend on WhatsApp with the message "try this, it was useful". The friend opens the link, takes the test, and maybe sends their own scorecard to someone else. That chain is a referral loop. The idea is plain: your users become one of your ways of reaching new users, and each new user may bring in more. Referred people usually arrive already trusting you, because the invitation came from someone they know, so they are more likely to try the app and finish something in it.
2
There are two numbers worth knowing. The viral coefficient (also called the K-factor) is how many invitations each user sends, multiplied by the share of those invitations that turn into a new user. If K is above 1, every user brings in more than one new user and growth feeds itself. Very few products reach that, so do not aim for it. Even a K of 0.2 helps, because it means one in five of your users brings a friend and you did not have to pay or post to get that friend. The second number is NPS (Net Promoter Score), which asks "how likely are you to recommend this, from 0 to 10?" and is a quick way to learn whether people would be willing to refer at all.
3
For HV Verse this matters more than for most businesses, because you have no ad budget by decision. Your reach comes from time, WhatsApp groups, college groups and word of mouth. A working share loop is the cheapest reach you can build.
Do you need this now? Partly. Building a full invite programme with rewards is for later, because it only works once students already come back to the apps. But two small things are worth doing now and cost nothing: put a label on the HV Assess scorecard share link so you can finally count visits that came from sharing, and read that number each week. Everything else in this lesson waits until activation and week-one return are measured.

Before you ask anyone to tell a friend, four things should be true. Here is each one, how to check it, and where HV Verse stands, from your files.

APeople must get value firstIf the first visit is poor, nobody recommends it.

A student only shares something that helped them. So referral sits after activation and retention in your order of work. You decided activation means a first task done in HV Reset, a first job added in HV Hunt and a completed test in HV Assess. Those events exist in the code, but there are no reliable counts yet. How to check: ask five students who tried an app what they would tell a friend about it. If they cannot say anything specific, the app is not ready to be passed on yet.

BSomething worth sharingYou need one thing that is easy and pleasant to pass on.

The best candidate is the HV Assess scorecard. It has a unique ID that can be checked, a QR code on the closing page of the PDF, and a share event. HV Reset and HV Hunt have no invite or share step at all. How to check: open your own scorecard on a phone, tap share, and see what a friend would receive. If the message is blank or the link is long and ugly, fix that first.

CA link you can countWithout a label on the link, shared visits look like random visits.

The system only counts a visit as shared when the link carries a label such as utm_source=scorecard or utm_medium=share. A label like that is called a UTM tag: a short piece of text at the end of a web address that tells your analytics where the visit came from. It is not confirmed that the scorecard link and the QR code carry one today, and the loop is not measured as referral. How to check: share a scorecard to yourself, open it, and look at the address bar for the label.

DConsent and ageUsers under 18 need extra care before you push invitations.

Your audience draft starts at 16, and the privacy text and parental consent for under 18 users still need review. Do not add anything that sends messages to other people's contacts. A share button that the student taps, and sends from their own WhatsApp, is the safe version.

There are two broad kinds of referral. Built-in virality means that using the product naturally shows it to others: a shared link, an exported item with a small mark on it, an invitation inside the product. Incentivised referral means you reward the person who invites, the friend who joins, or both. A well-known example is a file storage company that gave extra space to both sides. Rewards work, but they need tracking, clear rules and a way to stop people cheating, and they attract people who only want the reward.

SStart with what you already haveThe HV Assess scorecard is your hook.

A scorecard is something a student is often proud or curious to show a friend, so it carries its own reason to be shared. Make the share easy: one tap, a ready message in the student's own voice, and a tagged link. Do not make the message sound like an advert. Something like "I took this self-assessment, here is my result, you can try it free" reads like a friend, which is what it is.

TThen add one invite stepA share-my-plan link in HV Reset.

Your approaches on file are to add a share-my-plan link in HV Reset with a tagged link, and later to give Beta points for friends who join. Note that Beta points today reward contribution, not invites, and the Beta says perks are not guaranteed. Keep that wording honest if you ever connect points to invitations.

TThe real route todayWhatsApp and college groups.

Your own plan treats WhatsApp and college groups as the real word of mouth channel. That is where a student forwards a link. So the loop you design should survive being pasted into a group chat: the link must open fast on a phone, show what the app is in one line, and work without sign-in where possible, which HV Assess already does.

Here is the setup in the order you do it. Menu names in tools change from time to time, so treat the paths below as a guide and look for the closest label if one has moved.

1Build the tagged linkSame pattern every time, or the data splits.

Take the scorecard address and add a label for the source and the medium, for example ?utm_source=scorecard&utm_medium=share. Use the UTM calculator on the Growth page if you want help. Keep the spelling and lower case identical each time, because "Scorecard" and "scorecard" are counted as two sources.

2Use the same link in the QRThe printed code must carry the label too.

Generate the QR code on the PDF closing page from the tagged link, not the plain one. Then scan it with your phone and check that it opens correctly. Old PDFs already sent will keep the old link, so only new ones are counted.

3Read it in your admin dashboardYour own dashboard already has a Shared by users line.

Open the admin dashboard, go to the HV Assess funnel panel and find the shared figure. Note it weekly. Since the dashboard counter keeps totals only, with no cookies and no user IDs, it can tell you how many shared visits arrived, but not who they were. That is by design and it is the privacy approach you chose.

4Read it in GA4GA4 gives the second view.

In GA4 open Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition. Change the first column to Session source / medium, and look for the row with your scorecard label. If the row is missing after a few days, the tag is not on the link or the GA4 tag is not firing on the page. Note that the GA4 tag itself is not confirmed in the local files, so verify it with the Realtime report while you open a tagged link yourself.

Read the loop and tell if it works

Three numbers: how many share, how many click, how many finish.

100Made up
30Made up
24Made up
8Made up

Made-up example to show the arithmetic, not your numbers. Here K is 0.08, because 100 finishers brought 8 new finishers.

1
The loop has three links and you want a number for each. First, the share rate: of the students who finish a scorecard, how many tap share. Second, the click rate: how many visits arrive for each share. Third, the completion rate: of those visitors, how many finish the test themselves. Multiply them together and you get the new users each current user brings, which is the K-factor.
2
Say, with made up numbers, 100 students finish a scorecard, 30 share, those shares bring 24 visits, and 8 of those visitors finish a test. K is 8 divided by 100, which is 0.08. It is far below 1, and that is normal. What matters is the direction over time and which of the three links is weakest. If the share rate is low, make sharing easier. If clicks are low, the message or the preview is weak. If completions are low, the first screen for a newcomer needs work.
3
Also keep the time in mind: a loop takes days, not hours, because a student shares in the evening and the friend opens it later. Judge it over a few weeks, and do not compare a Monday with a Tuesday. Finally, if you ask the NPS question, ask it right after a win, such as a finished test, and calculate promoters (9 or 10) minus detractors (0 to 6) as a percentage. With few responses treat the result as a hint, not a score.

Ask five students what they would tell a friend. Change the thing, not the button.

1
When the loop is quiet, do not jump to rewards. Walk down the three links in order, and use the table above. The pattern tells you the problem: a gap between finishing and sharing is a product or button problem, a gap between sharing and arriving is a link problem, and a gap between arriving and finishing is a first experience problem.
2
Change one thing at a time. For example, rewrite the ready made share message and leave everything else alone for two weeks, then compare the share rate before and after. If you change the message, the button and the link at once, you will not know which one helped. Write each change and its result in your learning log, so you do not repeat a test.
3
Stop adding effort to the loop if, after a few clear fixes, the share rate stays very low. That is useful information: it may mean the apps are used privately, such as a job tracker, and are not naturally shared. In that case, referral may not be your strongest route and you should spend your time on retention or on the WhatsApp groups where you already reach students.

Next steps and when to add rewards

A short order of work that costs no money.

1 Tagscorecard link and QR
2 Countweekly in the dashboard
3 Improveshare message and button
4 Rewardonly if the rest works
1
The order of work is short. First, tag the scorecard link and the QR code. Second, read the shared visits each week for a month, so you know the starting level. Third, improve the weakest link, one change at a time. Fourth, and only then, consider adding a share-my-plan link inside HV Reset.
2
A reward is for later, and only if you can answer yes to four questions. Do students already return to the apps? Is the share loop measured? Can you stop one person from gaming the reward? Have you checked the rules for rewards and disclosure, and the position on students under 18? If any answer is no, a reward will cost you more trouble than it brings.
How you know you are done: you can say how many visits came from shared scorecards last week, and which of the three links is the weakest. When to come back: after the three month review you set for ads, or sooner if the share rate rises and you want to build an invite step.
