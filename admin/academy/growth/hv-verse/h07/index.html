Part 1 of 6

What email and WhatsApp lists are for HV Verse

A list is permission to reach people again, and it only helps once there is something worth sending.

EmailSlow, searchable, formal

Good for welcome mails, guides and a short monthly note.

vs
WhatsAppFast, personal, where students are

Good for groups, a channel and quick replies.

1
A list is a group of people who have agreed to hear from you again. Think of a tuition teacher who keeps a notebook of parents who asked to be told about the next batch. Each name in the notebook is worth more than a hundred strangers who saw a poster once, because the person has already shown interest. For HV Verse there are two kinds of list. Email lists hold addresses from Google sign-in and from the Beta form. WhatsApp lists are groups, a channel, or a broadcast list, which is a way to message many saved contacts one by one, and only reaches people who have saved your number.
2
Today you already send some email, but only as part of the apps. A welcome mail goes out on first sign-in to HV Reset and HV Hunt. The Beta has a pass, a tester guide a few minutes later, a reminder for quiet testers at most once a week, a left-the-Beta mail, a welcome-back mail and a thank-you. There is no newsletter, no mail for HV Assess, no re-engagement mail and no weekly digest. The size of the contact list is not on file.
3
The risk of starting too early is real. Mail from a domain with no proof records can land in spam. A mail with no unsubscribe link breaks the trust you are trying to build with 16 and 17 year olds as well as adults. And an email blast to a small, cold list teaches you almost nothing.
Do you need this now? Not as a campaign. This is a later activity for HV Verse. What you need now is the foundation: a proper sender address, an unsubscribe line, domain proof records, and a clear reason to write. Until a few hundred real people have signed in, your best use of email is to make the existing welcome and Beta mails excellent, and to use WhatsApp for fast, personal contact with testers.

If these are mostly red, starting a campaign would be premature. A mail you cannot unsubscribe from, sent from an unproven address, is the fastest way to be marked as spam. The better move is to fix the basics once and then use them for every mail.

AThe address people seeChoose the sender.

App mails still go out from the old address. A switch to support@thehvverse.com is planned and waits for the Gmail Send-as setup. Until then every mail points at the wrong name. Choose one HV Verse address and one provider, and keep it for years.

BSPF, DKIM and DMARCProve the mail is genuine.

These are three short records you add to your domain settings. SPF lists which services may send mail for your domain. DKIM adds a signature that proves the message was not changed. DMARC tells receiving mail systems what to do if a message fails those checks. Without them, inboxes trust you less. Your DNS is on Cloudflare, so this is done there, as shown in part 4.

CA real unsubscribe lineGive people a way out.

Your mails say "reply STOP" and a Do not email list exists, but there is no unsubscribe link. Add a line that matches the list, and honour it the same day.

DConsentBe sure people agreed.

Contacts come from sign-ins and Beta forms. Check that the form or sign-in text says they may get mail from HV Verse. If it does not say so, send only the mails the person clearly expects, such as a welcome or a Beta pass.

ECount the listKnow what you are sending to.

Open the Contacts tab in your marketing admin and note the number, split by app. Until you know it, you cannot judge whether Gmail is enough: free Gmail sending allows about 100 recipients a day.

The best email is one that arrives right when the student has just done something and needs a next step. That is what a lifecycle mail is: an automatic message tied to a moment. Your welcome and Beta mails are already that. Before adding a newsletter, read each existing mail as the student would and ask what you want them to do next. A welcome mail that ends with "More from" four products pushes in four directions. One clear next step works better, such as finishing a first task.

HV Assess has no mail at all, yet its scorecard is your best sharing hook: it has a unique checkable ID, a QR code on the PDF and share events. A single mail after a saved scorecard with the link is a natural fit. Keep to your rules while doing it: never send real mail without your say, keep the text very short, no personal name in the sign-off, and no mention of any other brand in an HV Verse mail apart from the permitted footer.

TThree different toolsWhere WhatsApp fits

A WhatsApp group lets everyone talk, so it suits testers. A WhatsApp channel is one-way, so it suits announcements to anyone who follows. A broadcast list reaches only people who have saved your number. Do not add people to groups without asking. Start with one Beta testers group and one channel only if you have steady news.

WWhere the first names come fromBuilding the list honestly

Do not buy lists or copy numbers from a class group. Your real sources are the Beta form, Google sign-in on HV Reset and HV Hunt, and a clear opt-in line on the HV Assess scorecard page. Each of these should say in one sentence what the person will get and how often. Ask students to join the WhatsApp channel from a post or a poster, not by adding them yourself. A list of two hundred people who chose to be there is worth more than two thousand who did not.

LLess than you thinkHow often

One mail a week is the maximum you already use for Beta reminders. For students, one short mail a month is enough outside onboarding.

Part 5 of 6

Reading the results: did it work?

Clicks and replies tell you more than opens do.

DeliveryBounces and spam

Did it arrive? Few bounces, no spam complaints.

ActionTagged clicks

Did they do the next step, such as open an app?

ResponseReplies and leaves

Real replies are strong. Many unsubscribes is a warning.

Gmail cannot show opens, and opens are unreliable anyway, so judge a mail by what people did next. Your marketing dashboard shows sent, delivered (an estimate: sent minus bounced), bounced and failed, and the click counter tells you who followed which link. Tag mail links with utm_medium=email, as the em short link does, so your analytics shows email as its own channel instead of Direct.

What counts as success depends on the mail. For a welcome mail it is the share of new users who then finish a first task. For a Beta reminder it is the share of quiet testers who open the app again that week. For a scorecard mail it is shares. A quiet list that clicks is better than a big list that does not. Count unsubscribes and bounces as honest feedback, and look at them after each send.

WWhere the clicks show upReading it inside your analytics

Open the Campaigns and channels tables in your admin dashboard and find email as a channel. Compare the visits from it with the number of mails delivered. If 100 mails were delivered and 4 visits came from email, the click rate is about 4 in 100. That figure is made up for the example, but the method is real. Then check how many of those visits reached an activation event, because a click that stops at the home page has not helped a student yet. GA4, once set up as in lesson 10, will show the same channel under its own name.

AA simple readingWhat a good week looks like

Few bounces, a handful of real replies, at least some clicks to an app, and no spam complaints. If a mail gets none of those, the problem is in the message or the audience, not the tool.

Match the symptom to delivery, message, audience or product.

Work through the symptom in the order of the journey: did it arrive, did it get read, did the click lead somewhere useful, did the person stay? Fix the earliest broken step first. Then retest with a small send to a comparable group and compare the same numbers.

A common hidden cause is that the mail itself is fine but the destination is not. The link may open an app that asks for sign-in before showing anything, or a page that still carries an old name. Open every link in your own mail on a phone, signed out, before blaming the wording. Another common cause is timing: a reminder that arrives a month after the Beta pass feels random, while one a few days after feels helpful.

1One line or one linkChange one thing.

Rewrite only the subject or only the button. Send version B to a similar small group a week later, since your list is too small to split in the same hour.

2What, when, resultKeep a send log.

Write down the date, list, subject, clicks and replies. After three sends you will see a pattern.

3Decide on evidenceStop or scale.

If a mail type brings people back, keep it. If three sends bring no clicks and some unsubscribes, stop and talk to five students about what they actually want to hear.
